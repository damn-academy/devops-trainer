<!-- section: hook -->
This lesson brings the entire course together in one business game. You lead marketing at an infrastructure company for a quarter: you have a limited budget, one half-time employee, engineers who can spare a little time to write and speak, a founder with expectations, and a sales team that judges results by meetings. An AI system runs the game, and the outcome of each decision depends on its quality, how well it fits your buyers, competitors, market conditions, and hidden circumstances that you can only discover through clues. A good decision may fail, while a questionable one sometimes works, and at the end the game master will explain where quality mattered and where luck did.

<!-- section: theory | How to play -->
Before starting, choose a preset company or build your own: its code model, revenue model, product type, stage, home region, and primary buyer segment. Then choose the difficulty and number of turns. You can copy the prompt into any AI chat or play on this page if you have connected your AI system in the settings. Each turn, the game master sends a briefing, incoming messages, and a question about your decisions, followed at the end of the turn by a state block with what you can see: indicators, remaining budget, open commitments. Hidden circumstances are not in this block, so finish the game in the same conversation; in a new one the game master restores the visible state from the block and invents the hidden part anew. Write your decisions as you would write to your team: what you are doing, who is doing it, how much it costs, and why.

<!-- section: task | Task: review your game -->
Play a game to the end and save it. On this page with a connected AI system, use the “Export the game” button. In an external chat, save the conversation with the chat’s own tools or copy the last state block and the game master’s final review into a text file. From the game master's review, write down the three main decisions, the reason each succeeded or failed, and which of the five chance multipliers had the strongest effect. Then play a second game with the same company and a different game number, and compare which decisions you changed.
