<!-- section: hook -->
A platform engineer who had spent six months advocating for your product at a telecom operator left to join a bank on your list. You found out two months later, when the operator's new manager wrote that they were “reviewing vendors.” Had you noticed the move immediately, you could have congratulated the engineer, asked whether they were responsible for infrastructure at the bank, and also found out who now owned your pilot at the operator. This lesson covers signals: events in the lives of companies and people that make a conversation timely, and how to distinguish a signal from noise.

<!-- section: theory | Переходы людей -->
Someone who knows your product and changes jobs is one of the most useful signals. Services such as UserGems and Champify track former customers, users, and people involved in deals and report when they change jobs. GitLab's public handbook describes how it links people's records to previous companies and marks departures; GitLab's former-champion campaign went live on 21 September 2026. In its Heap case study, Champify reports that 73 % of meetings sourced through job changes became opportunities, twice the usual rate, but does not disclose the number of meetings or observation period. UserGems claims that former champions buy three times as often, without describing the sample. In October 2026, Clay described its process: detect a significant person's departure from a paying customer, check whether the new company is a fit, record the move in the CRM, and prepare an email draft for human review; over 60 days, it identified 62 such people, roughly one a day.

LinkedIn Sales Navigator lets you save people and companies and receive notifications about job and role changes, as well as hiring growth, funding, and mergers at companies. If someone has moved, verify the move, determine whether the new company has a relevant problem, refer to your past work together without exaggeration, ask whether they are responsible for infrastructure, and offer a useful resource. Separately, ask the old company who now owns your initiative. “Worked at a customer” does not yet mean “used and liked the product.”

<!-- section: theory | Вакансии, деньги и списки фонда -->
Monitor target companies' careers pages for Kubernetes, KubeVirt, OpenStack, VMware, vSphere, bare metal, multi-tenant, platform engineer, and explicit references to migration. Save the link, first appearance date, location, team, and exact phrase. Distinguish three kinds of job postings: “operate the current system,” “expand capabilities,” and “replace the platform.” Verify any migration deadline inferred from a job posting in a conversation.

For a funding round, save the company's announcement and what it says it will spend the money on; for a public company, save the statement from a report or earnings call about infrastructure spending, margins, or vendor consolidation. A funding round means the company has money, but does not prove it has a budget for your product, and a management directive to cut costs may mean a freeze on new vendors.

Conference schedules and speaker biographies show who is solving which problem and give you a reason to ask a question. CNCF compiled a list of 104 talks at KubeCon North America 2025 from end-user companies; this is a count of talks, with fewer buyers behind them. Foundation membership lists change too: in November 2025, CNCF announced its End User Contributor program and closed the paid End User Supporter program, so an old membership list does not describe all current users, and foundation membership does not grant permission to send email campaigns.

<!-- section: theory | Активность в открытом коде и визиты на сайт -->
Company employees' activity in open-source projects can also be a signal. Pulumi combined activity on GitHub, X, Reddit, and DEV with account research, and Common Room reports that this work contributed to 27 % of booked meetings in one quarter. Weaviate combined Scarf download and usage data with activity such as stars to select accounts. RisingWave describes sales cycles lasting from 12 to 24 months, so it retains signal history for a long time.

These signals have strict limits. Identifying a company from a visitor's address remains an inference: VPNs, cloud egress, shared networks, home internet connections, build systems, and mirrors conceal or substitute for the actual organization, and an employer match does not tell you which employee visited. Downloads can be automated, repeated, or served from cache and say nothing about a production installation. A GitHub star may be a bookmark, a fork may be an experiment, and someone may have contributed to a project in a personal capacity. GitHub's rules prohibit using service data, including data collected through the API, for spam, including unsolicited emails to users. In 2024, Scarf's founder described how the community objected to telemetry collection and the company had to change.

<!-- section: theory | Как обрабатывать сигналы -->
Process signals in this order: collect permitted information, match it to a company and record confidence, apply qualification and disqualification rules, remove duplicates and stale data, have a human review it, choose an appropriate channel, check contact suppression, and only then create a task. A documentation visit attributed with low confidence calls for a research task; it does not warrant an email saying “I see you are looking into us.” If you do not know who visited, record that and do not invent a person from an address.

Before trusting a signal source, audit it manually: review thirty matched companies and record how many matches are correct, incorrect, and unclear; check the ten most important triggers for relevance; and compare meetings per researched company with a similar group of companies without signals. The number of signals collected is not a measure of success on its own.
