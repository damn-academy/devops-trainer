# Dream 100 for open-source infrastructure: an account-based selling operating system, 2025–2026

Research cutoff: **25 November 2026**. Audience: a founder or first seller serving enterprise platform teams and hosting providers; includes a Europe-to-US founder scenario.

## How to read the evidence

- **Evidence labels:** **Verified rule** means an official rule or documented product capability; **Reported result** means a named company's or research vendor's own account, not independently audited causation; **Practitioner opinion** means experience or judgment; **Proposed implementation** means this document's original design, not a measured industry standard. Recommendations cite their relevant evidence or constraints, but their exact schemas, schedules, thresholds and scripts are proposals. Vendor case studies cannot establish what would have happened without the vendor. The distinction matters particularly for “sourced or influenced” pipeline. ([Example of a vendor-reported attribution metric](https://www.commonroom.io/customers/pulumi-signals-meetings-booked/), publication date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Date convention:** publication/update dates are stated when available; `n.d.` means publication date not verified, with the access date shown separately. A 2026 crawl does not turn an undated case into a 2026 result. Older-than-2024 evidence is marked **[OLD]**; 2024 material is explicitly identified as background. Living documentation is retained for current rules even when its original publication date is unavailable. The 2025 Belkins report, for example, actually studies 2024 activity. ([Belkins methodology](https://7292705.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7292705/Download%20Marketing%20Files/Cold%20outreach%20benchmarks%202025-%20The%20Belkins%20and%20friends%20report.pdf), published 2025, exact day not verified; observation window 2024)
- **Scope:** use Cozystack's Kubernetes/bare-metal cloud model to formulate account hypotheses, not to assume that every Kubernetes user needs another platform. Its maintainer's recorded introduction addresses data control, proprietary dependency and hosting-provider differentiation. This document does not rely on a particular CNCF maturity designation to predict purchasing intent. ([Andrei Kvapil, CNCF project lightning talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-Q2ZQuzvHH8), published 2025-11-24)
- **Main evidence gap:** no verified 2025–2026 controlled study was found showing that a 100-account program, a particular number of touches, or moving a European infrastructure founder to the US produces a specified revenue uplift. Available evidence combines actual operating processes, selected customer cases and broader surveys. Use the implementation below as an instrumented experiment. ([PostHog outbound operating handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; living page accessed 2026-11-25; [Sifted/AVP original founder research](https://www.datocms-assets.com/60124/1748430884-transatlantic-founder-index.pdf), published 2025-05)

## 1. Tracking: build a relationship database before buying an automation stack

### What practitioners actually do, and what the results establish

- **Reported result — Railway:** its case describes workspaces, usage and revenue in Attio, with an adoption score fed from Segment, BigQuery and manual inputs. Workflows surface spend changes and deals for triage. Railway reports **100% GTM adoption**, without GTM-team sample size or incremental revenue lift. Publication and measurement dates are **not verified**. ([Attio/Railway case](https://attio.com/customers/railway), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Failure — forcing a business into the wrong CRM model:** Railway says earlier CRM attempts failed to represent its metered-billing model, leaving adoption alerts scattered in chat. This testimonial does not prove that other CRMs cannot support usage-based selling. The failure was losing account context across systems. ([Attio/Railway case](https://attio.com/customers/railway), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Verified practice — PostHog:** technical account executives work a **top-10 cold account list**, alongside different warm-outbound motions. This supports having a small active subset, not treating all 100 targets as equally active. No conversion benchmark for that top-10 list is published on the page. ([PostHog outbound handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Proposed relational model: eight small tables, stable identifiers

- **Accounts:** `account_id`, canonical domain and aliases, legal name, parent/subsidiary, country, operating locations, segment, owner, tier, fit rationale, disqualifiers, account stage, stage-entered date, first/last meaningful engagement, next action/date, and suppression status. Infrastructure-specific fields: current virtualization/control plane, owned versus rented hardware, deployment model, tenant model, regulatory constraints, renewal/migration window and who verified each claim. Keep unknowns null; “uses VMware” is not “approved a VMware exit.” This is an original schema compatible with ordinary organization/custom-field models. ([Pipedrive field model](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/data-fields-in-pipedrive), updated 2026-09-03)
- **People and employment:** `person_id`, name, public professional URL, permitted work-contact details, role/function, timezone, language, communication preferences and verification date. Store employment separately: `person_id`, `account_id`, title, start/end dates, source and observed date. Changing jobs must not overwrite the historical relationship or erase the old account's champion-loss risk. Treat corporate email as an employment attribute, not the person's permanent identifier. ([GitLab's UserGems implementation, including past-account/past-contact fields](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/marketing-operations/usergems/), n.d.; campaign launch explicitly 2026-09-21; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Buying-committee relationships:** map people to the specific account initiative and, later, opportunity. Suggested roles: practitioner/operator, technical evaluator, internal champion, platform owner, economic buyer, security/compliance reviewer, procurement/legal, and blocker. One person can hold several roles; several people can share a role. Store each person's priorities, influence evidence, position on the proposal and who can introduce you. An enthusiastic engineer is not automatically a champion with organizational influence. ([HubSpot buying-role mechanics](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/branding/get-started-with-account-based-marketing-in-hubspot), updated 2026-10-06)
- **Relationship strength — proposed scale:** 0 = identified; 1 = interacted; 2 = substantive two-way discussion; 3 = willingly helps with context or introductions; 4 = actively advocates internally. Store supporting evidence and the date, plus a separate `permission_to_reference` field. Connection acceptance, an investor's address book entry and a GitHub star cannot establish level 3. These levels are an operating convention, not a validated scoring model. Implement eight tables: Accounts, People, Employment, CommitteeRelationships, Signals, Activities, Opportunities and Suppressions. ([LinkedIn saved-lead mechanics](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a10748103), exact update date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Signals:** append-only `signal_id`, account/person if known, type, original URL, short factual extract, event date, detection date, confidence, identity-match method, expiry/review date, proposed relevance and reviewer. Keep “facts observed” separate from “sales interpretation.” A hiring ad can establish that a company advertised a Kubernetes role; it does not establish a new platform budget. ([Clay's distinction between job start and detection dates](https://www.clay.com/blog/how-we-turn-a-champions-departure-into-a-buying-signal), published 2026-10-27)
- **Activities and opportunities:** activities record inbound/outbound direction, channel, person, account, owner, actual timestamp, outcome, promised deliverable and next step. Opportunities record a specific initiative, sponsor, measurable problem, agreed evaluation, technical criteria, budget/procurement path, next decision and loss reason. Do not create revenue pipeline because someone downloaded software. Use a separate consent/suppression ledger that every sending tool checks. ([Pipedrive organization/person/activity structure](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/how-is-pipedrive-data-organized), exact publication date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25; [CRTC consent-record guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Tiers, stages and the weekly control loop

- **Proposed tiers:** start with 10 Tier A accounts receiving individual research, 30 Tier B accounts grouped by a specific problem, and 60 Tier C accounts monitored for change. Reallocate monthly. A hosting provider belongs in A if the evidence supports a commercially relevant platform initiative and a feasible path to people, not merely because it is famous. Keep separate views for enterprise internal platforms, regional hosters/MSPs and partners: their buyer economics differ. The 10/30/60 allocation is a capacity assumption, not a benchmark. ([PostHog's actual top-10 outbound practice](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed stage definitions:** `unaware/unknown` = no evidence of awareness; `aware` = attributable human recognition or participation; `engaged` = substantive two-way exchange about a relevant issue; `meeting` = qualifying meeting actually held, with `meeting_booked` recorded separately; `opportunity` = buyer-confirmed initiative and agreed next evaluation/decision. Also allow nurture, disqualified, won and lost. Store evidence, dates and regressions. An impression, tracking-pixel open or inferred IP visit should not advance an account by itself. ([HubSpot target-account workspace](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/prospecting/use-the-target-accounts-index-page), update date varies between indexed versions, September 2026; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed Monday review, 45 minutes:** first resolve overdue promises and opt-outs; then review A-account changes and committee gaps; select the few accounts with a new reason to act; assign one owner and dated action; finally review last week's held meetings and qualified opportunities. Do not spend the meeting reading every row. Create views for “active without next step,” “single-threaded,” “champion departed,” “unverified trigger,” and “meeting held but no buyer decision.” Exact duration and views are proposed. ([GitLab's field-program ownership and campaign templates](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/field-marketing/field-marketing-epics/), exact publication date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed hygiene:** deduplicate by stable IDs and verified domains; model subsidiaries explicitly; normalize dates to UTC while retaining local meeting timezone; refresh employment before outreach; reconcile bounces; mark stale fields; log enrichment provenance and changes. Keep a minimal suppression record even when deleting unnecessary prospect data, subject to the applicable retention basis. Never let a new enrichment re-enroll an opted-out person. ([CRTC consent guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [European Commission legal-basis guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed metrics:** count distinct target accounts researched, engaged, met, qualified and won; committee roles covered; held/booked ratio; qualified/held ratio; days in stage; overdue next steps; and human hours/cash per qualified opportunity. Report positive replies separately from all replies. For tiny cohorts, show counts such as `3/12`, not just `25%`; record cohort entry dates to avoid comparing a mature quarter with last week's outreach. ([Belkins' denominator disclosure illustrates the measurement problem](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), updated 2026-06-26)

### Spreadsheet, CRM and enrichment: a practical choice

- **Proposed spreadsheet setup:** one workbook with the eight tables above, protected identifiers, controlled dropdowns, filtered next-action views, a shared owner and a daily export/backup. It is reasonable for a solo founder who actually maintains it. Move when shared ownership, permissions, history or synchronization causes missed actions—not at a mythical account-count threshold. A verified 2025–2026 OSS-infrastructure founder case proving spreadsheet superiority was **not verified**. ([Pipedrive documents spreadsheet import/update compatibility](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/data-fields-in-pipedrive), updated 2026-09-03)
- **Attio:** choose it when flexible relationships between people, companies and product workspaces are central. Railway is a verified named user and implementation example. Proposed setup: Companies + People + Workspaces, an account-priority list, an opportunity pipeline and a small API sync of product summaries. Do not start with a raw event firehose or copy Railway's adoption formula without validating your own usage economics. ([Attio/Railway case](https://attio.com/customers/railway), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **HubSpot:** its documented ABM features include Target account, ICP tier and multi-valued Buying role, with associated account reporting and LinkedIn audiences. The documented ABM package requires qualifying Professional/Enterprise subscriptions; do not present it as included in the free CRM. Proposed low-cost alternative: ordinary company/contact records and manual custom properties until those paid features solve a demonstrated problem. ([HubSpot ABM setup and entitlement](https://knowledge.hubspot.com/branding/get-started-with-account-based-marketing-in-hubspot), updated 2026-10-06)
- **Pipedrive:** useful as a proposed activity-oriented implementation: Organizations hold account fields, People hold contacts, Activities hold next actions, and Deals start only after qualification. Custom-field quotas and advanced field controls depend on plan. A named 2025–2026 small OSS-infrastructure customer's measured Pipedrive implementation was **not verified**; this is a documented-capability recommendation. ([Pipedrive data fields](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/data-fields-in-pipedrive), updated 2026-09-03)
- **Clay:** use it as a researched-data and workflow layer feeding one authoritative CRM. Proposed controls: test 10 rows, require source URLs, review identity matches, cap recurring credits, prevent overwriting human-verified fields and make retries idempotent. Its job-change monitor requires a LinkedIn URL and supports a selected frequency; checks currently consume an action plus data credits. Avoid baking a changing price into course exercises. ([Clay job-change documentation](https://university.clay.com/docs/job-change-signal-overview), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **What has not been established:** there is no verified 2025–2026 platform change that made spreadsheets stop working. Proposed decision rule: avoid duplicate contacts, missing next steps and unreviewed automation. Buy additional tooling only when a demonstrated workflow needs it. ([Pipedrive field/organization mechanics](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/data-fields-in-pipedrive), updated 2026-09-03)

## 2. Signals: prioritize useful evidence without pretending to know intent

### Champion changes: mechanisms, real adoption and bounded numbers

- **UserGems mechanics:** upload or automatically import relevant former customers, users and opportunity contacts; scanning can be weekly or monthly depending on license. GitLab's live handbook documents past-account/past-contact links and flags for leaving a company; its past-champion campaign went live on **21 September 2026**. That is current deployment evidence, not a published win-rate result. ([UserGems tracking documentation](https://help.usergems.com/settings/track-contacts), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [GitLab implementation](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/marketing-operations/usergems/), campaign date 2026-09-21, page publication date not verified)
- **Champify mechanics and result:** Heap tracked former power users and buyers, filtered their new employer/role for fit and routed alerts into Salesforce, Slack and Outreach. The case reports **73% of Champify-sourced meetings converting to pipeline**, described as twice baseline, and **24% of new-logo revenue sourced or heavily influenced**. Meeting count, observation period and publication date are not disclosed in the inspected page. These are selected-vendor attribution metrics, not proof of a 73% cold-prospect conversion rate or fresh 2025–2026 performance. ([Champify/Heap](https://www.champify.io/customers/heap), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; freshness not verified)
- **Clay's own current implementation:** detect a senior person leaving a paying customer, verify new-account fit and prior usage, record the transition in Salesforce and place a draft sequence in Gong Engage for human review. Clay reports **62 qualifying people in the preceding 60 days**, approximately one enrollment daily; it does not report wins. Keep job start date separate from detection date because profile employment dates may have only month precision. ([Clay growth team write-up](https://www.clay.com/blog/how-we-turn-a-champions-departure-into-a-buying-signal), published 2026-10-27)
- **LinkedIn Sales Navigator:** save the relevant people and accounts, enable job/role-change notifications and weekly email notifications, and review alerts against your own committee map. Saved-account alerts also cover hiring growth, funding and M&A. These depend on LinkedIn's observable data; no independently verified coverage or detection-lag benchmark for the reference class was found. ([Sales Navigator alerts](https://www.linkedin.com/help/sales-navigator/answer/a105133), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Benchmark caution:** UserGems advertises that past champions are **3× more likely to buy again**, but the inspected campaign-library page supplies no denominator, sample or period. Keep it as an unvalidated vendor claim. It does not conflict mathematically with Heap's twice-baseline meeting-to-pipeline result: the outcomes and cohorts differ. Do not blend either into a forecast. ([UserGems campaign library](https://help.usergems.com/campaign-library), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed response to a job change:** verify the move; assess whether the new company has the relevant operating problem; acknowledge the previous work truthfully; ask whether infrastructure ownership sits with them; offer a useful migration/operations artifact. Separately ask the old account who now owns the initiative. “Worked at a customer” is not evidence that the person used, liked or bought your product. No universal “contact within X days” conversion benchmark was verified. ([UserGems customer-success risk use cases](https://help.usergems.com/article/customer-success-use-cases), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Hiring, financial and ecosystem signals

- **Proposed hiring workflow:** monitor first-party careers/ATS pages for `Kubernetes`, `KubeVirt`, `OpenStack`, `VMware`, `vSphere`, `bare metal`, `multi-tenant`, `platform engineer` and explicit migration language. Save the requisition URL, posted/first-seen date, location, hiring team and exact relevant clause. Deduplicate reposts and agencies. Classify “operate incumbent stack,” “expand capability” and “replace platform” separately; verify any inferred deadline in conversation. This is a research design, not evidence that these keywords predict purchases. ([PostHog's firsthand use of job postings in account research](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed financial workflow:** for a funding signal, use the company's dated announcement and record stated use of proceeds; for a public company, use its investor-relations release, earnings transcript or filing and retain the exact sentence on infrastructure, margin, capex or consolidation. A funding round establishes financing, not a software budget. An executive cost-reduction mandate can mean “freeze vendors.” Plain's **$15M Series A** announcement explicitly linked its plans to product investment, hiring and an SF office; that is evidence of declared priorities, not a prospecting conversion statistic. ([Plain founders' announcement](https://www.plain.com/blog/we-ve-raised-a-15m-series-a), published 2025-02-10)
- **Conference speakers:** start with the official schedule and named engineering talks, not a purported full attendee list. A session title and abstract can connect a person to a documented problem and provide a credible question. CNCF's end-user compilation of KubeCon NA 2025 lists **104 sessions** classified from speakers' organizations; the count describes the agenda, not unique buying accounts or opted-in contacts. ([CNCF end-user session directory](https://endusers.cncf.io/events/2025/kubecon-na/), event 2025-11-10–13; directory verification 2026-11-01; publication date not verified)
- **CNCF lists need segmentation:** end users consume cloud-native technology internally; hosting vendors and service providers may belong in a different segment. In November 2025 CNCF announced a contribution-based End User Contributor Program and retirement of the paid End User Supporter program, with a transition provision into 2026. An older membership list is therefore not a complete current buying universe, and community eligibility is not marketing permission. ([CNCF program announcement](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2025/11/05/introducing-the-cncf-end-user-contributor-program-earn-access-influence-and-recognition/), published 2025-11-05)

### OSS activity and website intelligence: what can and cannot be inferred

- **Reported result — Pulumi:** it combined activity from GitHub, X, Reddit and DEV with account research and sales tooling. Common Room reports that it supported **27% of sales-qualified leads, defined there as meetings booked, over one quarter**. This is an attributed share, not a 27% uplift, and neither the quarter nor absolute meeting count is specified. Publication date and current performance are **not verified**. ([Common Room/Pulumi](https://www.commonroom.io/customers/pulumi-signals-meetings-booked/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Dated practitioner case — Weaviate:** its RevOps leader describes combining Scarf download/usage information with Common Room activity such as stars to prioritize accounts. The January 2025 case mentions **13M downloads and 10,000+ stars**, but supplies no measured incremental revenue attributable to the workflow. Use it to teach signal combination; never turn project download totals into enterprise demand. ([Scarf/Weaviate interview](https://about.scarf.sh/post/how-weaviate-leverages-open-source-usage-data-to-drive-revops/), published 2025-01-15)
- **Dated practitioner case — RisingWave:** its sales director explains moving beyond an anonymous Metabase dashboard to company-level engagement, with sales cycles described as **12–24 months**. This supports storing signal history and planned re-engagement over long periods. It is one company's characterization, not a median for Kubernetes platforms, and no before/after win-rate measurement is provided. ([Scarf/RisingWave](https://about.scarf.sh/post/how-risingwave-is-driving-open-source-growth-with-scarf/), published 2025-02-12)
- **Scarf mechanics:** gateway requests and documentation image requests can be enriched into business metadata. Scarf says raw IP addresses are discarded and not exposed; that is the vendor's technical/privacy representation, not an independent determination that every deployment is outside privacy law. Its FAQ notes IPv6 limitations and lower enrichment accuracy. The same FAQ does not provide a universal account-match precision figure. ([Scarf FAQ](https://docs.scarf.sh/faq/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed accuracy model:** corporate reverse-IP matches are account hypotheses. VPNs, cloud egress, shared NAT, home networks, CI and mirrors can obscure or misattribute the human organization; an employer match does not identify the visitor. Separate `company_match_confidence` from `purchase_intent`. Downloads can be automated, repeated or cached, and cannot demonstrate production deployment. Scarf's own changelog now explicitly distinguishes download signals from confirmed runtime usage. ([Scarf changelog](https://docs.scarf.sh/changelog/), living page accessed 2026-11-25; date of the specific distinction not verified)
- **GitHub interpretation:** a star can be a bookmark; a fork can be an experiment; a contribution can be made personally. Verify employment independently and distinguish repository-owner activity from an employee's personal account. Most importantly, GitHub's information-use restrictions prohibit using service information—including API-collected data—for spamming, explicitly including unsolicited emails to users. Public visibility and API access are not blanket permission for an email-enrichment campaign. ([GitHub Acceptable Use Policies, section 7](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Reo.dev evidence boundary:** the Kubegrade case below documents repository-based segmentation and an outreach outcome. It does not establish the accuracy of every identity match, the legality of each data-processing step, or platform authorization for the reported outreach automation. Before adopting any enrichment vendor, inspect data origin, permitted purposes, processor/controller roles, deletion propagation, retention and international transfers. An independently audited Reo.dev-versus-Scarf accuracy comparison was **not verified**. ([Reo.dev/Kubegrade](https://www.reo.dev/customer-stories/kubegrade), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [European Commission legal-basis guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Failure modes and a defensible signal pipeline

- **Actual backlash:** Scarf's founder retrospective acknowledges community backlash over telemetry and subsequent adaptation. The retrospective is from **2024**, and it does not quantify lost users or establish a new 2025 policy change. It is evidence that technically available collection can damage trust. A verified 2025–2026 numerical backlash case for these exact reverse-IP workflows was **not verified**. ([Scarf founder retrospective](https://about.scarf.sh/post/building-scarf-avi-press-on-haskell-telemetry-and-open-source-challenges/), published 2024-07-19; background)
- **Proposed processing order:** collect permitted evidence → resolve account with confidence → apply fit and exclusion rules → deduplicate/expire → human review → select an appropriate channel → check suppression → create a task. A low-confidence docs visit should create research work, not an automatic “I saw you researching us” message. Preserve unknown identities rather than hallucinating a person from an IP address. ([Scarf enrichment mechanics](https://docs.scarf.sh/faq/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [GitHub use restrictions](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed pilot:** review 30 matched accounts manually, recording correct, incorrect and unresolved matches; audit 10 high-priority triggers for relevance; compare held meetings per researched account with an equally qualified non-signal cohort. These are suggested sample sizes for finding obvious problems, not statistically sufficient proof. Keep false positives and opt-outs in the dashboard. “More signals ingested” is not a commercial success measure. ([Weaviate's signal-combination example](https://about.scarf.sh/post/how-weaviate-leverages-open-source-usage-data-to-drive-revops/), published 2025-01-15)

## 3. Multichannel sequencing: coordinated, permission-aware and measurable

### A relevant small-sample case, with important limitations

- **Reported result — Kubegrade:** founder Tim Grassin selected Kubernetes-related repository activity within **180 days**, including Kube-no-trouble, KubePug and Pluto. Of **113 selected developers, 112 were contacted**, producing **25 responses, reported as 22%, and five demo requests**. The sequence used LinkedIn invitations/messages followed by email; it was pre-product feedback recruitment, sometimes offering compensation. There is no revenue result, control group or channel-isolated attribution. Publication and campaign dates are **not verified**. The case also used La Growth Machine automation; reporting it does not endorse that method under LinkedIn/GitHub rules. ([Reo.dev/Kubegrade case](https://www.reo.dev/customer-stories/kubegrade), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Proposed three-week sequence for a real infrastructure hypothesis

- **Preparation:** identify one account problem, two plausible committee roles and a useful artifact: for example, a KubeVirt migration checklist, tenant-isolation test plan or calculator with explicit hardware/support assumptions. Seek a legitimate warm introduction first. Write the observation and hypothesis separately: “Your public talk describes X; we wondered whether Y is still difficult.” Do not claim to know their private architecture. ([PostHog research-before-discovery practice](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Days 1–4, proposed:** send one concise, truthful email with the account-specific problem, one useful result or artifact and a small question; include required identity/opt-out information. If the person has a relevant public post, contribute a substantive answer, not a disguised pitch. A connection request is appropriate only with an honest professional reason. There is no verified lift from mechanically liking three posts before messaging. ([FTC commercial-email guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), original page 2023 [OLD], currently maintained; accessed 2026-11-25; [LinkedIn invitation guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153), exact update date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Days 7–10, proposed:** make one follow-up that adds information—a reproducible failure test, a relevant operator case or an answer to a public question. If already connected and the medium suits them, use a short voice message with a text summary. Where lawful and appropriate, a manual business call can ask who owns the issue. Do not email, call and DM the same person on the same day merely to force a response. The timing is a conservative proposal, not a conversion benchmark. ([LinkedIn voice-message mechanics](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548233), exact update date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25; [FTC B2B telemarketing rule announcement](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/03/ftc-implements-new-protections-businesses-against-telemarketing-fraud-affirms-protections-against-ai), published 2024-03-07)
- **Days 14–21, proposed:** offer a relevant event conversation or a brief close-the-loop message, then pause unanswered outreach. Continue only after a new substantive trigger or an agreed follow-up date. If another role genuinely owns the problem, contact that role with its own context; do not surround the original person with colleagues repeating the same pitch. Explicit rejection or opt-out overrides every sequence. ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), exact effective date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **An original email example:** “Your team's public session on operating tenant clusters raised the question of upgrades across customer-specific configurations. We maintain an open-source cloud platform and wrote a test plan for rollback and tenant isolation. Would the checklist be useful to the person owning that work? If this is not relevant, tell me and I will stop.” Add the legally required business identity, address and opt-out handling. Use this only when the factual reference is true and the contact source/channel is permitted. ([FTC email requirements](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), original 2023 [OLD], maintained page accessed 2026-11-25)

### LinkedIn mechanics and actual restrictions

- **Weekly invitation limits:** LinkedIn confirms that all members, including Premium, are subject to invitation limits, but the inspected official guidance does **not publish a universal numeric weekly quota**. Claims such as “100/week” or “200/week with Sales Navigator” are not verified official allowances. A restriction commonly lasts one week; withdrawing pending invitations does not lift it and buying Premium does not purchase more invitations during a restriction. ([LinkedIn invitation-limit page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551527), exact update date not verified, displayed as approximately one year old; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Official contradiction — free personalized notes:** “Personalize invitations” says **three per month, up to 200 characters**; “Invitation limit reached” says **five per month**. Premium permits notes on all eligible invitations, while connection-request restrictions still apply. Record the allowance shown in the actual account UI; do not pretend the documentation agrees. The precise rollout/change date explaining the discrepancy is **not verified**. ([LinkedIn personalization page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a563153), exact update date not verified, displayed as five months old; [LinkedIn limit page](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551527), exact update date not verified; both accessed 2026-11-25)
- **InMail:** Sales Navigator provides **50 InMail credits per month**; this is separate from invitations and does not remove invitation restrictions. Use a credit for a genuinely relevant non-connection rather than treating a pending invitation as permission to repeatedly chase. Premium note length is documented as **300 characters** on the InMail comparison page, versus **200** for free notes; verify the composer because help-page versions differ. ([Sales Navigator InMail credits](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a112784/), n.d.; [InMail versus personalized invitations](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a6239760), exact update date not verified, approximately 2024; both accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Voice messages:** LinkedIn documents mobile-app recording, a maximum of **one minute**, and use with first-degree connections or group chats. It is not a way to voice-message arbitrary unconnected executives. A current controlled reply-rate advantage for voice notes to platform engineers was **not verified**. ([LinkedIn voice messages](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548233), exact update date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Automation policy:** LinkedIn's User Agreement prohibits bots and unauthorized automation for accessing the service, collecting contacts, messaging and inauthentic engagement. Low volume, randomized delays or a vendor's “safe” label do not establish authorization. Use native alerts and manual engagement; distinguish approved integrations from a browser extension automating the website. ([LinkedIn User Agreement, section 8.2](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), exact effective date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Actual enforcement, 2025:** LinkedIn announced legal proceedings against Proxycurl on **24 January 2025** over scraping/fake accounts and announced resolution on **28 July 2025**. The short resolution announcement does not publish all settlement terms. This is concrete evidence that data sourcing has platform-enforcement exposure, not proof that every enrichment vendor or API is unlawful. ([LinkedIn filing announcement](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-takes-legal-action-to-defend-member-privacy), published 2025-01-24; [LinkedIn resolution announcement](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/LinkedInWinsLegalBattleToProtectMemberData), published 2025-07-28)
- **If ignored:** a mutual connection can request permission for an introduction; an existing investor/advisor can identify the actual owner; a public technical discussion can establish relevance; an approved meetup talk or partner introduction can create a natural encounter. Groups, Slack communities, alumni and events are contexts for participation, not consent to private prospecting. Do not evade restrictions with new accounts or continually withdraw/re-send invitations. ([LinkedIn connection guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548242), n.d.; [GitHub anti-spam rules](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies), n.d.; both accessed 2026-11-25)

### Cold email: requirements, reputation and benchmark denominators

- **Gmail rules:** requirements began **1 February 2024** for personal Gmail recipients. All senders need SPF or DKIM, TLS and appropriate DNS; bulk senders additionally need both SPF and DKIM, DMARC and alignment, plus one-click unsubscribe for marketing/subscribed mail. Implement the stronger authentication baseline even for a founder's small program. These rules are not a declaration that unsolicited outreach is welcome. ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126), living page, requirements effective 2024-02-01; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **What changed in 2025:** Google says enforcement ramped up in **November 2025**, including temporary and permanent rejection of non-compliant traffic. Roughly **5,000 personal-Gmail messages in 24 hours**, aggregated across the same primary domain and its subdomains, establishes permanent bulk-sender status. Google recommends spam complaints below **0.1%**, avoiding **0.3% or more**; qualifying high-complaint senders lose mitigation eligibility. The FAQ explicitly excludes inbound Google Workspace accounts from this particular bulk-sender scope. ([Google sender FAQ](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414), living page, enforcement change 2025-11; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Yahoo:** official indexed guidance says bulk senders must authenticate, support easy unsubscribe and remain below **0.3% complaints**; it recommends separating bulk/marketing traffic from transactional/user mail infrastructure. Enforcement began in **February 2024**. The direct page returned HTTP 429 during research, so these statements were checked against indexed official text; its exact current revision date and a fresh numeric bulk-volume definition were **not verified**. Do not import Gmail's 5,000 threshold into Yahoo policy. ([Yahoo Sender Hub](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/), n.d.; enforcement from 2024-02; official indexed text checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed domain setup:** use a transparently branded sending identity, configured SPF/DKIM/DMARC, a working reply mailbox and consistent volume. Separate marketing operations from product alerts where appropriate; monitor both. A separate domain does not legalize spam or guarantee isolation from reputation effects, and subdomains do not evade Google's bulk classification. Avoid lookalike domains designed to obscure identity and “burn/recreate” routines. ([Google sender FAQ](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/14229414), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [Yahoo traffic-separation guidance](https://senders.yahooinc.com/best-practices/), n.d.; indexed text checked 2026-11-25)
- **Warm-up:** Google's guidance favors consistent sending, gradual increases and monitoring server responses/reputation. No verified official “14-day warm-up guarantees inbox placement” rule exists in the inspected material. Synthetic reply/open exchanges are not demonstrated deliverability insurance. Proposed founder practice: authenticate first, send low-volume useful messages to appropriate recipients, investigate bounces/rejections and pause on complaints rather than increasing mailbox count. ([Google sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **2026 benchmark:** Belkins reports **7,530,489 emails and 34,393 replies** from 2025, averaging **0.45% replies per email sent**, excluding automated replies and bounce notifications. The 10,000+-employee segment is **0.22%**. This is agency-client cold outreach, not a random sample of OSS enterprise accounts; replies include negative responses and are not meetings. The page explicitly says its methodology changed. ([Belkins original study](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), updated 2026-06-26; data January–December 2025)
- **Conflicting measurement descriptions:** the 2025 Belkins PDF describes reply rate as the share of unique recipients responding; the 2026 article characterizes prior studies as replies divided by recipients who opened. Show both disclosures rather than silently reconciling them. The 2025 PDF analyzes **16.5M emails across 93 business domains**, mostly 2024 data. A comparison with 2026's **0.45% per send** cannot establish a collapse in channel performance. ([2025 report methodology](https://7292705.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/7292705/Download%20Marketing%20Files/Cold%20outreach%20benchmarks%202025-%20The%20Belkins%20and%20friends%20report.pdf), published 2025, exact day not verified; [2026 methodology](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), updated 2026-06-26)
- **Another benchmark:** Mailshake's 2025 report says most campaigns fall in the **1–4% reply-rate range**, with **16% exceeding 5%**. The inspected report excerpt does not establish a denominator directly comparable with Belkins' per-send metric, so retain the difference as non-comparable, not a leaderboard. A course exercise should require each learner to define sends, delivered unique people, positive replies and held meetings separately. ([Mailshake original report](https://assets.mailshake.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/16091740/Cold-Email-Report-2025-Mailshake.pdf), published 2025, file hosted April 2025; exact publication day not verified)

### Phone, video, gifts and direct mail

- **Phone — proposed role:** a short, manual, context-based business call can resolve ownership or confirm an agreed meeting. Log the outcome and stop request; avoid personal-number escalation merely because email failed. B2B does not mean unregulated: the FTC expanded misrepresentation protections to B2B telemarketing in 2024, and calling/recording requirements vary by jurisdiction and number type. No trustworthy 2025–2026 Kubernetes-founder cold-call conversion baseline was verified. ([FTC rule announcement](https://search.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/03/ftc-implements-new-protections-businesses-against-telemarketing-fraud-affirms-protections-against-ai), published 2024-03-07)
- **Loom/Vidyard — proposed role:** use a 60–120-second, captioned explanation of one relevant architecture diagram, with a text alternative and no customer secrets. Prefer it after demonstrated interest; test whether adding a video improves held meetings, not just plays. Vidyard's 2025 research surveyed **656 senior sales/marketing professionals** in the US and Canada in April; **93% of users of video reported positive results**. That is self-reported sentiment, not a randomized reply-rate lift or an OSS-specific result. ([Vidyard research release](https://www.vidyard.com/press-releases/vidyard-releases-the-future-revenue-report-revealing-untapped-pipeline-and-revenue-potential-for-gtm-teams/), published 2025-06-10; [report](https://www.vidyard.com/future-revenue-strategy-agentic-ai-and-video/), published 2025, exact day not verified)
- **Gifts/direct mail — proposed role:** after a relationship exists, ask whether a modest book, workshop meal or printed technical guide is permitted and where it should be sent. Record gift approval, value and delivery separately from sales stage. Do not obtain a home address from enrichment or make a gift conditional on a purchase. For regulated/public-sector accounts, get the recipient organization's actual policy. A fresh, controlled OSS-infrastructure gift-to-revenue benchmark was **not verified**; sending gifts is not a mandatory sequence step. ([GitLab documents direct mail as a distinct field-program type](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/growth-marketing/regional-marketing/), updated 2026-10-30)

### Legal operating boundaries: US, EU and Canada

- **US — CAN-SPAM:** the FTC says the law covers commercial B2B email, not only bulk mail. Requirements include accurate headers/subjects, advertising identification, a valid physical postal address and a clear opt-out. Honor opt-outs within **10 business days** and keep the mechanism functioning for at least **30 days** after sending. Outsourcing outreach does not eliminate the advertiser's responsibility. Proposed operation: suppress immediately rather than use the legal maximum as a service target. ([FTC compliance guide](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business), original page 2023 [OLD], maintained guidance accessed 2026-11-25)
- **EU — legitimate interest is conditional:** assess a legitimate purpose, necessity and the person's interests/rights; reasonable expectations matter. Direct marketing is not automatically prohibited, but neither is “B2B” a blanket exemption. GDPR processing grounds and national rules implementing ePrivacy for electronic marketing must both be satisfied. EDPB's cited 2024 text is a **consultation-version guideline**, not a claim that a 2025 final version was verified. A country-specific sender matrix is needed before EU outreach. ([EDPB Guidelines 1/2024, version 1.0](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2024-10/edpb_guidelines_202401_legitimateinterest_en.pdf), adopted 2024-10-08; [European Commission legal grounds](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **EU — proposed CRM controls:** record the lawful basis, assessment owner/date, source of the contact, transparency-notice delivery, objections and retention review. The European Commission points to GDPR Articles 14 and 21 for information/objection obligations; an objection to direct marketing must stop that processing, rather than trigger another balancing exercise; do not treat a vendor's “GDPR compliant” badge as your own assessment. UK ICO guidance is useful for UK work but is not evidence of every EU member state's electronic-marketing law. ([European Commission legal-basis guidance](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/legal-grounds-processing-data_en), n.d.; [ICO marketing planning guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/direct-marketing-guidance/plan-direct-marketing/), n.d.; both accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Canada — CASL:** establish express consent, a qualifying implied-consent basis or an applicable exemption, and meet identification/unsubscribe requirements. A conspicuously published address can support implied consent only under conditions: no no-solicitation statement and a message relevant to the role. A third party copying/selling an address does not itself create that basis. The sender must prove consent; retain the original publication and relevance rationale. A public GitHub profile is not general Canadian email permission. ([CRTC FAQ](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm), n.d.; [CRTC consent guidance](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/guide.htm), n.d.; both accessed 2026-11-25)

## 4. Be visible in the account's professional world, without stalking it

### Account-based advertising and platform mechanics

- **LinkedIn Matched Audiences:** company/contact matching and retargeting are documented, but a campaign needs at least **300 matched members**, not 300 companies or uploaded rows. Geography and additional filters can shrink the usable audience. A small Dream 100 can therefore fail to deliver after you narrow it to the right people; do not enlarge it with irrelevant people merely to satisfy the threshold. EEA/Swiss matching is also affected by member consent. ([LinkedIn Matched Audiences best practices](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a424450), exact update date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed LinkedIn ad design:** group targets by a shared problem—provider multi-tenancy, enterprise virtualization exit or platform upgrades—then match companies and relevant functions. Exclude customers where inappropriate, check expansion settings and track delivery by company when reporting supports it. Use a small holdout cohort and measure qualified conversations over a suitable lag. No verified 2025–2026 causal revenue study for a tiny OSS-infrastructure Dream 100 audience was found. ([LinkedIn company-targeting guidance](https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/ads/best-practices/matched-audiences-tips), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Reddit is principally contextual here:** community targeting covers people who subscribed to, viewed or visited selected communities within **28 days**, and does not mean ads appear only inside that subreddit or reach verified employees of your accounts. Automated targeting can broaden audience combinations. Use it for a relevant technical problem, not a claim that you can identify the Kubernetes buyer at a particular enterprise. ([Reddit audience mechanics](https://business.reddithelp.com/articles/Knowledge/Overview-Reddit-Ads-Audience-and-Targeting), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Dated change:** Reddit documentation says automated targeting became required for lookalike campaigns starting **March 2026**. An old playbook promising a strictly bounded lookalike audience is therefore unsafe to reuse. Its audience forecasting also changed in **September 2026**; estimated-size changes should not be mistaken for real audience growth. ([Reddit custom audiences](https://business.reddithelp.com/articles/Knowledge/custom-audiences), n.d., policy date 2026-03; [Reddit targeting overview](https://business.reddithelp.com/articles/Knowledge/Overview-Reddit-Ads-Audience-and-Targeting), n.d., forecasting change 2026-09; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Useful public presence and evidence of participation

- **Proposed account content:** write a reusable technical artifact for a real operating constraint, then send the appropriate section to a relevant contact. For a hoster: tenant isolation, per-tenant operations and unit economics; for an enterprise: upgrade/rollback tests, support responsibility and migration risk. Show versions, hardware, commands and failure cases. Avoid inserting the target's logo into a pseudo-customer page or presenting inferred architecture as confirmed. ([Michelin's transparent operator case is a useful standard for evidence](https://www.cncf.io/case-studies/michelin/), published 2025-06-12)
- **Podcasts/newsletters/meetups:** ask existing contacts what they actually read or attend; record named channels and dates in the account map. Pitch a technical contribution suited to the host's audience, disclose commercial affiliation and label paid placement. Evil Martians' business-development lead documented in-person interviews with Sentry, WorkOS, Resend, PlanetScale, Chroma and Mastra founders during 2025; this demonstrates access through a substantive practitioner-interview format, not a quantified pipeline result. ([Victoria Melnikova's firsthand write-up](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/what-devtools-founders-making-100m-arr-told-in-san-francisco), published 2026-01-07)
- **OSS contribution:** fix a reproducible bug, add a missing integration test or improve documentation in a project the account genuinely uses. Follow the project's contribution process and retain the PR as evidence of work; do not attach a sales pitch to review discussions. A merged PR demonstrates collaboration, not an obligation to buy or permission to harvest maintainer contacts. ([GitHub acceptable-use rules](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Concrete visibility case:** the CNCF recording of Cloupard's DevSpace talk describes building a public-cloud service on Cozystack, including its architecture and component choices. It supplies a relevant operator-to-operator asset for a hosting-provider conversation. The public recording does not disclose meetings, pipeline or revenue generated by the talk; that result is **not verified**. ([Cloupard practitioner talk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=klbPnqv5HmA), published 2026-06-09)

### Persistence versus harassment: operational examples

- **Acceptable proposed pattern:** respond to a public technical question with a reproducible answer, ask once whether a relevant private discussion would help, and honor the answer. **Unacceptable pattern:** a prospect declines, so the seller contacts their personal phone, partner and boss, tags them repeatedly or uses another account. Silence is not encouragement to add channels indefinitely. LinkedIn reserves account restrictions for violations, including prohibited automation and misuse. ([LinkedIn User Agreement](https://www.linkedin.com/legal/user-agreement), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Actual failure evidence versus invented stories:** the Proxycurl proceedings and Scarf's telemetry backlash are documented; a named 2025–2026 OSS vendor banned specifically for a “Dream 100 persistence” campaign was **not verified**. Do not invent one. Proposed prevention: shared suppression, contact-frequency review, no household targeting, no fabricated familiarity and no sensitive personal facts in personalization. ([LinkedIn Proxycurl announcement](https://news.linkedin.com/2025/linkedin-takes-legal-action-to-defend-member-privacy), published 2025-01-24; [Scarf retrospective](https://about.scarf.sh/post/building-scarf-avi-press-on-haskell-telemetry-and-open-source-challenges/), published 2024-07-19, background)

## 5. Building trust with engineers and executives

### Give-first offers that can be assessed technically

- **Proposed architecture review:** use only the account's published talk, diagram or repository; state what is observable, what is assumed and which questions remain. Offer a private one-page review with two trade-offs and one reproducible experiment. Do not scan infrastructure or imply a vulnerability without authorization. A useful offer for Cozystack's reference class is “review our tenant-isolation test plan against your published requirements,” rather than “your infrastructure is broken.” ([Cloupard's public architecture talk provides an example of permissible input](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=klbPnqv5HmA), published 2026-06-09)
- **Proposed benchmark package:** include exact versions, hardware/storage/network configuration, dataset, warm/cold cache conditions, repetition count, variance, workload generator and raw output. Separate engineering findings from estimated cost. Include an unfavorable result and conditions under which the incumbent is preferable. This package is a proposed trust artifact; a verified general benchmark-to-enterprise-close rate was **not verified**. ([Michelin operator case](https://www.cncf.io/case-studies/michelin/), published 2025-06-12)
- **Measured reference, not your ROI promise — Michelin:** after rebuilding its Kubernetes environment with open-source tools, the published case reports **44% lower platform costs**, **85% lower upgrade lead time** and a doubled footprint; it describes **62 clusters in 42 locations and 850 worker nodes**. These are case-specific operational results, not proof that your platform, an ABM campaign or a single component caused the same savings. Use the case to ask which cost/upgrade measures the buyer uses. ([CNCF/Michelin](https://www.cncf.io/case-studies/michelin/), published 2025-06-12)
- **Proposed introductions and peer help:** introduce an operator who solved the same problem only after both sides agree. Offer an incident-review template, migration decision tree or relevant maintainer conversation without demanding a meeting in return. Track whether the recipient found it useful rather than assigning an artificial dollar value to goodwill. PostHog's actual sales principles emphasize technical competence, transparent answers and refusing unsuitable deals. ([PostHog sales overview](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/overview), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Advisory boards, roundtables and reference calls

- **Proposed advisory invitation:** state the actual topic, expected preparation/time, compensation if any, confidentiality and what product decisions the participant can influence. Do not rename a sales demo an “advisory board.” For a first seller, a small operator roundtable on upgrades or service-provider isolation is more credible than an honorary title with no governance. GitLab documents interactive executive roundtables, delegate research and pain-focused notes; no controlled conversion lift is published there. ([GitLab regional-marketing practice](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/growth-marketing/regional-marketing/), updated 2026-10-30)
- **Proposed reference-call process:** establish the buyer's technical uncertainty first; match scale/deployment constraints; ask the reference's permission for this particular call; share three questions and allow candid discussion without seller pressure. Record what can be quoted publicly separately. For infrastructure, ask about incidents, upgrades, staffing, migration surprises and support escalation—not just whether the reference “likes the product.” No recent controlled reference-call uplift was verified. ([CNCF's customer-led evidence format](https://www.cncf.io/case-studies/michelin/), published 2025-06-12)
- **Peer community case:** CNCF's 2025 Envoy Gateway retrospective reported **211 contributors across 54 companies** and described recurring end-user meetings. It demonstrates a real shared technical forum; it does not establish a prospect database or sales entitlement. Participate by helping with the work, with commercial affiliation visible. ([Envoy Gateway community retrospective](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2025/06/11/a-year-of-envoy-gateway-ga-building-growing-and-innovating-together/), published 2025-06-11)
- **What fails:** PostHog explicitly rejects winning deals at any cost, including commitments that distort its product direction or create poor-fit long-term relationships. Its enterprise guidance avoids contracted feature deliverables and requires users to try the product before demanding changes. This is a practitioner's operating policy, not evidence that all negotiated enterprise development is bad. A named 2025–2026 failed advisory-board sales experiment with measured losses was **not verified**. ([PostHog sales overview](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/overview), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

## 6. In person: conferences, offices, local communities and US relocation

### Conferences: lawful discovery and a practical six-week workflow

- **Attendee access is not a purchased entitlement:** Linux Foundation's KubeCon NA 2025 sponsor page says sponsor reports include people whose badges were scanned at that sponsor's booth. This is not a promise of every attendee's email address. Use the public schedule, speaker bios, opt-in networking facilities and contacts who voluntarily announce attendance; treat unsolicited offers to sell a complete conference list as unverified. ([KubeCon NA sponsor rules](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/archive/2025/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/sponsor/), 2025 event page; exact publication date not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed T−6 to T−3 weeks:** intersect the account list with speakers, sponsors, public attendance announcements and existing relationships. Offer two short slots tied to a specific question; track invited, accepted, declined and uncertain separately. Reserve travel and private rooms only against a reasonable meeting plan. Start this process for KubeCon, re:Invent or Open Source Summit, but check each event's own networking and sponsorship terms. **Three to six weeks is a planning recommendation, not a verified optimum.** ([GitLab's conference meeting-setting process](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/growth-marketing/regional-marketing/), updated 2026-10-30)
- **Proposed T−2 weeks to event day:** send attendees the exact purpose, host, location and duration; reconfirm shortly before the meeting; leave walking time between venues. For a side dinner, invite a small coherent peer group, disclose sponsors, ask dietary/accessibility needs and avoid forcing a presentation. No verified rule establishes that eight guests or a particular dinner spend maximizes enterprise conversion. ([GitLab executive-roundtable practice](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/growth-marketing/regional-marketing/), updated 2026-10-30)
- **At a booth or after a talk — proposed script:** “Your point about upgrading tenant clusters was useful. We maintain a platform in that area; could I ask one technical question?” Keep the initial exchange short, do not obstruct a queue or interrupt another conversation, and ask permission for a later discussion. At your own booth, identify the visitor's actual role/problem before scanning; explain the follow-up they will receive. ([KubeCon sponsor badge-scan terms](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/archive/2025/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/sponsor/), 2025 event page, publication day not verified)
- **Proposed within 48 hours:** deliver the promised link or introduction, summarize the specific issue and propose only the next step discussed. Log notes immediately enough to preserve context, then maintain a 30/60/90-day account-outcome view. **48 hours is a service target**, not a verified causal cutoff. Do not add all badge scans to an indefinite sequence or call a scan a sales-qualified opportunity. ([GitLab event follow-up/task templates](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/field-marketing/field-marketing-epics/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Numbers and failure cases worth teaching

- **Conference benchmark:** KubeCon NA 2025's transparency report publishes **90,571 total leads and 288 average leads per booth**. This is a conference lead/scan measure across booths, not unique buyers, qualified pipeline or a prediction for a startup stand. The report does not provide a comparable median startup conversion-to-revenue rate; do not divide total leads by unique sponsors and assume the denominators match. ([CNCF transparency report, sponsor section](https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/KCNA25_TransparencyReport.pdf), published 2025-12, exact day not verified)
- **Firsthand operating numbers — PostHog:** its October 2025 planning issue says **15 events per quarter and a $10,000 monthly budget** felt appropriate for meeting champions, customers and learning. It identifies cooking-class/workshop combinations, breakfast sessions, founder firesides and conference speaking as formats worth repeating. That is a team's qualitative assessment plus spend/activity counts; no attributable revenue or comparison group is given. ([PostHog public planning issue](https://github.com/PostHog/requests-for-comments-public/issues/395), published 2025-10-09)
- **Failure/constraint in the same evidence:** PostHog's planners explicitly wanted to focus on key products, say no more often and find larger opportunities. Its sales overview separately warns that events are not a scalable/automatable sales channel. These sources can coexist: an event program may serve community/customer learning without being every seller's primary acquisition motion. They do not establish that events categorically stopped working. ([PostHog planning issue](https://github.com/PostHog/requests-for-comments-public/issues/395), published 2025-10-09; [sales overview](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/overview), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed event economics:** count ticket, travel, lodging, food, sponsorship, production and founder hours; divide by held ICP meetings and later qualified opportunities. Example arithmetic, not a market quote: a $6,000 trip producing six held relevant meetings costs $1,000 per held meeting; if two become qualified opportunities, cash cost is $3,000 each before labor. Keep sourced and influenced revenue separate and avoid attributing an existing late-stage deal entirely to a dinner. ([CNCF transparency report illustrates why raw leads need qualification](https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/KCNA25_TransparencyReport.pdf), published 2025-12, exact day not verified)

### Visiting offices and running useful working sessions

- **Verified practitioner model — PostHog:** its onsite guidance prioritizes accounts paying or likely to pay **$50,000+/year**, with an expansion, renewal or technical/organizational obstacle. It recommends seller-owned preparation rather than making the champion organize everything. Session examples include a roughly one-hour working session, a 30–60-minute champion discussion and a 30-minute executive summary. This is PostHog's heuristic, not a universal minimum contract value or measured ROI threshold. ([PostHog onsite handbook](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/customer-onsites), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Original onsite request:** “I will be in your city on [dates]. If your platform team wants to examine the upgrade/rollback issue we discussed, I can bring our maintainer for a 60-minute working session. We will provide the agenda and test environment. Would that be useful, and who should join?” Offer remote participation and a neutral venue if office attendance is low. An executive briefing should address cost, delivery risk and operating responsibility; an engineer session should produce a test or decision. ([PostHog onsite session model](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/customer-onsites), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **What US offices demonstrably accept:** approved technical events with named hosts and registration—not unannounced vendor drop-ins. ### Founder relocation: evidence, useful hypotheses and a reversible plan

- **Dated adjacent-company evidence — Plain:** its European founders announced an SF office to support a growing customer base alongside their $15M financing in February 2025. This supports a customer-proximity rationale, but the announcement does not measure relocation's effect on revenue or prove that the founders' move caused growth. A precise causal Europe-to-US relocation result for an OSS-infrastructure first seller was **not verified**. ([Plain founders' announcement](https://www.plain.com/blog/we-ve-raised-a-15m-series-a), published 2025-02-10)
- **[OLD] Direct OSS founder experience — PostHog:** the founders wrote that joining YC in San Francisco provided access to a dense founder network; they did not plan to remain permanently for family reasons and intended frequent visits. This is a historical alternative to permanent relocation, not fresh evidence about 2026 sales conditions. ([PostHog founders' fundraising account](https://medium.com/posthog/how-we-raised-3m-for-an-open-source-project-daf631be2fe0), published 2020, exact day not verified [OLD])
- **Fresh founder interviews and counterexample:** Sifted/AVP surveyed **250+ founders**, approximately evenly split between Europe and the US, in March–early April 2025. Nabla cofounder Delphine Groll describes more spontaneous help and coffee invitations after moving to New York, but also a **six-month visa wait** associated with her particular application circumstances. These are firsthand observations in a sponsored research report, not representative visa processing times or proof of sales uplift; Nabla is adjacent AI software, not infrastructure. ([Sifted/AVP original report, methodology and pages 17/20](https://www.datocms-assets.com/60124/1748430884-transatlantic-founder-index.pdf), published 2025-05)
- **Proposed physical-presence hypothesis:** local availability can make repeat coffees, customer working sessions, partner meetings and short-notice events easier. Test this against the accounts' actual decision-maker locations, not corporate headquarters. A US-hosted prospect may have its platform team in Europe; a hoster may be far from SF. Maintain the European engineering relationship rather than turning every US request into an overnight handoff. The claimed mechanism is an inference, not a quantified study result. ([PostHog onsite operating practice](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/customer-onsites), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed before moving:** map buyer locations and partner routes; run a concentrated exploratory trip with pre-booked conversations; verify immigration feasibility; price housing, healthcare and travel; decide which European responsibilities remain covered. A practical go/no-go test could be three accounts requesting repeat working sessions and two partners willing to collaborate. Those numbers are example decision criteria, not a research-backed threshold. ([Sifted/AVP relocation experiences](https://www.datocms-assets.com/60124/1748430884-transatlantic-founder-index.pdf), published 2025-05)
- **Proposed first 90 days:** choose one home hub; keep one recurring practitioner community; schedule account working sessions and partner meetings in geographic clusters; reserve time for follow-up and European engineering overlap. Review net new committee relationships, held ICP meetings, repeat invitations, qualified evaluations and actual cash burn at days 30/60/90. Compare with the pre-move baseline; networking activity alone cannot justify the move. ([Evil Martians' account of in-person practitioner interviews](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/what-devtools-founders-making-100m-arr-told-in-san-francisco), published 2026-01-07)

### Visa realities and costs, without false precision

- **Business travel is not work authorization:** the US State Department allows qualifying B-1 activities such as contract negotiations, consultations and conferences, while distinguishing these from gainful US employment. A conference invitation, US incorporation, coworking desk or investor does not itself authorize operating a business from the US. Citizenship, residence, employment structure and actual activities matter; validate the proposed work pattern before relocating. ([US State Department B-1 fact sheet](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/business/b-1-fact-sheet.html), publication date not verified; accessed via official indexed text 2026-11-25)
- **Paths to assess, not assumed eligibility:** O-1A concerns extraordinary ability; L-1 involves a qualifying overseas/US business relationship and qualifying prior employment; E-2 depends on treaty nationality and investment; International Entrepreneur parole has its own ownership, funding and other conditions and is not a visa. USCIS's entrepreneur overview was blocked with HTTP 403 during direct retrieval; current detailed requirements, fees and processing times were therefore **not verified** here. Use an immigration lawyer and the current official category pages rather than this summary to select a route. ([USCIS entrepreneur pathways](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/options-for-alien-entrepreneurs-to-work-in-the-united-states), n.d.; direct access attempted 2026-11-25, blocked)
- **Coworking mechanics:** WeWork's current plan page documents daily workspace bookings and limited meeting-room credits, with extra charges beyond allowances; location access depends on plan. A “global membership” is not an unlimited private client-meeting budget. Obtain a location-specific quote. Several indexed promotional pages are expired, so a universal current US monthly price was **not verified**. ([WeWork current All Access page](https://www.wework.com/solutions/wework-all-access), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Founder communities:** an investor's portfolio events or a relevant founder community can provide repeated professional encounters, but access may be curated. A June 2026 Unusual Ventures/PostHog dinner explicitly required attendance approval and withheld the address until approval. Do not assume coworking or an accelerator's public event listing provides access to private customer networks. ([Organizer's founder-dinner listing](https://luma.com/sopar50j), event 2026-06-24; publication date not verified)
- **Proposed monthly budget worksheet, illustrative USD assumptions:** housing/utilities $3,500 + food/local travel $1,200 + workspace $350 + insurance/admin $700 + intercity travel/events $1,500 + sales tools $250 = **$7,500/month**, or **$22,500 for 90 days**, before salary, taxes, immigration counsel, deposits, family costs and contingency. These are planning inputs, **not verified market averages or quotations**. Replace each with dated quotes for the actual city. For perspective, PostHog's $10,000/month was an event-program budget, not a founder's living budget. ([WeWork service scope](https://www.wework.com/solutions/wework-all-access), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25; [PostHog event budget](https://github.com/PostHog/requests-for-comments-public/issues/395), published 2025-10-09)

## 7. Initial contacts from zero: build paths through people and partners

### Double opt-in introductions and a forwardable message

- **Proposed introduction workflow:** identify a connector who genuinely knows the relevant person; explain the specific technical reason and give an easy no; provide a short forwardable note; let the connector ask the recipient privately; introduce only after both agree. Record connector, recipient, requested date, status and permission. Do not upload an investor's whole address book or treat their name as blanket endorsement. No reliable 2025–2026 OSS-specific intro-to-meeting benchmark was verified. ([LinkedIn networking guidance](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a548242), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Original forwardable note:** “Alex maintains an open-source Kubernetes cloud platform. Your team's public migration talk overlaps with work on tenant isolation and upgrade rollback. Alex can share a reproducible test plan and would value a 20-minute technical comparison if useful. There is no need to reply if this is outside your remit.” Adapt the roles and claims to reality, attach one evidence link and avoid a deck that makes the connector write the pitch. This is a proposed template, not a sourced practitioner quote. ([CNCF practitioner-talk example](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=klbPnqv5HmA), published 2026-06-09)
- **Investor/advisor network — proposed routine:** send a short list of five named accounts with exact roles, the problem hypothesis, your contribution and why the connector might know them. Ask for relationship-strength judgments, not only intros. Advisors should not imply access they lack. Track accepted introductions, held conversations and qualified outcomes separately; a useful “wrong person, talk to platform operations” correction is progress without being pipeline. ([Plain founders document an actual investor-backed expansion context](https://www.plain.com/blog/we-ve-raised-a-15m-series-a), published 2025-02-10)

### Marketplace and SI co-selling

- **AWS mechanics:** ISV Accelerate is a co-sell program for qualifying software that runs on/integrates with AWS and is sold through Marketplace; ACE supports opportunity collaboration. The current page lists prerequisites including a generally available listing, ACE eligibility, appropriate partner status, **five launched opportunities and 15 qualified ACE opportunities in the past 12 months**, plus other conditions. Listing alone does not guarantee AWS seller introductions. Check current enrollment requirements before allocating work. ([AWS ISV Accelerate](https://aws.amazon.com/partners/programs/isv-accelerate/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Marketplace benchmark and sample:** AWS's channel page cites **50% faster deal closure, 4–5× deal size and 234% ROI** from a Forrester Consulting model based on **14 partner interviews and aggregated data**. These are vendor-promoted channel-practice economics, not a randomized result or a forecast for a first-time OSS founder; publication and observation dates are not verified on the inspected landing page. Keep the claim separate from current program mechanics. ([AWS Marketplace Channel](https://www.aws.amazon.com/marketplace/partners), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed co-sell package:** a one-page problem/ICP brief, deployment compatibility, validated architecture, customer permission to involve the partner, a shared account owner, referral/registration rules and a next joint action. For a bare-metal/private-cloud vendor, first test whether the cloud marketplace fits the product and buyer's procurement path; an SI or regional hoster may be a more natural partner. Do not disguise a competitive platform as AWS consumption growth. ([AWS's seller-brief and ACE guidance](https://aws.amazon.com/isv/resources/the-4-stage-isv-revenue-engine-to-activate-aws-marketplace/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed SI/hosting-partner arrangement:** define who sells migration services, who supports production, who owns the relationship and escalation, how revenue/margin is split, and whether the partner can introduce named accounts with permission. Start with one joint workshop or evaluation and a written handoff. Track qualified joint opportunities and time to first customer result, not signed partner logos. A fresh small-vendor SI conversion benchmark was **not verified**. ([AWS Channel Partner Private Offers mechanics as a documented commercial model](https://www.aws.amazon.com/marketplace/partners), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Community-sourced contact and the “give a talk” route

- **Concrete route:** Cloud Native NYC and SF explicitly invite speakers and demos. Approach the organizer with an abstract, learning outcome, reproducible demo and honest commercial affiliation. Offer to explain a failure or trade-off—such as tenant isolation or an upgrade rollback—rather than deliver a corporate overview. Ask permission to share slides and collect voluntary follow-up requests; never ask for the entire member list as the price of speaking. ([NYC organizer page](https://ocgroups.dev/cncf/group/nyc), n.d.; [SF organizer page](https://ocgroups.dev/cncf/group/cloud-native-sf), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed zero-network month:** choose a narrow account problem; identify two active relevant communities; contribute one useful artifact; submit one practical talk; request a few double-opt-in introductions; hold discovery conversations and revise the account hypothesis. These are workload suggestions, not numerical success promises. GitHub/community participation cannot be converted into a harvested-email campaign merely because the audience is public. ([GitHub information-use restrictions](https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/acceptable-use-policies/github-acceptable-use-policies), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Failure boundary:** sponsorship, an investor relationship or a marketplace listing can buy services or establish eligibility, but none guarantees trust or sales access. A partner who will not agree to a concrete next customer action is not yet a functioning acquisition channel. Current public evidence for an exact “warm intros beat cold by X” figure in this reference class was **not verified**; measure your own pathway by connector and account cohort. ([AWS co-sell guidance explicitly distinguishes listing from active co-selling](https://aws.amazon.com/isv/resources/the-4-stage-isv-revenue-engine-to-activate-aws-marketplace/), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)

## 8. Course implementation exercise and evidence gaps

### Build something testable in four weeks

- **Week 1 — proposed deliverable:** populate 10 real target accounts with a source-backed operating problem, a plausible owner, known unknowns and a lawful route to contact. Create the eight-table database and suppression rules. Acceptance test: another person can open every evidence link, distinguish fact from hypothesis and find the next action without asking the founder. This is a course exercise, not a claim that 10 researched accounts will convert. ([Pipedrive data model](https://support.pipedrive.com/en/article/data-fields-in-pipedrive), updated 2026-09-03)
- **Week 2 — proposed deliverable:** implement one champion-change feed or one first-party hiring feed, with account matching, detection timestamps, deduplication and human review. Test a wrong employer, duplicate signal, stale source, departed champion and opted-out person. Success means the system creates the correct research task and suppresses prohibited action; do not measure success as emails dispatched. ([Clay signal mechanics](https://university.clay.com/docs/job-change-signal-overview), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Week 3 — proposed deliverable:** produce one useful technical artifact, attempt a small number of permitted contacts/intros, submit one meetup proposal and book only appropriate conversations. Log exactly which content and channel each person received. No automatic escalation after silence, and no fake “research” request whose actual purpose is an undisclosed sales pitch. ([PostHog transparency and discovery practice](https://posthog.com/handbook/growth/sales/outbound-sales), n.d.; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Week 4 — proposed deliverable:** review accounts advanced, meetings actually held, buyer-confirmed problems, committee gaps, opt-outs, false signals, spend and founder time. Choose one change for the next cohort. Keep 2025/2026 industry benchmarks beside—not inside—the forecast until their cohorts and denominators match your own. ([Belkins measurement disclosure](https://belkins.io/blog/cold-email-response-rates), updated 2026-06-26)

### Claims the learner should not teach as established fact

- **Not verified:** a universal LinkedIn numeric weekly invitation quota; an exact date for the three-versus-five free-note discrepancy; a guaranteed safe third-party automation volume; an independent reverse-IP precision rate across all infrastructures; a fixed domain-warm-up duration; a causal benefit of eight/twelve touches; a universal 48-hour event-follow-up conversion cliff; or an OSS-enterprise relocation ROI multiplier. Official documentation and current cases support narrower claims, as stated above. ([LinkedIn limits](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a551527), n.d.; [Google sending guidance](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126), n.d.; [Scarf FAQ](https://docs.scarf.sh/faq/), n.d.; all accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Freshness limitation:** Pulumi/Common Room, Railway/Attio, Heap/Champify and Kubegrade/Reo.dev are verifiable published cases whose original dates or measurement windows were not verified. They are retained as transparent implementation examples, not relabeled as 2025–2026 results. For strictly dated teaching evidence, prioritize the 2025 Weaviate/RisingWave interviews, 2025 PostHog planning issue, 2026 Clay playbook, official platform changes and the dated operator/community sources. ([Weaviate interview](https://about.scarf.sh/post/how-weaviate-leverages-open-source-usage-data-to-drive-revops/), published 2025-01-15; [Clay playbook](https://www.clay.com/blog/how-we-turn-a-champions-departure-into-a-buying-signal), published 2026-10-27)
