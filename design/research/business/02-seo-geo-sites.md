# SEO, GEO, technical blogs, and websites for open-source infrastructure vendors

Research cutoff: **25 November 2026**. Prepared as source material for a free public B2B marketing course. Reference audience: maintainers, engineers, DevRel, and commercial teams selling infrastructure built around an open-source project.

## 1. Scope, evidence labels, and an important factual correction

Dates in citations are publication dates unless explicitly labeled “updated,” “report period,” or “accessed.” **n.d.** means the publication date was not verified; an access date is not a substitute publication date. **[OLD]** identifies pre-2024 material. Living documentation is included for current rules even when its original publication date is unavailable. “Verified” means the cited source supports the statement, not that a vendor's private analytics were independently audited. “Observation” means a measured association; “experiment” identifies an intervention, with limitations; “proposal” identifies the course author's operational recommendation. Missing measurements are stated as **not verified**.

- **Correction to the brief:** CNCF's live directory lists Cozystack as **Sandbox**, accepted on 4 March 2025. Its September 2026 announcement describes completion of the general technical review on the path toward incubation, not completed promotion. An Incubating claim is therefore **not verified** at this research cutoff. This is also a practical example of the kind of category/governance error an LLM positioning program should catch. (https://www.cncf.io/projects/cozystack/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25; https://cozystack.io/blog/2026/09/cozystack-cncf-general-technical-review/, 2026-09-29)
- **Interpretation:** appearing, being cited, receiving a visit, and acquiring a customer are separate outcomes. Ahrefs found citations without the promoted brand appearing in 43% of conference answers. Citation counts therefore cannot establish a universal GEO conversion rate. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/, 2026-07-06)
- **Evidence boundary:** broad search studies are useful for understanding mechanisms, but their samples are not benchmarks for Kubernetes infrastructure buyers. Pew observed US adults, while Seer's 2026 analysis covered 53 client brands. Neither establishes the traffic loss or pipeline impact a particular OSS vendor should expect. (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/21/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/, 2025-07-22 as displayed; https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update, 2026-04-24)

## 2. Google Search in 2024–2026: changes that alter the playbook

### 2.1 Helpful content, scaled publishing, and site reputation

- **Verified policy, 5 March 2024:** Google's helpful-content work became part of multiple core ranking systems; it is misleading to treat a separate “Helpful Content Update” as the current system. The accompanying spam changes cover scaled content abuse regardless of whether humans, automation, or both produce it. For infrastructure vendors, generating thousands of near-identical Kubernetes definitions or comparison permutations is not made acceptable merely by having an engineer approve the template. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies, 2024-03-05)
- **Verified policy, 19 November 2024:** site reputation abuse concerns third-party material exploiting a host's established ranking signals. First-party supervision does not automatically exempt it. Ordinary outside contributors are not inherently prohibited; the abusive purpose matters. Moving offending content to another subdirectory or subdomain is not a reliable remedy. This matters to established engineering blogs tempted to rent out their authority to unrelated sponsored commercial content. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse, 2024-11-19, FAQ update 2024-12-06)
- **Verified regional change, effective 30 August 2026:** Google's updated treatment differs in the EEA. Outside the EEA, manual actions continue to affect violating portions; within the EEA, Google describes separating such sections so they rank independently instead of applying that manual-action effect. Notifications and reconsideration remain relevant. Teaching the 2024 enforcement mechanism as globally unchanged in late 2026 would be inaccurate. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/08/update-site-reputation-policy, 2026-08-28)
- **Failure boundary:** a dated policy change establishes a real enforcement risk, but a specific 2025–2026 ban of Cozystack, Talos, Cilium, or another named reference-class project for reputation abuse was **not verified**. Do not turn general Google policy into a fabricated infrastructure-company incident. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/11/site-reputation-abuse, 2024-11-19)

### 2.2 AI Overviews and AI Mode: eligibility and control

- **Verified mechanics:** AI Overviews and AI Mode can issue multiple related searches through query fan-out. A page needs ordinary search eligibility, including indexing and eligibility to display a snippet; no special AI schema or machine-readable “AI SEO” file is required. Googlebot access and controls such as `nosnippet`, `data-nosnippet`, `max-snippet`, and `noindex` matter. Google-Extended controls other uses and is not the switch for inclusion in Search's AI features. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features, updated 2025-12-10)
- **Verified 2026 guidance:** Google's dedicated generative-AI optimization guide emphasizes useful, distinctive material and rejects special AI-only optimization requirements. Its clarification treats `llms.txt` as neither a positive nor a negative Search ranking factor. For an infrastructure publisher, reproducible deployment knowledge is a stronger editorial basis than rearranging every paragraph around a supposed universal “AI chunk size.” No such universally effective size was verified. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide, updated 2026-07-10; https://developers.google.com/search/updates, entry 2026-06-15)
- **What became outdated:** the statement “Search Console provides no separate AI visibility report” was reasonable in earlier reporting but is stale at this cutoff. Google announced dedicated generative-AI performance reports on 3 June 2026 and marked worldwide rollout complete on 31 August. This is a reporting change, not proof that AI-generated answers now send more traffic. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2026/06/gen-ai-performance-reports, 2026-06-03, updated 2026-08-31)

### 2.3 CTR and zero-click evidence: retain the denominators

- **Pew, observed browsing:** 900 US adults generated 68,879 unique Google searches in March 2025; reconstructed results contained 12,593 AI summaries. Users clicked a traditional result in **8%** of visits with a summary versus **15%** without; summary-source clicks occurred in **1%** of visits with a summary. Sessions ended in **26% versus 16%**. Results were reconstructed in April, not captured identically at search time; query selection and user intent differ. This is association, not randomized evidence that adding an overview halves every site's CTR. (https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/21/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/, 2025-07-22 as displayed)
- **Ahrefs, position-one counterfactual:** a 300,000-keyword study compared desktop Search Console data for December 2023 and December 2025, using AIO and informational non-AIO groups. Position-one CTR fell from **7.3% to 1.6%** in the AIO group and **7.6% to 3.9%** in the other group. Applying the comparison-group decline produced an expected AIO CTR of about **3.7%**; actual CTR was **58% lower than that estimate**. This is neither a 58-percentage-point decline nor a measured 58% loss of total site traffic. Its earlier April 2025 estimate was 34.5%. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/, 2026-02-04)
- **Seer, 2025 client cohort:** 3,119 informational terms across 42 clients generated 25.1 million organic and 1.1 million paid impressions. From June 2024 to September 2025, AIO-query organic CTR fell **1.76% to 0.61%**. Cited brands had approximately **35% higher organic CTR and 91% higher paid CTR** than uncited brands in its comparison. Citation was not randomly assigned; brands, positions, and query characteristics differed. The study used a latest-observed AIO classification retrospectively. It does not prove buying “GEO” produces that uplift. (https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update, 2025-11-04)
- **Seer, 2026 update—not the same cohort:** the expanded analysis covered **53 brands, 5.47 million queries, 2.43 billion organic impressions, and 296.9 million paid impressions** from January 2025 through February 2026. AIO organic CTR rose from **1.3% in December to 2.4% in February**, an **85% relative rebound**, but remained below January 2025's 3.2%. Non-AIO CTR moved from 2.8% to 3.8% over the longer period. Latest-observed AIO classification and a 2025 linear projection without seasonality limit causal interpretation. This contradicts a simple story of uninterrupted CTR collapse, not necessarily Ahrefs's differently designed estimate. (https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-2026-update, 2026-04-24)
- **Similarweb, clickstream:** its June 2026 account reports **68% zero-click Google searches** and attributes the data to its panel and the corresponding SparkToro study. This describes search behavior, not a 68% loss of publisher traffic caused by AI. Its discussion of roughly **80% desktop zero-click behavior** must not be relabeled “80% of B2B searches”: device is not buyer identity. Panel coverage, geography, time window, and the treatment of Google-owned destinations must accompany any classroom reuse. (https://aisearch.similarweb.com/blog/zero-click-marketing/, 2026-06-10)
- **Interpretation of disagreement:** Pew measures click behavior per observed search visit, Ahrefs estimates position-one CTR relative to a comparison trajectory, and Seer measures impressions and clicks in client query cohorts. Their percentages answer different questions. The defensible vendor exercise is a same-query, same-country/device, same-position analysis of its own data, with branded and nonbranded queries separated; these studies provide hypotheses, not interchangeable loss multipliers. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks-update/, 2026-02-04; https://www.seerinteractive.com/insights/aio-impact-on-google-ctr-september-2025-update, 2025-11-04)

## 3. How AI products choose citations: observed sources, not a universal ranking formula

### 3.1 ChatGPT, Perplexity, Google, and Claude differ

- **Profound, cross-platform observation:** its analysis of **680 million citations**, August 2024–June 2025, reported total citation shares including ChatGPT **Wikipedia 7.8%, Reddit 1.8%**; Google AI Overviews **Reddit 2.2%, YouTube 1.9%, Wikipedia 0.6%**; and Perplexity **Reddit 6.6%, YouTube 2.0%**. These are vendor-panel observations, not a representative census of infrastructure questions. The widely repeated **47.9% Wikipedia** statistic uses ChatGPT's **top ten cited domains** as its denominator, not all citations. (https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns, 2025-06-05, updated 2025-08)
- **Semrush, instability over time:** 230,000 prompts, more than 100 million citations, and weekly observations over 13 weeks from July to October 2025 showed sharp changes in ChatGPT's reliance on Reddit and Wikipedia. Reddit appeared in roughly **60% of responses in August versus 10% in September**; Wikipedia fell from around 55% to below 20%. These are response-presence rates, not shares of citation links. The analysis covers ChatGPT Search, Google AI Mode, and Perplexity—not every Gemini or Claude experience. Suggested causes of the shift were not established experimentally. (https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/, 2025-11-10)
- **Muck Rack, broader 2026 source study:** across **25 million-plus links** and 17 industries, its report found sources in **96% of ChatGPT responses, 82% of Gemini responses, and 55% of Claude responses**. The leading domains differed: Wikipedia, Reddit, and PubMed Central respectively. About **84%** of citations were classified as earned, **27%** as journalism, and **0.3%** as paid/advertorial. “Earned” includes another company's blog relative to the target company; it does not mean 84% were independent news reporting. Prompt mix and web-enabled model configuration matter; these are not infrastructure-specific shares. (https://media.muckrack.com/documents/What_Is_AI_Reading__May_2026.pdf, 2026-05, publication day not verified)
- **Evidence gap—documentation share:** these studies do not establish docs citation shares for Kubernetes procurement or troubleshooting. Corporate-domain categories can hide documentation. “LLMs prefer Reddit to infrastructure docs” is **not verified** by aggregate domain rankings. (https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns, 2025-06-05, updated 2025-08; https://media.muckrack.com/documents/What_Is_AI_Reading__May_2026.pdf, 2026-05)

### 3.2 What the evidence supports—and what it does not

- **Observed correlation, not a recipe:** Ahrefs's study of 75,000 brands reported a Spearman correlation of **0.664** between web mentions and AIO visibility, versus **0.218** for backlinks; branded search volume correlated at **0.392**. Better-known companies have more mentions and many other advantages. The result supports investigating external recognition, but cannot establish that purchasing a fixed number of mentions causes a predictable increase in citations. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/, 2025-05-26)
- **Proposal for technical audiences:** test sources by task: versioned docs for upgrades, reproducible tests for performance, and support terms plus user evidence for procurement. Aggregate domain popularity does not justify manufactured Reddit discussions or interchangeable videos. Prompt type changes cited sources. (https://media.muckrack.com/documents/What_Is_AI_Reading__May_2026.pdf, 2026-05)
- **What stopped being a safe assumption:** a fixed list of “winning citation domains” cannot be treated as a durable 12-month strategy. Semrush observed large changes within a single quarter. Preserve the actual answers and source URLs when monitoring; a dashboard's current domain distribution cannot reconstruct which sources a buyer saw months earlier. (https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/, 2025-11-10)

## 4. llms.txt, machine-readable docs, crawler access, and structured data

### 4.1 llms.txt: adoption is real; ranking benefit remains unproven

- **Verified specification status:** `llms.txt` is a proposal for a concise Markdown entry point to useful site resources, not a robots permission file or a universally implemented search standard. The living specification includes adoption examples involving OpenAI, Anthropic, and Gemini documentation. Publishing an index demonstrates that a publisher makes content available; it does not demonstrate that the publisher's own search engine uses other sites' indexes for ranking. (https://llmstxt.org/, published 2024-09-03, modified 2026-08-10)
- **Ahrefs usage study:** among **137,210 domains** with May 2026 traffic in Ahrefs Web Analytics, **28%** published a valid `llms.txt`; **97%** of those files received zero requests that month. Among requests to accessed files, **19.5%** came from named AI tools, including GPTBot and Claude-Code. This refutes both “every engine uses it” and “no AI tool ever fetches it.” The sample is self-selected, root-file observation does not cover every Markdown endpoint, and a fetch is not evidence of citation or ranking improvement. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/, 2026-06-15)
- **Google product distinction:** Chrome's experimental agentic-browsing Lighthouse audit can assess `llms.txt`, while Search documentation says it is unnecessary for Search visibility. The audit treats an absent file differently from a broken existing endpoint; this is tooling for agent interaction, not a new Google Search eligibility requirement. (https://developer.chrome.com/docs/lighthouse/agentic-browsing/llms-txt, updated 2026-05-05; https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide, updated 2026-07-10)
- **Mintlify, operational evidence:** its July-to-date sample recorded **213 million agent requests versus 105 million human page loads**, with **83.7%** of agent traffic using explicitly machine-oriented routes. In a **2,400-run, 20-site** comparison of agent access formats, richer Markdown/index arrangements substantially reduced failures, with the reported error reduction approaching 90%. These are documentation-consumption outcomes in a platform vendor's sample—not proof of more ChatGPT recommendations, search visits, or paid conversions. Requests also are not unique readers. (https://www.mintlify.com/blog/state-of-docs-traffic, 2026-07-29)
- **GitBook, separate denominator:** for 27 April–3 May 2026, among sites exceeding 100 human views in April, GitBook counted **61.2 million requests**: **36.2% humans, 38.9% agents, 24.1% crawlers**, with a small remainder. Its **51.8% agent share of intentional reads** excludes crawlers. This does not conflict directly with Mintlify's 66%: platforms, dates, classifications, and denominators differ. A later GitBook account also describes scanner traffic being mistaken for agents, reinforcing the need to audit classification. (https://www.gitbook.com/blog/ai-docs-data-april-2026, 2026-05-13; https://www.gitbook.com/blog/bot-traffic-vs-agent-traffic, 2026-08-27)
- **Decision for an OSS docs team—proposal:** generate a small, maintained index and clean Markdown from the same source as HTML when inexpensive, and test whether a coding agent can find installation prerequisites and supported versions. Do not put unmaintained claims in a separate `llms-full.txt` universe. Budget this as developer usability; a controlled increase in search citations attributable to `llms.txt` alone remains **not verified** in the reviewed evidence. (https://llmstxt.org/, modified 2026-08-10; https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/, 2026-06-15)

### 4.2 Crawler access is not one switch

- **Verified OpenAI controls:** `OAI-SearchBot` supports search, `GPTBot` concerns model-training crawling, and `ChatGPT-User` identifies user-triggered access. Their purposes and controls differ. OpenAI publishes IP information for verification; a user-agent string alone is weak evidence of identity. Allowing search discovery while making a different training-crawl choice is possible. Search visibility is not guaranteed by permitting a bot. (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Operational failure mode:** an anti-bot challenge or blanket AI-bot block can prevent useful retrieval even when the content is public to a browser. Conversely, high crawl volume does not imply high human referrals. Cloudflare's Radar work explicitly compares crawling with referred traffic; this is a better basis for cost and access decisions than counting all bot requests as “AI audience growth.” Its network-level observations are not a per-vendor conversion benchmark. (https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-search-crawl-refer-ratio-on-radar/, 2025-07-01)

### 4.3 Structured data that still has a job

- **Breadcrumbs for documentation:** Google still supports `BreadcrumbList` for desktop Search. Model the real documentation hierarchy and validate representative pages with Rich Results Test and URL Inspection. This can clarify context for deep documentation results; it does not guarantee display or an AI citation boost. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/breadcrumb, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

- **Organization identity:** truthful `Organization` information can clarify a company's name, logo, URL, and related profiles. For a company/project split, distinguish the commercial organization from the software project instead of assigning both the same identity. Markup must agree with visible content; it does not guarantee a knowledge panel, Wikipedia entry, or LLM recommendation. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/organization, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Article metadata:** technical posts benefit from accurate headline, author identity, images, and publication/modification dates through supported Article markup. A substantive upgrade to a benchmark warrants an explained revision; changing a timestamp without changing the substance is not evidence of freshness. Use markup to describe the actual publication, not to manufacture expertise. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25; https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/gen-ai-attribution-factuality, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **What stopped working:** Google retired multiple rich-result types in June 2025, including course information and learning videos; removal was not a general ranking penalty. FAQ rich results were discontinued in May 2026, according to the dated Search documentation change log. A vendor should not forecast extra SERP real estate from FAQ markup simply because an older SEO checklist recommends it. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2025/06/simplifying-search-results, 2025-06-12; https://developers.google.com/search/updates, entry 2026-05-08 documenting discontinuation on 2026-05-07)
- **[OLD] Historical context:** HowTo rich results had already been removed in September 2023, while FAQ eligibility was restricted before the later discontinuation. This source explains why older tutorials disagree; it is not a current growth tactic. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2023/08/howto-faq-changes, 2023-08-08, updated 2023-09-14)
- **Measured schema experiment:** Ahrefs compared **1,885 pages adding JSON-LD** with **4,000 matched controls**, using 30-day windows around additions between August 2025 and March 2026. Difference-in-differences estimates were **−4.6% for AIO**, **+2.4% for AI Mode**, and **+2.2% for ChatGPT**; only the first was statistically significant. Existing decline and remaining confounding prevent a causal “schema hurts” conclusion. The useful finding is absence of convincing evidence for a universal schema-driven AI citation boost. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/schema-ai-citations/, 2026-05-11)

## 5. Documentation as SEO and as the product experience

### 5.1 What can be demonstrated publicly

- **CNCF's operational criteria:** its documentation review framework checks public access, production-only analytics, Search Console ownership, search, errors, maintainability, and machine-oriented access. This supports treating documentation infrastructure as part of discoverability and onboarding. It does not establish that every project implementing the checklist receives a particular organic-growth percentage. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analysis/criteria/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Concrete failure, KubeVirt's 2026 review:** CNCF's published analysis identifies gaps including absent user-guide analytics, preview-site analytics contamination, sitemap/robots problems, unclear analytics ownership, and fragmented search arrangements. These are actionable failures: a team cannot diagnose traffic or onboarding if measurement is missing or polluted. The review does not publish a before/after GSC click recovery; such a recovery is **not verified**. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analyses/2026/kubevirt/analysis/, 2026 report, publication day not verified)
- **Evidence gap—public GSC:** a reusable 2025–2026, project-level Search Console export proving that a Kubernetes ecosystem docs redesign generated a specified increase in nonbranded clicks was **not verified** in this research. Public audits and estimated channel shares are available, but neither substitutes for query/page-level GSC data with migration dates, device/country filters, and comparable windows. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analyses/2026/kubevirt/analysis/, 2026 report, publication day not verified; https://www.similarweb.com/website/kubernetes.io/, October 2026 report period, accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Concrete implementation, Pulumi:** the Kubernetes provider registry combines versioned package information, installation/configuration, API resources, examples, schema, and source links. The inspected page identifies **v4.34.3, published 2 November 2026**. It demonstrates useful scalable documentation grounded in a real software interface. Incremental SEO traffic, trial starts, or revenue caused by this package-page architecture are **not verified**. (https://www.pulumi.com/registry/packages/kubernetes/, package publication 2026-11-02)

### 5.2 Applying this to an infrastructure project

- **Proposal—separate the user's tasks:** maintain accessible paths for evaluation, first installation, production architecture, upgrades, troubleshooting, and contribution. For Cozystack-like products, supported hardware, networking/storage prerequisites, tenancy boundaries, backup/restore, and upgrade compatibility should be answerable before a sales conversation. This is an editorial application of CNCF documentation criteria, not a measured promise that adding these pages increases conversion. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analysis/criteria/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—version truth over maximum indexing:** keep maintained releases discoverable and clearly label older instructions. Decide deliberately which obsolete/generated variants should remain searchable; do not mechanically canonicalize every old version to “latest” when instructions materially differ. Test a real operator query against rendered HTML and the site's search. The recurring failure to prevent is a valid-looking answer that installs the wrong version. Docusaurus provides metadata controls, but editorial version policy remains the publisher's responsibility. (https://docusaurus.io/docs/seo, living documentation v3.10.2, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Agent use changes the conversion path:** a human may never read the page that an assistant uses to produce a working deployment command. Measure installation success, documented integration use, and opt-in product activation separately from page views. GitBook's request evidence supports the existence of this consumption pattern, but it does not establish the percentage of OSS infrastructure buyers using agents or a standard agent-to-paid conversion rate. (https://www.gitbook.com/blog/ai-docs-data-april-2026, 2026-05-13)

## 6. Programmatic SEO: integrations, comparisons, and glossaries

### 6.1 Where the substantive value comes from

- **Verified implementation, not a traffic case study:** Pulumi's registry illustrates a useful programmatic unit: a real provider or resource with real configuration, version, examples, and schema. For an infrastructure vendor, an integration page should similarly answer actual compatibility and setup questions. Pages for nonexistent integrations or token-swapped “alternative to X” combinations lack that underlying product substance. No traffic uplift is claimed for Pulumi here. (https://www.pulumi.com/registry/packages/kubernetes/, package publication 2026-11-02)
- **Practitioner practice:** PostHog's public content handbook treats comparisons, tutorials, opinionated writing, and evergreen material as distinct jobs, uses dedicated technical writers, and rejects arbitrary volume targets. It is a useful reference for publishing technically meaningful “X vs Y” material. The handbook does not disclose an isolated 2025–2026 conversion lift attributable to comparison pages; that result is **not verified**. (https://posthog.com/handbook/content, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—comparison data model:** compare supported workloads, self-hosting requirements, license, control-plane ownership, upgrade responsibility, multi-tenancy, support, and total operating assumptions. Cite both products' current docs and date the comparison. State when the competitor is a better fit. This is an application of accurate technical publishing; the evidence does not support a guaranteed ranking advantage for the literal title “X vs Y.” (https://posthog.com/handbook/content, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

### 6.2 Measured intervention and a useful failure

- **Ahrefs intervention:** across **34 pages on five domains and 9,886 AI answers** collected February–May 2026, self-promotional content produced different outcomes for an established brand and a less-established conference. For Evolve, **72 previously empty prompt/engine combinations** began mentioning it; **82%** cited experimental content. For Ahrefs, only **6%** of new mentions cited the experimental pages, while **94%** cited third-party sources. The design was not randomized, and changes elsewhere could contribute. This is evidence that content can sometimes alter retrieved answers, not proof that mass page generation works. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/, 2026-07-06)
- **Failure interpretation:** 43% of conference answers citing experimental pages omitted the promoted conference. A comparison can win a citation while failing to position its publisher. Inspect recommendations, not just cited URLs. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/, 2026-07-06)
- **What stopped being defensible:** scaling a glossary primarily to capture rankings is exposed to Google's scaled-content policy regardless of production method. That is different from generating complete API reference pages from source definitions. The relevant distinction is usefulness and purpose, not “programmatic versus handcrafted.” A verified 2025–2026 first-party case showing an OSS infrastructure glossary's incremental pipeline, or a named reference-class vendor banned specifically for such a glossary, was **not verified**. (https://developers.google.com/search/blog/2024/03/core-update-spam-policies, 2024-03-05)

## 7. Technical SEO for Hugo and Docusaurus, including internationalization

### 7.1 A reviewable implementation checklist

- **Docusaurus mechanics:** use its supported global metadata and per-page metadata facilities; inspect generated HTML rather than assuming client-side navigation proves search accessibility. Set page-specific titles and descriptions and inspect canonical output on the deployed hostname. Check production builds of installation docs, API references, blog entries, and locale pages. This is implementation verification, not a claim that metadata alone changes rankings. (https://docusaurus.io/docs/seo, living documentation v3.10.2, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Hugo mechanics:** Hugo generates sitemaps and supports multilingual sitemap arrangements, with language-specific files and an index. Its current documentation describes template and front-matter controls. Check the installed Hugo version before copying old template paths; verify the generated sitemap contains public canonical URLs, not previews, removed pages, or an obsolete domain. A sitemap is a discovery aid, not a guarantee of indexing. (https://gohugo.io/templates/sitemap/, updated 2026-11-08)
- **Deployment acceptance—proposal:** inspect HTML without JavaScript; verify explanation, code, links, title, status, redirects, canonical, robots, sitemap, and 404. Test browser and permitted-agent access. Accidental `noindex`, WAF blocks, and broken paths precede speculative GEO formatting. CNCF audits demonstrate that basic failures persist. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analyses/2026/kubevirt/analysis/, 2026 report, publication day not verified)
- **Migration acceptance—proposal:** inventory high-use docs and external-link destinations, map changed URLs to their closest equivalent, preserve meaningful anchors where possible, and monitor errors plus indexed canonicals after release. Keep analytics off preview deployments. Assign a named owner for GSC and analytics so an employee departure does not remove access. These measures address concrete gaps identified by CNCF's documentation reviews; they are not a proprietary ranking technique. (https://contribute.cncf.io/techdocs/analysis/criteria/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

### 7.2 i18n and hreflang

- **Verified mechanics:** Google supports localized-version signals through HTML, HTTP headers, or XML sitemaps. Use fully qualified URLs, valid language/region codes, reciprocal references, and a reference to the page itself. `x-default` can identify a fallback. A language label is not proof that the page is translated; Google also evaluates visible content. Choose a maintainable implementation rather than duplicating all methods for their own sake. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal for versioned technical docs:** test the locale × version matrix. A Russian installation page for one supported release should not alternate to an English page for an incompatible release. Keep commands, parameter names, and compatibility facts consistent while translating explanations. Avoid automatic locale redirects that prevent users or crawlers from reaching the requested language. These are operational safeguards, not evidence that translating into a certain number of languages guarantees incremental demand. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/localized-versions, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Evidence gap:** a controlled 2025–2026 case from the named reference companies attributing an organic or paid-conversion increase specifically to `hreflang`, Hugo versus Docusaurus, or multilingual docs was **not verified**. Teach these as correctness and accessibility mechanisms; do not invent percentage uplifts to make the lesson appear quantitative. (https://gohugo.io/templates/sitemap/, updated 2026-11-08; https://docusaurus.io/docs/seo, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

## 9. Company website versus OSS project website

### 9.1 Governance and responsibilities

- **Verified CNCF guidance:** vendor neutrality means the project should not function as one company's advertising channel. CNCF's guidance discusses equal vendor representation and neutral enterprise-resource pages rather than privileging one vendor in project materials. Neutrality is an operational governance issue; merely buying a second domain does not establish it. Equally, this is not evidence that every project must use an identical URL structure. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/best-practices/community/vendor-neutrality/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—project site contract:** publish software purpose, license, governance, maintainers, releases, architecture, installation, security process, and contribution paths. Keep docs public and commercial-provider listings equitable. This is an application of CNCF guidance, not a mandatory page inventory quoted from it. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/best-practices/community/vendor-neutrality/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—company site contract:** identify the legal vendor and its specific offer: supported distributions/services, support scope, operational responsibilities, pricing or procurement route, security/compliance evidence, customer evidence, and a credible trial or contact path. Explain the relationship to the upstream project explicitly. Aenix's description of its relationship to Cozystack provides a current reference; it does not justify promoting an unconfirmed CNCF graduation level. (https://aenix.io/about/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25; https://www.cncf.io/projects/cozystack/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—avoid ambiguous duplication:** keep upstream installation truth in project docs and commercial commitments on the vendor site, with explicit contextual links. Mirrored descriptions can complicate correction and blur software capabilities versus service obligations. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/best-practices/community/vendor-neutrality/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

### 9.2 Public traffic mix: two examples, no “typical OSS” average

- **Kubernetes project site:** Similarweb's October 2026 page reports **47.8% desktop traffic from organic search**, with direct second and referrals third. This is an estimated channel distribution for that domain and period, not first-party analytics, not all devices, and not a smaller project's expected mix. Documentation use by existing operators can be substantial without representing new commercial demand. (https://www.similarweb.com/website/kubernetes.io/, October 2026 report period, publication day not verified, accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Tailscale commercial site:** the corresponding October 2026 page reports **62.36% desktop direct traffic**, followed by organic search and referrals. Domain scope and returning users make comparison with Kubernetes imperfect; “direct” also does not establish that every visitor deliberately typed the URL. These two examples demonstrate variation, not a statistically representative benchmark for infrastructure companies. (https://www.similarweb.com/website/tailscale.com/, October 2026 report period, publication day not verified, accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Failure to avoid:** estimated third-party traffic changes should not be turned into a claim that a particular blog strategy caused paid growth. For small domains especially, the reviewed public pages do not supply a project-specific confidence interval or a full acquisition-to-revenue funnel. A current, credible Cozystack-wide traffic-source and conversion benchmark was **not verified**. (https://www.similarweb.com/website/kubernetes.io/, October 2026 report period, accessed 2026-11-25; https://www.similarweb.com/website/tailscale.com/, October 2026 report period, accessed 2026-11-25)

### 9.3 Docs → evaluation → trial or contact

- **Proposal—two legitimate paths:** self-service evaluators need prerequisites and a working deployment; enterprise evaluators need architecture, support, and procurement details. A neutral project can list eligible providers while a company offers its own trial. Avoid privileging one vendor through upstream navigation. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/best-practices/community/vendor-neutrality/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Verified measurement mechanics:** GA4 cross-domain measurement can preserve identifiers across controlled domains via the `_gl` linker parameter when consent and configuration allow it. Without this, one person may become multiple users/sessions; self-referrals can distort attribution. The docs also note that configured cross-domain links are not treated as ordinary enhanced-measurement outbound clicks. Do not assume that a neutral upstream project permits a vendor's cross-domain tracking; governance and consent must support the arrangement. (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10071811?hl=en, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—minimum funnel:** record eligible docs visits, explicit evaluation clicks, completed trial/installation, first useful workload, and qualified contact separately. Define activation in product terms, such as a successful workload rather than merely an account. Use cohorts and elapsed time because infrastructure procurement can span sessions and domains. No common 2025–2026 docs-to-trial-to-sales benchmark for this reference class was verified; instrument the local funnel before setting numerical targets. (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10071811?hl=en, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

## 10. Measuring AI referrals and visibility without confusing the signals

### 10.1 GA4, server logs, and native search reports

- **Important GA4 change:** Google's release notes dated **13 May 2026** introduce the **AI Assistant** default channel, with recognized AI traffic using the `ai-assistant` medium and associated campaign labeling. Older instructions that rely exclusively on `referral` medium and a custom source regex may miss or misclassify newer data. Preserve historical definitions and validate actual source/medium values before comparing periods across the change. (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9164320?hl=en, release entry 2026-05-13)
- **Proposal—GA4 verification:** inspect traffic acquisition, landing page, hostname, and key events for recognized assistant sources; test links from the actual products used by buyers. Keep a versioned source map for historical/custom reports, but do not force every Google referral into an “AI” bucket. AIO/AI Mode visits cannot be reliably isolated merely by guessing a referrer-domain regex. Native channel recognition is useful, but it does not recover copied links, blocked analytics, or all cross-device journeys. (https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9164320?hl=en, release entry 2026-05-13; https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/9756891?hl=en, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—log classification:** retain timestamp, hostname/path, status, response type, referrer when present, and bot identity evidence. Separate human referred navigation, user-triggered retrieval, search crawling, training crawling, and generic scanners. Verify major bot identities using provider information rather than trusting a string. A request for Markdown is evidence of access; it is not a consented human session or a trial start. Aggregate and minimize personal data in teaching datasets. (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/bots, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25; https://www.gitbook.com/blog/bot-traffic-vs-agent-traffic, 2026-08-27)
- **Google's native report:** the current generative-AI performance documentation describes **impressions** for AI Overviews and AI Mode, with page/country/device/date views and canonical-URL attribution. Availability requires sufficient data; Labs experiences are excluded. It is not an unrestricted prompt log or a complete standalone AI click/conversion report. Do not divide ordinary site clicks by this report's impressions and call the result a validated AIO CTR. (https://support.google.com/webmasters/answer/16984139, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Bing's native report:** AI Performance launched in public preview on **10 February 2026**, exposing cited pages, citation activity, and sampled grounding queries across supported Microsoft AI surfaces and partners. Subsequent updates added intent/topic and citation-share comparisons. Grounding queries are search/retrieval signals, not a census of user prompts; Microsoft coverage must not be relabeled “all ChatGPT visibility.” (https://blogs.bing.com/webmaster/2026/2/Introducing-AI-Performance-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Public-Preview, 2026-02-10; https://blogs.bing.com/search/2026/6/New-AI-Visibility-Insights-in-Bing-Webmaster-Tools-Intents-Topics-Citation-Share-Compare, 2026-06-16)
- **Attribution limit:** dashboards cannot observe every later branded search or direct visit influenced by an answer. A short voluntary “how did you first hear about us?” field can complement analytics, but recall is not precise attribution. Report observed referrals, self-reported influence, and experimental estimates separately. Cloudflare's crawl/referral work illustrates why even abundant machine access does not translate directly into measurable visits. (https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-search-crawl-refer-ratio-on-radar/, 2025-07-01)

### 10.2 Brand-monitoring tools and their reliability

- **What tools actually sample:** vendor panels observe chosen prompts, model surfaces, locations, and times—not all buyer conversations. Distinguish mentions, recommendations, and citation share. Require raw responses and URLs before interpreting an “AI share of voice” score. (https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns, 2025-06-05, updated 2025-08; https://www.semrush.com/blog/most-cited-domains-ai/, 2025-11-10)
- **Reliability study:** SparkToro's experiment used **600 volunteers, 12 prompts, and 2,961 runs** across ChatGPT, Claude, and Google experiences in November–December 2025. Repeating a brand/product recommendation rarely reproduced the same list—less than one in 100 comparisons—and the same ordering was rarer still. User settings, history, and location were not fully controlled. The authors distinguish unreliable individual rankings from more defensible aggregate mention frequency across repeated runs; the study does not show that every monitoring method is useless. (https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/, 2026-01-27)
- **Proposed panel:** repeat fixed discovery, comparison, deployment, security, licensing, and governance prompts in buyers’ languages. Separate exploratory prompts from the trend panel. Record model/surface, date, browsing, locale, personalization, answers, citations, and errors. Treat UI and API observations separately. This is a proposed design, not a proven optimal panel size. (https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/, 2026-01-27)
- **Proposal—tool acceptance test:** inspect exported answers, entity disambiguation, and citation URLs; repeat prompts before committing. Check whether the tool confuses company with project or citation with recommendation. Independent accuracy benchmarks covering every vendor were **not verified**. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/self-promotional-content-ai-seo-experiment/, 2026-07-06)

## 11. LLM positioning: correct descriptions, evidence, and interventions

### 11.1 Build a factual record that can be checked

- **Proposal—canonical fact sheet:** maintain category, license, governance, deployment model, responsibilities, and commercial relationship. Align README, overview, architecture docs, and company explanation with supporting evidence. Do not repeat unconfirmed Incubating status. Consistency is a maintenance objective, not a proven ranking factor. (https://www.cncf.io/projects/cozystack/, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25; https://cozystack.io/blog/2026/09/cozystack-cncf-general-technical-review/, 2026-09-29)
- **Proposal—specific comparisons:** explicitly distinguish a cloud-building platform, Kubernetes distribution, cluster-management product, managed service, and application platform where these distinctions matter. Explain which layer the project supplies and which dependencies remain. Correct third-party descriptions through normal editorial channels with evidence; do not manufacture independent endorsements. The association between web mentions and AI visibility is a reason to inspect third-party understanding, not proof that synchronized wording controls a model. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/, 2025-05-26)

### 11.2 Evidence that answers can move—and limits to that evidence

- **Controlled enough to be informative, not definitive:** Ahrefs tested a fictional brand, Xarumei, with **56 leading false-premise questions across eight AI products**. In a later phase it published an authoritative FAQ and also contradictory fictional third-party material. GPT-4/5 cited the FAQ in about **84%** of tested answers and were manipulated in fewer than 7%, while other products behaved differently. Simultaneous interventions, one invented brand, and adversarial prompts limit generalization. The useful lesson is that retrieval can incorporate corrections; it is not a recommendation to seed false material. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-vs-made-up-brand-experiment/, 2025-12-10)
- **What remains unproven:** an observed improvement after updating a README, comparison page, and external profile does not identify which change caused it. Models, retrieval indexes, and competitors change concurrently. A reproducible 2025–2026 case proving that a named infrastructure vendor changed its category description across all major models through one isolated intervention was **not verified**. The Ahrefs fictional-brand study demonstrates uneven responsiveness, not universal control. (https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-vs-made-up-brand-experiment/, 2025-12-10)
- **Verified correction routes:** Google's guidance recommends fixing ambiguity or inaccurate material on the source page and using product feedback for incorrect attribution or generated facts. A substantive source correction and recrawl can help retrieval, but neither instantly updates model parameters nor guarantees that a particular answer changes. Keep the original erroneous response, affected URL, correction date, and later reruns as an audit trail. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/gen-ai-attribution-factuality, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Proposal—evaluate correctness separately:** score category, licensing, project/company boundary, capabilities, and governance. Separately track mentions, recommendations, citation accuracy, and referrals. Repeat stable prompts and retain unchanged comparison topics. Accurate competitor recommendations and fabricated product capabilities are different outcomes. (https://sparktoro.com/blog/new-research-ais-are-highly-inconsistent-when-recommending-brands-or-products-marketers-should-take-care-when-tracking-ai-visibility/, 2026-01-27)

## 12. Wikipedia and Wikidata: eligibility and editorial independence

### 12.1 Wikipedia presence is earned coverage, not a signup flow

- **Current rule:** the organizations/companies guideline requires significant coverage in multiple reliable, independent secondary sources. A press release, company's own blog, investor announcement, GitHub stars, or affiliation with a notable organization does not automatically establish notability. For a software project, consult the applicable topic guidance as well; the course should not promise an article merely because a project joins CNCF. Living guideline, original publication date not verified. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Notability_(organizations_and_companies), n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Current conflict-of-interest rule:** employees, founders, and paid representatives have a conflict when writing about their own organization. Wikipedia strongly discourages direct COI editing and requires applicable paid-contribution disclosures. Use transparent talk-page edit requests and appropriate draft-review routes, supplying independent sources rather than promotional copy. A PR agency's promise of guaranteed placement is not an exception to community rules. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Failure boundary:** promotional drafts may be rejected or removed, and policy violations can lead to editing restrictions; however, a fresh, documented Wikipedia ban of one of the named infrastructure companies for a GEO campaign was **not verified**. Do not present this potential consequence as a named incident. Wikipedia also does not offer a vendor-controlled correction SLA. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Conflict_of_interest, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)

### 12.2 Wikidata is a different eligibility system

- **Current rule:** a Wikidata item can qualify through an eligible sitelink, a clearly identifiable entity described in serious public sources, or a structural need under its policy. This differs from English Wikipedia article notability. Creating a duplicate or unsupported promotional item is not a substitute for independent evidence; references and the distinction between company and software project matter. Living policy, original publication date not verified. (https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Notability, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Evidence limit:** Wikipedia citation frequency does not establish a causal benefit from creating an article or Wikidata item. An isolated infrastructure-vendor uplift was **not verified**. Improve eligible public records for accuracy, not as a model-positioning switch. (https://www.tryprofound.com/blog/ai-platform-citation-patterns, 2025-06-05, updated 2025-08; https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Notability, n.d., accessed 2026-11-25)
