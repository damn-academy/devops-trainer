# Events and paid promotion for an open-source infrastructure vendor, 2025–2026

Research cutoff: **25 November 2026**. Prepared as source material for a public educational course. The intended audience is a vendor selling infrastructure, support, or a managed service around an open-source project. Product-engineer and general B2B examples are explicitly identified when they are not evidence about Kubernetes infrastructure buyers.

## How to read this evidence

**Verified** means that the cited primary source actually states the fact, not that its commercial claims were independently audited. **Reported result** means a company, founder, or platform reports its own outcome. **Practitioner view** is advice or an interpretation. **Course synthesis** is a proposed application, not an experimentally established rule. **Not verified** marks a missing result, price, date, or causal claim. Absence of a published result does not establish that a channel fails.

Dates in citations are publication dates where available. Otherwise they identify the report edition, document version, or explicitly undated living documentation. An access date is not a publication date. **[OLD]** marks material published before 2024. Historical 2024 observations are retained only as labeled comparisons. Prices are advertised inventory prices, not independently verified invoices; taxes, membership, production, travel, and labor may be additional.

### Findings that change a purchase or teaching decision

- **Verified change — KubeWeekly is no longer a current standalone newsletter buy.** Its final edition announced a merger into CNCF Monthly Newsletter on 30 May 2025. A sponsorship plan copied from an older channel list is therefore obsolete; current paid inventory and rates for the replacement were **not verified**. (https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-434-this-isnt-goodbye-its-just-the-beginning-of-something-even-better/, 2025-05-30)
- **Reported result — booths can generate substantial technical engagement, but that does not establish payback.** Dash0 founder Mirko Novakovic describes spending $250,000 on its first three-day KubeCon, delivering 1,800 demos and generating hundreds of follow-up meetings. Closed revenue, opportunity conversion, and the cost boundary were not disclosed. This is a useful infrastructure example, not a universal booth ROI benchmark. (https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7434598506889207808/, 2026; exact publication date not verified)
- **Reported budget — paid distribution is not inherently incompatible with developer marketing.** PostHog disclosed approximately $488,000/month for paid ads, $228,000 for newsletter and influencer sponsorships, and $65,000 for events in its 2026 budget. It also described 215 employees and $194 million raised: these are scale-stage expenditure choices, not a starter budget or proof that every channel pays back. (https://posthog.com/founders/actual-marketing-budget-2026, 2026-06-24)
- **Verified constraint — buying conference sponsorship does not buy arbitrary editorial access.** KubeCon's CFP reviewer rubric penalizes product pitches and generic proposals. The published process advances roughly the top 30% to a further review stage; that percentage is not the final acceptance rate. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/submission-reviewer-guidelines/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)

## 1. Webinars: attendance, replay, engineering formats, and partners

### Benchmarks: keep the cohorts and denominators separate

- **Verified benchmark — ON24's 2025 report describes 2024 behavior:** 57% registration-to-attendance conversion, 216 average attendees, and 51 minutes of engagement. Its body reports 56% live and 45% on-demand attendance; the overview instead says 50% on-demand. Preserve that inconsistency rather than silently choosing a number. The accessible summary does not provide an exact webinar/company sample or an infrastructure-only segment. These are ON24 customers, not all webinar programs. (https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2025-webinar-benchmarks-report/, 2025-02-20)
- **Verified benchmark — ON24's 2026 report describes 2025 behavior:** its detailed figures are 60% registration-to-attendance conversion, 239 average attendees, 49 minutes of engagement, 67% live and 43% on-demand participation. Live and replay audiences can overlap. Its overview retains different duration/on-demand figures, so use the detailed section with this caveat; an exact accessible sample size and engineering segment were **not verified**. (https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-webinar-benchmarks-report/, 2026-06-19)
- **Verified benchmark — Goldcast 2025:** 19,531 webinars from 418 B2B brands during calendar 2024; reported attendance rate 33%, average registrations 238, average attendees 51. Dividing those two averages gives approximately 21%, not 33%. Different aggregation or inclusion rules can produce different results; the accessible figures do not resolve the discrepancy. Do not reconstruct an individual-event attendance rate from pooled averages. (https://www.goldcast.io/reports/b2b-webinar-benchmark-report-2025, 2025 edition; exact publication date not stated)
- **Verified benchmark — Goldcast 2026:** 26,190 webinars from 522 B2B organizations in calendar 2025; headline attendance approximately 40%, detailed rate 40.1%, average registrations 251 and attendees 102. Its report distinguishes **89.1% of webinars made available on demand** from **15.9% of viewership being on demand**. Those are different denominators. The vendor's customer mix, event definitions, and reporting window prevent treating this as a contradiction of ON24's replay share. (https://www.goldcast.io/reports/b2b-webinar-benchmark-report-2026, 2026 edition; exact publication date not stated)
- **Verified benchmark — BigMarker 2025:** the report says it analyzed more than two million B2B marketing, thought-leadership, and customer-education webinars in 2024. It reports 322 average registrants and 171 attendees; its selected high-performing cohort has 375 and 214. The calculated ratios of means are 53.1% and 57.1%, respectively, **not verified event-level conversion rates**. The high-performing cohort is selected, not randomized, and the accessible report does not isolate infrastructure buyers. (https://5419875.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/5419875/Gated%20Content%20Downloads/BigMarker-2025-B2B-Webinar-Benchmarks_043025.pdf, 2025 edition; filename version 2025-04-30; publication day not verified)
- **Course synthesis — planning range, not a pooled industry average.** Model separate scenarios around the reported 33–60% registration-to-attendance figures, then replace them with your own event history. Do not label that range “live show-up”: replay inclusion and aggregation differ. Report live unique attendees, replay-only unique attendees, both, and total deduplicated attendees independently. (https://www.goldcast.io/reports/b2b-webinar-benchmark-report-2025, 2025 edition; exact publication date not stated; https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-webinar-benchmarks-report/, 2026-06-19)

### What has concrete evidence, and what is only a format hypothesis

- **Verified infrastructure example — a CNCF-hosted technical webinar is an available distribution format.** KubeWeekly listed “GPU-powered AI on VMs, Kubernetes & Bare Metal with Cozystack,” presented by Andrei Kvapil of Ænix on 22 May 2025. That verifies a relevant vendor/project educational collaboration, not its attendance, lead quality, or sales outcome: those results were **not verified**. (https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-434-this-isnt-goodbye-its-just-the-beginning-of-something-even-better/, 2025-05-30)
- **Verified mechanics — foundation support is conditional.** CNCF's project marketing services include webinar and release-promotion support for eligible projects. A project's standing and the specific program rules matter; a commercial vendor cannot infer unrestricted promotional rights from an association with an open-source project. Confirm eligibility and editorial expectations before promising a CNCF co-host to a partner. (https://contribute.cncf.io/resources/services/marketing/, publication date not stated; living documentation accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Reported pattern — recurring programs are common, not proven causal winners.** Goldcast reports that 31.2% of its 2025 webinars belonged to a series. Its observed attendance for titles containing “webinar” was 43%, against 40.1% overall. Neither comparison controls for brand, audience, or topic; it does not prove that renaming a session or creating a series raises attendance. (https://www.goldcast.io/reports/b2b-webinar-benchmark-report-2026, 2026 edition; exact publication date not stated)
- **Course synthesis — an engineer-oriented session to test:** reproduce one failure or build one working system, show the commands and architecture, explain the tradeoff, and take troubleshooting questions. A Cozystack-class example is “Provision a tenant cluster and VM through one Kubernetes API; inspect what breaks during recovery.” Publish the repository and replay. This is a proposed format grounded in a real technical webinar, not a verified format-specific conversion uplift. (https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-434-this-isnt-goodbye-its-just-the-beginning-of-something-even-better/, 2025-05-30)
- **Verified measurement mechanics — exports support more than registration counts.** BigMarker documents attendance/replay reports and interaction records including questions, polls and calls to action. Use those to distinguish “registered,” “watched,” and “asked for evaluation help”; a poll response is not an opportunity. For a partner webinar, agree before launch which party receives which records and what follow-up was offered. (https://kb.bigmarker.com/knowledge/downloading-your-reports, publication date not stated; documentation accessed 2026-11-25)

### Failures, limits, and what has stopped being a safe assumption

- **Practitioner counterexample — not every successful developer company chooses webinars.** PostHog's current event handbook explicitly favors small in-person building and relationship formats and says it avoids webinars and conventional booth/scan programs. This is a company's audience and operating-model choice, not experimental evidence that webinars fail for cloud-platform buyers. No fresh controlled engineer-specific “webinars are dead” finding was verified. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/events, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — live-only reporting misses part of the result.** ON24 documents meaningful replay viewing and rising engagement with interactive webinar elements. Record a consistent replay window, such as 30 days, and separate replay viewers from live attendance rather than classifying every live absence as a failed registration. Thirty days is a proposed reporting convention, not an ON24 benchmark. (https://www.on24.com/blog/key-takeaways-from-the-2025-webinar-benchmarks-report/, 2025-02-20)

## 2. Online workshops and hands-on labs

### Cases, mechanics, and completion rates

- **Reported infrastructure case — Syntasso and The Access Group at KubeCon EU 2025.** Instruqt describes a Kratix hackathon serving more than 200 attendees with browser-based environments. This establishes that a practical platform-engineering workshop can operate at conference scale without every attendee configuring a laptop. The case does not publish participant-to-opportunity conversion or an independently audited completion rate. (https://instruqt.com/blog/instruqt-in-action-how-syntasso-and-the-access-group-delivered-a-scalable-hackathon-at-kubecon, 2025-06-16)
- **Reported operational result — the same event's year-end account:** more than 200 environments, about 25 minutes of setup saved per attendee, and more than 120 aggregate hours of hands-on activity. These are vendor-reported delivery measures. Aggregate activity hours are not the percentage of participants who completed the exercise, learned the material, or adopted Kratix. (https://instruqt.com/blog/instruqt-year-in-review-how-customers-powered-hands-on-learning-at-scale, 2025-12-22)
- **Undated supporting case — SUSE RancherDays.** Instruqt's SUSE story describes expanding workshop capacity and tracking lab engagement, with a headline claim of tripled capacity. Publication date and recent measurement period are **not verified**; do not present it as a fresh 2025–2026 outcome. Its useful lesson is operational: make the environment ready before the teaching starts and connect lab activity to the registration record. (https://instruqt.com/customer-stories/suse, publication date and measurement period not verified; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Verified mechanics — Killercoda-style labs.** Creators define scenarios in GitHub using configuration, Markdown instructions, and scripts; verification scripts can test whether a step succeeded. This permits objective completion checks instead of counting a clicked “next” button as learning. Ephemeral environments require an intentional reset/retry path and appropriate treatment of credentials. Infrastructure-vendor completion and sales-conversion benchmarks were **not verified** in the creator documentation. (https://killercoda.com/creators, publication date not stated; living documentation accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — instrument the actual exercise.** For a Kubernetes cloud lab, record unique starts, successful tenant creation, successful workload deployment, recovery-test success, and return visits. Define completion as a checked technical outcome; report completions divided by starts and by registrations separately. Track environment failures separately from learning abandonment. This applies the platform's verification capability; no universal “good completion rate” is claimed. (https://killercoda.com/creators, publication date not stated; living documentation accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — follow-up should follow what happened.** Send finishers the production deployment path, stalled participants the relevant troubleshooting step, and absentees a fresh lab link. Instruqt exposes progress/completion tracking, but a claimed uplift from this exact segmentation is **not verified**. Evaluate the result using repeat lab use, qualified evaluation requests, and activated organizations, not only certificate downloads. (https://instruqt.com/solutions/workshops, publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Failure modes and budget uncertainty

- **Evidence limit — do not substitute setup time for acquisition ROI.** The Syntasso/Access Group case supports reducing setup friction; it does not establish lab completion, retention, conversion, or a public per-attendee platform price. Budget engineering preparation, environment runtime, assistants, and follow-up separately, then obtain a current platform quote. No fresh named failed lab campaign with a disclosed financial loss was verified. (https://instruqt.com/blog/instruqt-in-action-how-syntasso-and-the-access-group-delivered-a-scalable-hackathon-at-kubecon, 2025-06-16)

## 3. Offline workshops and city meetups

### A documented operating model

- **Reported practice — PostHog ran 45 in-person events in 2025.** Its handbook describes small recurring coworking/building gatherings and community support of approximately $500–$3,500. Suggested venues include technology-company or VC offices, coworking spaces and libraries with quiet space and reliable Wi-Fi. These are one company's operating choices and support amounts, not total-event cost benchmarks or a measured pipeline result. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/events, publication date not stated; living handbook reporting 2025 activity; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — a concrete city workshop design.** Pair a local platform team with an infrastructure practitioner, use a host's meeting space, and teach one reproducible deployment/recovery lab. Confirm seating, power, network access, accessibility, catering, and a person responsible for environment resets. Ask the local partner to supply a real operational problem. Treat venue support as an explicit partner contribution, not as a universal zero-cost venue assumption. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/events, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)

### Attendance drop-off: conflicting figures are useful if their scope is preserved

- **Verified platform analysis — PheedLoop's September 2026 study:** 216,282 registrations across 467 events. Pooled no-show rates were 42.4% for same-week registration and 18.4% for registration at least six months ahead. Much of that apparent timing relationship disappeared within events; free versus paid event composition mattered. Its free-event cuts span roughly 40–70% no-shows. This is not a Kubernetes meetup sample or a causal estimate of charging admission. (https://api.pheedloop.com/blog/event-data-lab-report-09-late-registrants-arent-flakier-their-events-are-different, 2026-09-10)
- **Different platform summary — PheedLoop's August recap:** median event no-show rate 20%, mean 28%; free events 28.0%, paid events 17.5%. The recap does not expose an exact sample for each comparison. Preserve these numbers alongside the September analysis rather than averaging them: the reports use different slices and aggregation, and “free event” is not synonymous with “local engineering meetup.” (https://pheedloop.com/blog/horizons-i-recap-horizons-ii-announcement, 2026-08-20)
- **Relevant community observation — KCD Hangzhou/OID China 2025.** Its transparency report describes onsite attendance exceeding 50% of registrations. This is a particular local event, not a global free-meetup benchmark; it demonstrates why registration count alone is a poor catering or impact measure. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/KCD-Hangzhou-OID-China-2025-Transparency-Report.pdf, 2026-01 upload; exact publication date not verified)
- **Verified mechanics — count attendance deliberately.** Meetup provides attendance and no-show management; its newer attendee-list workflow also supports QR check-in. A registered attendee list is not evidence of physical attendance. Use observed check-ins, cancellations and walk-ins to build a local baseline before making capacity or overbooking decisions. (https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/9389668230541-Manage-attendees-and-track-attendance-for-your-Meetup-event-on-the-web, publication date not stated; documentation accessed 2026-11-25; https://help.meetup.com/hc/en-us/articles/45352992737677-Introducing-the-new-attendee-list-and-QR-code-check-in, publication date not stated; documentation accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — follow-up and failure test.** Send the lab/repository and answers to the questions actually raised, offer a second working session, and record repeat attendance and completed deployments. A room filled by free food without subsequent technical engagement has achieved hospitality, not demonstrated product adoption. The relevant public case supports recurring community events, but a numerical meetup-to-opportunity rate was **not verified**. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/events, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)

### What reviewers accept or reject

- **Verified 2025 review mechanics.** The archived NA reviewer guide requires independent review, handling conflicts of interest, and confidentiality; product pitches are a poor fit. A vendor employee can submit valuable technical material, but sponsorship and employment do not substitute for educational merit. The 2024 EU selection analysis confirms that vendor speakers were represented among accepted talks. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/archive/2025/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/submission-reviewer-guidelines/, 2025 event documentation; publication date not stated; https://www.cncf.io/blog/2024/01/30/inside-the-numbers-the-kubecon-cloudnativecon-selection-process-for-europe-2024/, 2024-01-30)
- **Verified 2026 rejection signals.** The NA guide's low-score criteria include generic content, product pitches, copied or low-quality AI-generated submissions, and weak educational value. Reviewers need a clear technical scope and audience benefit. This is evidence of editorial selection rules, not proof that all rejected submissions share one cause. A named fresh vendor ban for submitting a pitch was **not verified**. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/submission-reviewer-guidelines/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Verified submission constraint.** NA 2026 limits a person to three proposals, including appearances as a co-speaker; exceeding the limit can result in removal of submissions. Match the actual track, audience level, format, and technical learning objectives. Reusing a generic abstract across many tracks is not a substitute for addressing those fields. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/cfp/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — an abstract template, not an acceptance formula.** State the operator's problem, the system and constraints, the intervention, measured evidence or failure, and what attendees can reproduce. Example: “We used Kubernetes API aggregation to expose VM and tenant-cluster provisioning. This talk traces reconciliation failures, recovery behavior, and isolation tradeoffs, then provides a reproducible test.” Insert only measurements actually obtained; do not invent deployment scale for credibility. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/cfp/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)

### Maintainer track, co-located events, practice, and recordings

- **Verified separate pathways.** KubeCon project opportunities include maintainer sessions, ContribFest, kiosks, and lightning formats. The 2026 Maintainer Summit has its own eligibility and process. Eligibility belongs to the participating project or maintainer role, not automatically to every commercial company that packages the project. These routes should be planned separately from a general CFP and a paid sponsor package. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/features-add-ons/project-opportunities/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; https://sessionize.com/maintainer-summit-na-2026/, 2026 event listing; publication date not stated)
- **Verified separate CFP — CNCF-hosted co-located events.** The NA 2026 listing has a distinct proposal process for specialist events such as ArgoCon and CiliumCon. A narrower operational topic may fit a specialist audience, but neither acceptance nor sales performance follows automatically from smaller event size. Do not merge their submission totals with KubeCon's main CFP. (https://sessionize.com/cncf-hosted-co-located-events-north-america-2026/, 2026 event listing; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Practitioner guidance — preparation with technical substance.** PostHog's speaker guide asks speakers to learn the room and format, center a real scenario, and explain what broke or was learned. Its demo preparation includes realistic demo data, readable code/screens, testing the projector, and a backup for failed connectivity. A live production customer account is not a suitable demo environment. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/speaker-guide, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — rehearse and distribute.** Time a complete run with a colleague playing the intended operator, including Q&A and a failed-demo recovery; remove details that obscure the technical result. Afterward link the official recording, slides, commands, and corrections together, then measure qualified inbound questions and lab reuse. This is a proposed workflow; a measured rehearsal-to-acceptance or recording-to-revenue uplift was **not verified**. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/speaker-guide, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)

### Demonstrations, staffing, lead scanning, and swag

- **Reported case — Dash0's approach was labor-intensive.** Its founder describes a large technical team, many demo stations, conspicuous red jumpsuits, and backpack giveaways alongside 1,800 demos. Dividing the reported $250,000 by 1,800 gives approximately **$139 per reported demo**, a calculated activity cost. It is not CAC, cost per qualified opportunity, or evidence that giveaways caused demand. No comparable result for a two-person startup booth was disclosed. (https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7434598506889207808/, 2026; exact publication date not verified)
- **Verified aggregate — KubeCon EU 2025 recorded 97,185 scans and an average 461 per booth.** These are organizer-reported lead-retrieval counts, not necessarily unique people, qualified accounts, meetings, or opportunities. A cross-exhibitor lead-to-opportunity rate was **not verified**. Buying a tier on the assumption that each scan has equal commercial value is unsupported. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/KubeCon_EU_25_TransparencyReport.pdf, 2025-06 publication; exact day not verified)
- **Verified aggregate — KubeCon EU 2026 recorded 110,213 leads and 557 average leads per booth.** The report's approximately 20% growth corresponds to the per-booth comparison with 461, whereas total lead growth from 97,185 is approximately 13.4% by calculation. Preserve which denominator is growing; neither figure establishes improvement in opportunity quality. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/KCEU26-Transparency-Report.pdf, 2026-04-28 report release)
- **Course synthesis — minimum functional staffing roles.** Cover technical discovery/demo, greeting/queue triage, and notes/next-step scheduling; rotate breaks and account for people giving talks. One person may combine roles at quiet times. Rehearse a short demo, keep a resettable environment and offline fallback, and show readable terminal output. This is an operating design, not a published “optimal staff per square meter” benchmark. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/speaker-guide, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)

## 8. Event ROI: a measurement model that does not confuse activity with revenue

### Use a funnel whose stages can be audited

- **Course synthesis — maintain distinct event stages:** invitation/registration → checked-in attendee or qualified booth conversation → agreed next step → meeting held → sales-accepted opportunity → closed-won account. For a workshop add verified technical completion and subsequent activation. Keep event contacts and distinct accounts separate; several employees of one company do not equal several independent opportunities. This addresses the limitations of published scan totals. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/KCEU26-Transparency-Report.pdf, 2026-04-28 report release)
- **Course synthesis — report two different financial quantities.** “Pipeline per dollar” is unweighted opportunity value divided by all-in event cost; it is not profit. Event ROI should use attributable gross profit minus cost, divided by cost, with the attribution model and period disclosed. These are proposed accounting definitions, not claims about any vendor's achieved ROI. Published cases that report pipeline do not establish either closed revenue or incrementality. (https://advertise.tldr.tech/case-studies/plaid-adds-382k-in-pipeline-with-tldr-campaign-their-best-direct-buy-yet/, 2025-09-16)
- **Course synthesis — a falsifiable break-even worksheet.** Let all-in event cost be C and expected first-year gross profit per won account be G. Break-even wins = C/G; required opportunities = C/(G × your historical opportunity win rate). Use your own observed rate, not a scan-to-opportunity number borrowed from another market. Disclose whether expansion and renewals are included. This is a planning calculation; the conference prospectus supplies only part of C. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/sponsor-cncf-2026_120425.pdf, version 2025-12-04; exact publication day not verified)
- **Verified reason to avoid a one-month revenue verdict.** Dreamdata's B2B customer data reports average intervals of 281 days from first LinkedIn ad impression to revenue, 214 from conversion and 212 from engagement. These are advertising cohorts, not event sales-cycle benchmarks. They demonstrate how an early-touch acquisition report can be immature; use consistent 30/90/180-day event cohorts as a proposed tracking schedule, not a promised close window. (https://dreamdata.io/blog/announcing-linkedin-ads-benchmarks-report-2026, 2026-03-10)
- **Course synthesis — measure incremental value separately from attributed value.** Compare comparable invited and uninvited accounts where feasible, mark existing pipeline, deduplicate people/accounts across events, and record the source of the first useful conversation. “Influenced” opportunity value may include deals already progressing before the event. No fresh randomized KubeCon booth ROI study was **verified** in the reviewed reports. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/KubeCon_EU_25_TransparencyReport.pdf, 2025-06 publication; exact day not verified)

## 18. G2, TrustRadius, and Gartner Peer Insights

### Review collection, paid vendor services, and buying favorable reviews are different things

- **Verified G2 rules.** Its community guidelines restrict biased solicitation and incentives tied to favorable sentiment; relevant incentives must be disclosed. Buying vendor software or advertising does not turn employee, fabricated or selectively rewarded positive reviews into acceptable evidence. A fresh named OSS infrastructure paid-G2 campaign with public spend and closed revenue was **not verified**. - **Verified TrustRadius rules.** It allows neutral incentives, discloses them, and rejects incentives conditional on positive wording or ratings. Its verification program calls for representative solicitation rather than selecting only promoters. Basic listing and response capabilities are free; that is not a price quote for paid intent or marketing products. Fresh infrastructure-vendor ROI and current paid-package prices were **not verified**. (https://www.trustradius.com/static/content-integrity, publication date not stated; policy accessed 2026-11-25)

## 19. Retargeting: current browser reality and attribution traps

- **Verified policy direction — Chrome did not universally remove third-party cookies in 2025.** Google's 22 April 2025 announcement retained its existing user-choice approach and said it would not introduce the previously proposed standalone prompt. Incognito continued blocking third-party cookies. A course asserting that all browser retargeting disappeared in 2025 is inaccurate. This does not imply universal tracking availability across browsers or users. (https://privacysandbox.google.com/blog/privacy-sandbox-next-steps, 2025-04-22)
- **Reported benchmark — Metadata.** Its 2026 report covering 2025 campaigns reports retargeting at $196 CPL and 9.6 times sourced pipeline per dollar, versus cold prospecting CPL $187. Its overall dataset is 153 B2B advertisers and $57.6 million; the summary does not establish randomized incrementality or infrastructure-specific results. People already interested in the product are a selected audience, so attribution is not proof that the ad caused the opportunity. (https://metadata.io/benchmark-report-2026, 2026 report; exact publication date not stated)
- **Practitioner alternative — PostHog's measurement architecture.** Its current paid handbook describes using click identifiers and first-party/CDP-based conversion plumbing rather than indiscriminately installing third-party ad pixels. That is one company's implementation choice, not an exemption from consent or platform rules. A fresh isolated retargeting CAC for its infrastructure-like audience was **not verified**. (https://posthog.com/handbook/marketing/paid, publication date not stated; living handbook accessed 2026-11-25)
- **Course synthesis — test assistance to evaluation.** Separate documentation visitors, lab starters and commercial evaluators; exclude irrelevant completed actions and keep frequency and audience age visible. Use a holdout where feasible and disclose view-through attribution. Small infrastructure audiences can produce unstable estimates; do not transplant a general B2B pipeline multiple into a small cloud-platform forecast. (https://metadata.io/benchmark-report-2026, 2026 report; exact publication date not stated)

## 22. What does not work for engineers: precise claims instead of folklore

- **“Gated content never works” is not supported.** Plaid's 2025 report campaign generated reported pipeline, while Metadata's 2025 data gives lower CPL for native lead forms and Document Ads. Neither proves that gating installation instructions helps developers. Distinguish an optional research/evaluation asset from documentation required to assess or run an open-source product; a fresh controlled engineer-specific gating experiment was **not verified**. (https://advertise.tldr.tech/case-studies/plaid-adds-382k-in-pipeline-with-tldr-campaign-their-best-direct-buy-yet/, 2025-09-16; https://metadata.io/benchmark-report-2026, 2026 report; exact publication date not stated)
- **“Cold ads to developers never work” is also too broad.** PostHog discloses substantial current paid investment, but spend is not ROI. Its current strategy and earlier failures support evaluating audience, destination and activated organizations independently. A fresh randomized estimate of engineers' general aversion to advertising, applicable to all infrastructure products, was **not verified**. (https://posthog.com/founders/actual-marketing-budget-2026, 2026-06-24)
- **What is demonstrably rejected — a product pitch in a technical CFP.** KubeCon's reviewer criteria give this a poor score. That is a documented mismatch between content and platform rules, rather than a vague belief that engineers dislike marketing. Use an operational result, reproducible method, or honest failure analysis for the speaking proposal; keep commercial discovery in the channels that permit it. (https://events.linuxfoundation.org/kubecon-cloudnativecon-north-america/program/submission-reviewer-guidelines/, 2026 event documentation; publication date not stated; accessed 2026-11-25)
- **What no longer works as an unchanged playbook:** buying “KubeWeekly” as a current standalone newsletter, expecting The New Stack's former free byline workflow, and launching the retired Video Action Campaign type. These are documented distribution/product changes, not speculative claims about developer psychology. (https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-434-this-isnt-goodbye-its-just-the-beginning-of-something-even-better/, 2025-05-30; https://thenewstack.io/contributions/, updated 2026-07-17; https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10146226?hl=en, publication date not stated; documentation describing May 2026 migration completion, accessed 2026-11-25)
