# Local events and company/community podcasts for open-source infrastructure

Research cutoff: **25 November 2026**. Prepared as source material for a free public B2B marketing course. Reference audience: maintainers, platform engineers, SREs, infrastructure buyers, and DevRel teams around projects such as Cozystack.

## Reading this evidence correctly

This document distinguishes **verified platform rules**, **first-party reported results**, **practitioner opinion**, **proposed operating procedures**, and **not verified**. A reported result means the source actually states it; it does not mean an independent audit or causal experiment exists. Proposed procedures are course exercises, not historical claims about a company's workflow.

Dates in citations are publication dates where available. **Undated; checked 2026-11-25** means the page was inspected but its publication date was not verified. Event dates, data periods, and update dates are explicitly distinguished. A copyright year, search-engine crawl date, or year in a filename is not treated as a publication date. **[OLD]** identifies material published before 2024; 2024 material is retained only where it explains a rule change, baseline, or continuing limitation. No full-year 2026 totals are inferred from an incomplete year.

## Topic A — Small local events as a repeatable infrastructure-marketing activity

### A1. What the evidence supports

- **Verified scale, not sales attribution:** CNCF reports **35 KCDs in 27 countries and 10,000+ attendees/check-ins in 2024**. This is an aggregate across events, not 10,000 unique prospective buyers, and not a benchmark for an ordinary evening meetup. The report was published in 2025, although the underlying activity is from 2024. ([2024 report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/cncf-annual-report-2024/), published 2025-05-19; [report PDF](https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/CNCF-Annual-Report-2024_v2.pdf), 2025 edition reporting 2024)
- **Verified 2025 outcome:** CNCF's 2025 annual report records **29 KCDs, 22 countries, and 10,500+ attendees**, reporting a 17% decrease in event count and a 3.5% attendance increase. Preserve the reported growth rate: calculating from rounded “10,000+” and “10,500+” headlines cannot reproduce it reliably. The report landing page is dated February 24; the inspected PDF is a later March-path version. ([annual report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/cncf-annual-report-2025/), published 2026-02-24; [inspected PDF](https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/cncf_ar25_033126a.pdf), March 2026 file version, exact revision date not verified)
- **Conflicting counts are a planning-versus-outcome issue:** the October 2024 announcement described **61 applications competing for 30 places** for 2025; the later annual report counted 29 events. Teach “30 planned/available” and “29 reported held,” not a silently corrected single number. A public explanation for the one-event difference was not verified. ([2025 calendar announcement](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2024/10/30/mark-your-calendars-here-come-the-2025-kubernetes-community-days/), 2024-10-30; [2025 annual report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/cncf-annual-report-2025/), 2026-02-24)
- **Measured company activity: Diagrid/Dapr:** Sam Kendziora reports co-hosting Women Who Go and a Meetup for Meetup Organizers at GopherCon 2025, **each drawing 50+ attendees**. The company also demonstrated Workflow Composer to nearly 1,000 people on the main stage. These are three different exposures; the conference audience must not be attributed to the small meetups. No resulting Dapr installs, qualified leads, or revenue count is disclosed. ([Diagrid practitioner recap](https://www.diagrid.io/blog/gophercon-2025-workflows-community-and-whats-next-for-dapr-diagrid), 2025-08-29)
- **Measured relevance of events to an OSS audience:** the State of Dapr 2025 report says **27%** use events as a technology-information source, mentioning local meetups alongside major conferences. The study covers **202 daily Dapr practitioners**, commissioned by Diagrid and conducted by Dimensional Research. This is an existing-user sample, not a representative survey of all infrastructure engineers; the 27% does not isolate meetups or prove acquisition. ([report PDF](https://22146261.fs1.hubspotusercontent-na1.net/hubfs/22146261/State%20of%20Dapr/state-of-dapr-2025-report.pdf), released 2025-03-14; [release and sample description](https://www.diagrid.io/blog/the-state-of-dapr-2025-report), 2025-03-14)
- **Working model with an observable artifact: Altinity, PostHog, CelerData, and Sentry:** a July 8, 2025 meetup at Sentry's San Francisco office combined technical data-lake presentations and generated a recap with recordings and slides. The CEO's report describes three hours of technical exchange. This verifies company-hosted venue sharing and event-to-content reuse; attendance, pipeline, and adoption uplift are **not verified**. ([Robert Hodges's recap](https://altinity.com/blog/real-time-data-lakes-meetup-at-sentry-in-san-francisco), published 2025-07-14, page also displays update 2025-09-16)

### A2. Build a regional event map, not a list of conference names

- **Proposed mapping procedure:** define the region as cities reachable within a stated travel budget, then search both topics and practitioner roles: Kubernetes, cloud native, Linux, SRE, DevOps, platform engineering, networking/eBPF, storage, virtualization/KubeVirt, GitOps, observability, and infrastructure-as-code. Maintain one record per organizer group and separate records for its event editions. This avoids confusing a dormant group with an upcoming opportunity. Meetup's discovery interface explicitly separates **Events** and **Groups** and supports location/radius filtering. ([Meetup discovery](https://www.meetup.com/find/), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Meetup.com mechanics:** search each city and neighboring city separately, inspect the group's recent event history, and follow the organizer's linked website. Member count and displayed RSVPs are not checked-in attendance. Use the last actual meeting, recurring topics, and organizer activity as qualification signals; completeness of Meetup's local coverage is **not verified**. ([Meetup discovery](https://www.meetup.com/find/), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Luma mechanics:** inspect city pages, Tech/AI categories, and community calendars; follow the calendar behind a relevant event instead of bookmarking only one RSVP page. Luma exposes featured calendars as well as individual events. For infrastructure products, use it to find adjacent builder/operator communities and company-hosted evenings; no evidence establishes that every Kubernetes meetup is indexed there. ([Luma discovery](https://luma.com/discover), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **CNCF groups and KCDs are separate discovery layers:** start from the CNCF community-group entry point and the official KCD directory. Search chapters even when they have no imminent conference: the relationship with a local organizer can produce an evening talk months before a KCD. Reconcile redirects and new chapter URLs following the 2026 platform migration. ([community groups](https://community.cncf.io/), publication undated; [KCD directory](https://www.cncf.io/kcds/), publication undated; [migration announcement](https://github.com/cncf/contribute-site/blob/main/blog/2026-05-29-cloud-native-project-monthly-may-2026.md), 2026-05-29; checked 2026-11-25)
- **DevOpsDays:** use the global calendar to find city editions, then inspect each edition's proposal, sponsorship, program, and contact pages. The calendar also links historical programs and recordings, useful for detecting repeated topics and identifying local speakers. Do not assume one sponsorship or one accepted proposal covers multiple cities. ([event calendar](https://devopsdays.org/events/), publication undated; [Chicago sponsor FAQ](https://assets.devopsdays.org/events/2025/chicago/2025-chicago-devopsdays-prospectus.pdf), 2025 edition, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Sessionize:** its official speaker documentation says Discover events requires a public profile with location, expertise, and topics. Organizers decide whether their CFP is discoverable; a missing result does not mean no CFP exists. Store direct CFP URLs from event sites and newsletters too. Saved sessions can be reused, and the speaker dashboard tracks submissions and organizer communications. ([Sessionize speaker dashboard documentation](https://sessionize.com/playbook/speaker-dashboard), publication date not independently verified; checked 2026-11-25)
- **PaperCall:** historical event-directory results are still discoverable, but direct access to the principal homepage/directory failed in this research. A competing CFP vendor reports a shutdown, but the primary shutdown notice and its date were **not verified**. Treat PaperCall as a historical lead source and resolve every opportunity on the organizer's own site; do not teach it as a confirmed functioning submission channel in November 2026. ([PaperCall directory](https://www.papercall.io/events), publication undated, direct retrieval unsuccessful; [indexed provider directory](https://prod.papercall.io/events), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **confs.tech:** its public conference-data repository is a practical machine-readable seed list; use it to identify candidate events and CFP links, then validate against the official event page. A community-maintained dataset is not a guarantee of complete regional coverage or current deadlines. ([confs.tech](https://confs.tech/), publication undated; [maintained dataset](https://github.com/tech-conferences/conference-data), continuously updated, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **dev.events:** use the country/region and topic filters, particularly Docker/Kubernetes, DevOps, SRE, Cloud, Linux/OS, and Open Source. Its listing inventory is a discovery aid rather than a count of all relevant local communities. Store the original event URL and verification date in the map. ([developer-event directory](https://dev.events/), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Weekly completeness check:** CNCF's KubeWeekly #419 linked open CFPs, sponsorship prospectuses, and upcoming local meetings, including a Dapr session in Luxembourg. #424 listed a Cilium introduction in Kochi and an Argo CD/GitOps meetup in Bratislava. Comparing a regional map against these lists catches events a single platform search missed. These are evidence of actual programming, not measured marketing conversion. ([KubeWeekly #419](https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-419/), 2025-02-07; [KubeWeekly #424](https://www.cncf.io/kubeweekly/kubeweekly-424/), 2025-03-14)
- **Universities and local user groups — proposed extension:** map campus computing societies, distributed-systems research groups, Linux user groups, and university-hosted cloud communities alongside commercial venues. Confirm whether outside attendees are permitted and whether the session is an introductory lab or an operator talk. KCD Lima 2026 used UTEC; its organizer is also a university professor, demonstrating a real university connection, not a measured university-to-enterprise-sales funnel. ([KCD Lima organizer retrospective](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/09/15/what-i-learned-organizing-kcd-lima-2026/), 2026-09-15)

### A3. CFP timing and becoming a useful speaker

- **Organizer guidance, not a statistical average:** DevOpsDays recommends keeping the CFP open for at least a month, allowing at least two weeks for selection, and closing **6–8 weeks before the event**; speakers generally need **4–6 weeks** to arrange travel/time off. Work backwards from these requirements rather than checking CFPs only a month before travel. ([DevOpsDays organizing guide](https://devopsdays.org/organizing), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Actual KCD calendar:** KCD Beijing 2025 opened its CFP on **December 26, 2024**, closed on **February 5, 2025**, and held the event on **March 15, 2025**. The close-to-event interval was **38 days**, calculated from the posted dates. That is shorter than the DevOpsDays recommendation: maintain event-specific deadlines and time zones. ([KCD Beijing CFP](https://sessionize.com/kcd-beijing-2025/), publication undated; CFP dates 2024-12-26–2025-02-05, event 2025-03-15)
- **Meetup calls can be much more immediate:** Chandigarh's July 26, 2025 meetup at Infosys welcomed first-time and experienced speakers; a September 20 edition again requested lightning talks, deep dives, demos, and case studies. Repeated calls support an ongoing need for programming. They do **not** prove the stronger blanket statement that all organizers are constantly short of speakers. ([July call](https://sessionize.com/cncgchandigarh/), publication undated, event 2025-07-26; [September call](https://sessionize.com/ctencfchandigarh/), publication undated, event 2025-09-20)
- **Direct evidence of organizer needs:** Cloud Native and Kubernetes Oslo invites speakers of all experience levels, points to a Sessionize submission route, and explicitly requests venue hosts and sponsors. Its repository includes a May 2026 event-material folder. An offer to solve an organizer's practical problem—speaker, room, or recording—is a more concrete entry point than asking for promotion. ([Oslo organizer repository](https://github.com/Cloud-Native-and-Kubernetes-Oslo/meetups), publication undated, includes 2026-05 material; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Verified acceptance constraints:** DevOpsDays Chicago's 2025 CFP required three concrete takeaways, rejected vendor pitches and third-party submissions, and preferred content the local audience was unlikely to have seen. Formats included a 30-minute talk, a five-minute Ignite with 20 automatically advancing slides, and a 45-minute workshop. Open Space topics could be proposed onsite. ([Chicago 2025 CFP](https://devopsdays.org/events/2025-chicago/propose), publication undated; CFP opened 2024-08-05 and closed 2024-10-20)
- **Proposed speaker package for a Cozystack-class project:** prepare a short bio, headshot, a 100–150-word abstract, three takeaways, target skill level, and 15/30/45-minute versions. Example topic: “Operating tenant Kubernetes clusters on shared bare metal: isolation, recovery, and cost tradeoffs.” Include a reproducible lab and a failure/recovery sequence; disclose your affiliation. These are suggested assets, not proven acceptance-rate optimizations. They operationalize the concrete-takeaway requirement. ([Chicago CFP requirements](https://devopsdays.org/events/2025-chicago/propose), publication undated, 2025 edition; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed first contact:** reference a recent session, offer one specific complementary talk, give two available dates and a recording/demo link, and state whether you can travel or speak remotely. Offer a backup lightning talk when the program is full. Do not send a marketing agency's proposal where the CFP requires the speaker to submit personally. Sessionize's ability to support submissions on another person's behalf does not override an event's own rules. ([Sessionize submission mechanics](https://sessionize.com/playbook/getting-started-speakers), publication date not independently verified; [Chicago restrictions](https://devopsdays.org/events/2025-chicago/propose), publication undated, 2025 edition; checked 2026-11-25)

### A4. KCD organization, sponsorship economics, and attendance

- **Historical policy change:** CNCF announced a refreshed program on August 6, 2024, including a **30-event annual limit**, a **500-registration cap**, use of CNCF's registration system, and branding requirements. These are historical rules, not an accurate standalone description of 2026. ([KCD program refresh](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2024/08/06/refreshing-the-kcd-program-a-new-chapter-in-community-building/), 2024-08-06)
- **Current official rules:** the inspected KCD FAQ describes a one-time application, staff-created GitHub tracking issues, nonprofit operation, a fiscal-sponsor contract, financial transparency, and CNCF-approved registration. It specifies **300/350/600-person registration tiers**, including speakers and sponsors, with exceptions requiring approval. Financial support is based on need/geography. These are event caps and support tiers, not sponsorship prices. ([KCD FAQ](https://www.cncf.io/kcds/faq/), publication undated, describes 2026 program; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Selection has two layers:** selection of cities into the program is distinct from selection of talks. For the 2025 host cohort, six CNCF staff reviewed applications using factors including community activity, maintainer/ambassador participation, regional balance, team quality, and past events. A sponsor cannot infer a speaking entitlement from the city's admission to the program. ([CNCF host-selection announcement](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2024/10/30/mark-your-calendars-here-come-the-2025-kubernetes-community-days/), 2024-10-30)
- **Public 2026 price example, not a universal rate card:** KCD Gujarat lists **Platinum $5,000; Gold $3,500; Silver $2,500; Bronze $1,200**. Listed benefits include different booth/table sizes, ticket allotments of 10/6/4/3, and stage benefits for some tiers. Diversity and swag options are **$800** each. These are advertised USD prices, not proof of payments, achieved leads, or availability; the site describes INR equivalents as approximate. ([KCD Gujarat sponsorships](https://www.kcdgujarat.com/sponsorship), publication undated, 2026 edition; checked 2026-11-25)
- **A provenance trap in a prospectus:** the Delhi PDF URL contains “2025,” while its opened cover says **KCD New Delhi 2026**. The document lists a **$5,000 Gold** package with a medium booth, eight tickets, and a technical presentation/demo. Do not cite this as a verified 2025 price. Retain the document's internal year and request a dated prospectus when budgeting. ([Delhi prospectus](https://www.kcddelhi.com/KCD%20NEW%20Delhi%202025%20SPONSORSHIP.pdf), publication date not stated; internally labeled 2026, checked 2026-11-25)
- **Rules differ by event family:** DevOpsDays explicitly does **not** sell conference speaking slots; sponsor employees apply through the CFP. Its Chicago FAQ also refuses attendee contact lists and multi-city sponsorship discounts. Some KCD prospectuses advertise technical stage benefits. Therefore inspect the local contract, editorial review conditions, and distinction between sponsor acknowledgments and independently selected talks; do not generalize one policy to the other. ([DevOpsDays sponsor policy](https://devopsdays.org/sponsor/), publication undated; [Chicago 2025 sponsor FAQ](https://assets.devopsdays.org/events/2025/chicago/2025-chicago-devopsdays-prospectus.pdf), publication undated, 2025 edition; [Gujarat offer](https://www.kcdgujarat.com/sponsorship), publication undated, 2026 edition; checked 2026-11-25)
- **First-event benchmark:** the organizers of KCD Helsinki 2025 report **300 participants, 34 breakout sessions, and 41 speakers**. The page links session recordings, photographs, an aftermovie, and a transparency report. This is a useful example of a local event with a public afterlife; it discloses no sponsor-attributed customer count. ([Helsinki event recap](https://community2.cncf.io/events/details/cncf-kcd-helsinki-presents-kcd-helsinki-2025/), publication undated, event 2025-05-06; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Attendance is not registration:** Lima's organizer reports **1,377 registrations and 520 attendees in 2025**, a calculated show-up rate of **37.8%**. For 2026 he reports **75% attendance growth**, 60 speakers, 54 sessions, and 89 submitted proposals. Survey satisfaction was 4.7/5, but the response count is not disclosed. These are one organizer's figures; no project-adoption attribution is provided, and 54 sessions divided by 89 proposals is not necessarily the CFP acceptance rate because formats/keynotes may differ. ([Lima retrospective](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/09/15/what-i-learned-organizing-kcd-lima-2026/), 2026-09-15)
- **Conflicting show-up guidance:** CNCF's meetup guide mentions roughly **30% RSVP no-shows** and **92% KCD show-up**, without a dated sample or methodology. Lima's 37.8% is dramatically lower. Keep both visible; tickets, free registration, counting rules, and local circumstances may differ, but the causes are **not verified**. Plan catering from your own confirmations and prior check-ins, not an asserted universal 70% or 92% rate. ([CNCF meetup guide](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups/blob/main/best_practices.md), publication undated; [Lima figures](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/09/15/what-i-learned-organizing-kcd-lima-2026/), 2026-09-15; checked 2026-11-25)
- **No completed 2026 global benchmark:** individual 2026 transparency reports are available, but a full-year 2026 KCD attendance total is **not verified** at this cutoff. Use the CNCF report library to retrieve the relevant city/year before buying a package; an advertised capacity is not a retrospective attendance count. ([CNCF report library](https://www.cncf.io/reports/), continuously updated, publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)

### A5. Start or co-organize a chapter without turning it into a vendor channel

- **Bevy is historical context:** CNCF's July 2020 announcement introduced Bevy and platform-cost coverage. **[OLD]** The May 29, 2026 project newsletter explicitly says migration to **Open Community Groups** is complete and highlights its built-in Call for Speakers workflow. A course should teach current organizer tooling rather than replay a Bevy walkthrough as current setup instructions. ([Bevy announcement](https://www.cncf.io/blog/2020/07/01/introducing-cloud-native-community-groups/), [OLD] 2020-07-01; [migration confirmation](https://github.com/cncf/contribute-site/blob/main/blog/2026-05-29-cloud-native-project-monthly-may-2026.md), 2026-05-29)
- **Chapter application and support:** the current CNCF README uses a GitHub issue for a new community group and asks for the name “Cloud Native CITY NAME.” It lists platform service coverage and a **one-time $100 swag coupon after two in-person events with more than ten attendees**. It explicitly does not promise general cash sponsorship for chapters. Do not confuse this application route with the KCD application route. ([CNCF group rules](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups/blob/main/README.md), publication undated, 2026 text; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Co-organizer governance:** CNCF recommends at least two organizers, with no more than 50% from the same company; it limits formal organizer membership to two groups and describes approval for adding organizers. Splitting venue, speakers, A/V, and sponsorship work is supported by the guide. Co-organizing is not permission to control all speaking slots for your employer. ([organizer rules](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups/blob/main/organizers.md), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Continued activity and consequences:** the CNCF README says groups with over 90 days without events may be removed; technical community groups must meet at least six times per calendar year. Repeated vendor-neutrality violations escalate from a written warning to a meeting/final warning and then disabling the chapter. This is a documented enforcement mechanism; a named 2025–2026 chapter ban was **not verified**. ([CNCF activity and neutrality rules](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups/blob/main/README.md), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Company-sponsored alternative: Grafana & Friends:** Grafana advertises **70+ groups**, a minimum **four meetups per year**, monthly organizer office hours, an attendance-based catering budget, and free/low-cost venues. Its speaker guidance requests technical **20–40-minute** talks. Unlike a vendor-neutral CNCF chapter, this branded program has Grafana-specific content and competitor guidance. This is a real operating model, not evidence that four meetings is the optimal cadence for every community. ([Grafana organizer and host program](https://grafana.com/community/meetups/), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed 30-person pilot budget, not a market benchmark:** use an employer-provided room/projector at $0 cash, 30 meals/drinks at an assumed $15 = $450, $75 for adapters/signage, and a 15% reserve of $78.75: **$603.75 cash**, excluding staff time, travel, tax, and any platform charges. Replace every assumption with local quotes. Separate “venue donated” from “venue free to society.” Company-hosted infrastructure meetups are evidenced by the Sentry event; its actual spend was not published. ([Sentry-hosted meetup recap](https://altinity.com/blog/real-time-data-lakes-meetup-at-sentry-in-san-francisco), 2025-07-14, updated 2025-09-16)
- **Operational constraints that affect a technical lab:** confirm guest Wi-Fi capacity, cloud quotas, screen readability, and projector adapters before promising a hands-on session. Arrange check-in, accessibility, recording permission, and cleanup ownership with the venue. The CNCF checklist includes technical checks, speaker logistics, dietary needs, and slide review for vendor pitches. ([CNCF host/organizer checklist](https://github.com/cncf/communitygroups/blob/main/best_practices.md), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)

### A6. A speaking circuit and daily/weekly routine

- **Observed multi-city pattern:** Grafana announced half-day GrafanaCON Local editions in **Singapore, Berlin, Toronto, and Bangalore** for May 27–31, 2025, following the main conference. This verifies a four-city content-distribution plan, not one person delivering an identical talk ten times and not measured conversion. The latter “ten-city circuit” remains a proposed tactic below. ([GrafanaCON Local announcement](https://grafana.com/blog/grafanacon-local-our-biggest-community-event-is-coming-to-a-city-near-you/), 2025-04-03)
- **Proposed ten-city exercise:** build one versioned core demonstration and three abstracts—operator reliability, platform architecture, and contributor internals. Pitch ten suitable groups in travel clusters; offer the same talk transparently, adapt prerequisites and local examples, and retain a record of which version each city saw. Reuse reduces preparation work, but repeated-content acceptance is organizer-specific; DevOpsDays prefers new material while allowing previously presented content. No verified “ten cities → X customers” result was found. ([DevOpsDays organizing guidance](https://devopsdays.org/organizing), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed daily routine, 30–45 minutes:** check upcoming CFP deadlines and organizer replies; verify two candidate events; send one individually relevant proposal or practical offer; update next-action dates. Use an additional technical preparation block for the demo rather than counting prospecting as the whole job. This is an instructional timebox, not a published Diagrid/Grafana staffing benchmark. Sessionize provides the submission/inbox mechanisms that make it feasible. ([Sessionize speaker workflow](https://sessionize.com/playbook/speaker-dashboard), publication date not verified; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed weekly routine:** Monday reconcile calendars and deadlines; Tuesday prepare or rehearse one technical segment; Wednesday handle organizer logistics and agreed follow-ups; Thursday attend/speak/co-host when scheduled; Friday publish reusable material and review outcomes. Travel weeks replace preparation capacity rather than adding unlimited work. Use monthly organizer office hours as a coordination point. Grafana documents monthly office hours and quarterly-minimum events, not this exact weekly schedule. ([Grafana organizer program](https://grafana.com/community/meetups/), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed organizer/event CRM schema:** `group_id`, city/language, public organizer contact and preferred channel, last observed event, technical audience, canonical event/CFP URL, deadline with timezone, notification date, format, costs/travel support, proposed topic/version, status, last contact, next action, and recording rights. Deduplicate listings by organizer/date rather than treating Meetup, Luma, and CNCF copies as separate events. This extends the submission tracking already provided by Sessionize to cover relationships and offline logistics. ([Sessionize speaker tools](https://sessionize.com/speakers), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed follow-up sequence:** within two working days send the organizer the slides, demo repository, and answers promised during Q&A; contact individual attendees only through the channel they agreed to use. After two weeks, ask whether the lab or evaluation was attempted; after 30/90 days, record a contribution, deployment, evaluation, or no observed outcome. These intervals are experimental defaults. Do not assume access to an attendee email list: DevOpsDays explicitly withholds it. ([DevOpsDays Chicago sponsor FAQ](https://assets.devopsdays.org/events/2025/chicago/2025-chicago-devopsdays-prospectus.pdf), publication undated, 2025 edition; checked 2026-11-25)

### A7. Measurement: stars, contributors, adopters, and commercial leads

- **Proposed event funnel:** count registrations, actual check-ins, relevant technical conversations, lab attempts/completions, agreed follow-ups, activated evaluations, first contributions, and retained adopters separately. Give each event a short URL/UTM and a versioned demo page; record self-reported discovery alongside click data. A star is an expression of interest, not a deployment. Track preparation, travel, venue, and follow-up costs; report cost per completed evaluation only when both numerator and denominator are observed. This measurement design addresses the causal limitation CNCF itself acknowledges in project-growth reporting. ([Cilium journey report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/cilium-project-journey-report/), 2024-11-11)
- **Cilium: useful evidence, wrong attribution shortcut:** the CNCF journey report explicitly says it cannot assign every data point to specific inputs. It also contains different contributor snapshots—**3,195+** in the project snapshot and **4,464** in the growth narrative—with differing chart periods. Do not select one and call it a meetup result. DevStats' broad contributor definition includes comments, reviews, issues, PRs, and commits; it is not equivalent to code authors. ([Cilium journey report](https://www.cncf.io/reports/cilium-project-journey-report/), 2024-11-11)
- **Crossplane: preserve differing source numbers:** its graduation post reports **3,000+ contributors from 480+ companies**, while CNCF's same-day announcement says **3,000+ contributors from 450+ organizations**. Both are project-wide figures; neither establishes meetup-sourced growth. Differences in snapshots/definitions may explain the discrepancy, but that reconciliation is **not verified**. ([Crossplane announcement](https://blog.crossplane.io/crossplane-cncf-graduation/), 2025-11-06; [CNCF announcement](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2025/11/06/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-graduation-of-crossplane/), 2025-11-06)
- **Argo, Dapr, and Talos: activity evidence has different strength:** CNCF's weekly calendar confirms Argo CD and Dapr local-session programming. TalosCon's 2025 prospectus specifies a planned **300 attendees** and local group/partner activities; that is an advertised plan, not actual attendance and not a small-meetup acquisition result. A verified attendee-to-contributor/adopter conversion series for these examples was not found. Its January 2026 sample was **2,050**, approximately half from a probability-based panel and half non-probability online sampling, weighted to the US population. The mixed audio/video definition and method change matter when comparing years; neither wave specifically measures B2B infrastructure buyers. ([Buzzsprout dashboard](https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats), October 2026 data, publication day not stated; [Edison preference table](https://www.edisonresearch.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/The-Infinite-Dial-2025-Presentation.pdf), 2025-03-20; checked 2026-11-25)

### B2. YouTube podcast rules and measurement changes

- **Verified mechanics:** a YouTube podcast is a **playlist**, and its episodes are videos. Keep full episodes in the podcast playlist, including multiple seasons; keep promotional clips separate. Eligible shows can receive podcast discovery features and appear in YouTube Music. Supporting Shorts do **not** appear in YouTube Music. Designating a playlist as a podcast does not guarantee recommendation or eligibility. ([YouTube podcast setup](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12751636?hl=en), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **RSS ingestion is an audio-to-static-video convenience:** YouTube converts selected feed episodes using show artwork and imports subsequent episodes; it does not distribute your show to other services. It does not automatically replace an already published video's audio when you replace the RSS file. Set the import start date to avoid duplicates when native videos already exist. Availability is country-dependent. ([YouTube RSS ingestion](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13525207?hl=en), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Ad handling matters:** YouTube's RSS instructions prohibit uploaded advertisements while distinguishing branded content such as host-read sponsorships, which require declaration and compliance with paid-promotion policies. Inspect the actual export rather than blindly importing an audio feed carrying third-party ad insertions. The docs recommend reviewing initially private imports for copyright/monetization issues before publishing. ([YouTube RSS rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13525207?hl=en), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **A dated measurement break:** from **March 31, 2025**, Shorts views count starts/replays without a minimum watch duration. The previous metric survives as **engaged views**; YPP eligibility and earnings remain based on engaged views. An increase in raw clip views across that date is not necessarily better distribution or more interested engineers. ([TeamYouTube announcement](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en), published 2025-03-26, effective 2025-03-31)
- **A dated monetization clarification, not a blanket clip ban:** on **July 15, 2025**, YouTube renamed repetitious content to inauthentic content and clarified repetitive/mass-produced material. It explicitly said the reused-content policy was unchanged. Do not teach “AI is banned” or “podcast clips were demonetized” as the rule. Original practitioner conversations and meaningful edits still require the normal eligibility checks. ([YouTube monetization policy](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), update 2025-07-15; checked 2026-11-25)

### B7. Guest booking, cadence, and growth mechanisms worth testing

- **Proposed guest selection:** use public incident reports, migration write-ups, release engineering changes, and maintainer design documents as the booking queue. Request the engineer who made the tradeoff, not automatically the founder. Ask for the baseline, workload, constraint, rejected alternatives, failure, measured outcome, and what remains uncertain. KubeFM's article-first selection supplies a verified model; the proposed question rubric is an instructional adaptation. ([KubeFM guest-selection FAQ](https://kube.fm/faqs), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed booking workflow:** keep a guest CRM with topic artifact, affiliation, timezone, technical proof point, invitation status, recording date, permission to publish/clip, correction deadline, and publication date. Send a concise invitation specifying the engineering question and time commitment. Prepare a backup episode without a guest and preflight audio before recording day. These are production safeguards, not claimed response-rate benchmarks. ([Changelog guest preparation](https://changelog.com/guest), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Observed sustainable schedules differ:** Google's show describes itself as biweekly; Changelog's 2025 plan specifies Monday news, midweek interviews, and Friday conversation. Neither is proof that a small infrastructure vendor should publish three times a week. A proposed starter season is six biweekly episodes with two completed before launch; review completed production hours and technical audience response before committing to another season. ([Google show description](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/kubernetes-podcast-from-google/id1370049232), publication undated; [Changelog schedule](https://changelog.com/posts/a-new-era-for-the-changelog-podcast-universe), 2024-12-05; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed differentiated formats:** maintainers explain one design choice; an operator reconstructs a production incident; two hosts interpret release news against a runnable example; a live session debugs a disposable lab. Preserve commands and versions for demos, and an audio explanation for non-viewers. Day Two DevOps' strong kro episode supports interest in concrete deployment topics, but no controlled study ranks these four formats. ([Day Two DevOps episode results](https://nedinthecloud.com/2025/12/30/2025-year-in-review/), 2025-12-30; [Rawkode format archive](https://rawkode.academy/shows/rawkode-live), publication undated, historical examples [OLD] 2020)
- **Evidence-supported growth mechanisms, with attribution limits:** KubeFM sources guests from already useful technical articles; Changelog asks guests to share the finished episode and produces full video plus clips; Spotify's selected creators report consumption gains under its program. These verify practices and some outcomes, not a causal claim that any guest cross-post or any three clips generate a predictable subscriber lift. Track each mechanism separately in your own pilot. ([KubeFM FAQ](https://kube.fm/faqs), publication undated; [Changelog guest guide](https://changelog.com/guest), publication undated; [Spotify creator outcomes](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2025-02-13/spotifys-partner-program-helps-creators-increase-revenue-and-consumption-of-video-podcasts/), 2025-02-13; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed clip experiment for Shorts, LinkedIn, and X:** choose one complete tradeoff or surprising debugging result per clip; retain the technical caveat and readable captions. Publish a native clip plus a clear route to the full episode/demo, and create a text thread explaining the same result with commands or diagrams. Compare qualified visits, full-episode continuation, and returning viewers—not only impressions. A current infra-specific causal benchmark for LinkedIn/X clip conversion is **not verified**. ([YouTube full-episode/clip distinction](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12751636?hl=en), publication undated; [Shorts measurement change](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en), 2025-03-26; checked 2026-11-25)

### B8. Benchmarks, sponsorship economics, and lead value

- **Fresh host benchmark:** Buzzsprout's **October 2026** dashboard covers **111,713 active hosted shows**, 150,766 new episodes, and 88,101,139 monthly downloads. Its displayed first-seven-day episode bands are: lower 50% **0–27**; next 25% **28–98**; next 15% **99–424**; next 5% **425–1,033**; next 4% **1,034–4,657**; top 1% **4,658+**. Thus the displayed top-quarter boundary is 99 and top-tenth boundary 425. These are Buzzsprout episode bands, not a universal ranking of all podcasts. ([Buzzsprout platform statistics](https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats), data October 2026, publication day not stated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Benchmark caveats:** Buzzsprout states IAB 2.2 compliance. Its host population is not a random sample of infrastructure podcasts, and the page's total active-show count is not an explicit count of episodes used for each percentile calculation. Downloads measure file delivery, not completion or deduplicated people. Day Two DevOps' 90-day numbers therefore cannot be placed directly in a seven-day percentile band. ([Buzzsprout statistics](https://www.buzzsprout.com/stats), October 2026 snapshot; [Day Two DevOps methodology](https://nedinthecloud.com/2025/12/30/2025-year-in-review/), 2025-12-30)
- **Libsyn:** its official analytics documentation exposes first **7/30/90-day** episode-download windows. A fresh primary-source numeric percentile table was **not verified**; The Feed's website could not be retrieved in this research. Do not paste old Libsyn thresholds beside current Buzzsprout thresholds without aligning dates, cohorts, and windows. ([Libsyn stats documentation](https://five.libsynsupport.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412870741275-Review-the-Statistics-That-Come-in-the-Basic-Package), publication/update date not independently verified; [The Feed](https://thefeed.libsyn.com/), retrieval unsuccessful; checked 2026-11-25)
- **A commercial rate card is not an effectiveness study:** KubeFM's FAQ offers sponsor disclosure, a logo, a 30-second pre/mid-roll, and campaign mentions; Changelog demonstrates customized host-led ad treatments. Those are purchasable deliverables. Neither inspected page establishes a standard infrastructure CPM, revenue per listener, or guaranteed lead return. ([KubeFM sponsorship terms](https://kube.fm/faqs), publication undated; [Changelog commercial examples](https://changelog.com/sponsor), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed sponsorship calculation:** at an assumed $40 CPM, 1,000 qualifying episode downloads yield **$40 gross per ad slot**; four such releases yield $160 before commissions, unsold inventory, and production costs. The $40 is an illustrative assumption, **not a verified 2025–2026 benchmark**. A fixed-price technical sponsorship may package more than audio reach, so request the exact window, measurement standard, make-good terms, and separate video/newsletter deliverables. ([example bundled offer](https://learnkube.com/sponsorships), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed lead-value calculation:** for a company-owned show, evaluate production cost against observed qualified evaluations and subsequent retained customers. A hypothetical $1,000 episode producing two qualified evaluations costs $500 per evaluation; without later conversion and margin data, it has no demonstrated positive ROI. Keep guest relationships, learning, recruiting, and brand effects as separate benefits rather than converting them into invented pipeline. The old Duckbill claim of long-lived leads is suggestive practitioner evidence, not a modern conversion coefficient. ([Duckbill practitioner material](https://www.lastweekinaws.com/media/2021/01/Devreloping-20210106.pdf), [OLD] 2021-01)
- **Proposed podcast scorecard:** for each episode retain topic, guest, production hours, 7/30/90-day RSS downloads, YouTube impressions/click-through/watch time, returning viewers, Spotify consumption, site visits, lab starts, and self-reported discovery. Deduplicate people only where you have a defensible method; do not sum podcast downloads, Shorts starts, and newsletter subscribers into “unique reach.” This extends the explicit time windows in host analytics to a company outcome ledger. ([Libsyn episode-window documentation](https://five.libsynsupport.com/hc/en-us/articles/4412870741275-Review-the-Statistics-That-Come-in-the-Basic-Package), publication undated; [YouTube Shorts counting rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en), 2025-03-26; checked 2026-11-25)

### B9. Transcripts, search, and LLM discovery

- **Verified user value:** Apple automatically creates transcripts after publication; users can search text and jump to that moment. Apple notes that changed dynamically inserted audio is not reflected in the original transcript. For a technical show, correct proper nouns and commands in the transcript you control and avoid treating an auto-transcript as an accurate permanent record of dynamically changing sponsorships. ([Apple transcript documentation](https://podcasters.apple.com/support/5316-transcripts-on-apple-podcasts), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed canonical episode page:** publish a crawlable HTML transcript, concise summary, chapters, guest/affiliation, release date, relevant versions, links to source code/design documents, and corrections. Put code blocks and diagrams next to the relevant explanation. Kube Cuddle and Arrested DevOps provide real transcript-plus-episode examples. Accessibility and source usability are directly observable; an SEO traffic multiplier is **not verified**. ([Kube Cuddle episode/transcript entry](https://kubecuddle.transistor.fm/episodes/taylor-dolezal), 2025-01-15; [Arrested DevOps transcript](https://www.arresteddevops.com/ai-sdlc/), 2025-10-01)
- **LLM/AI-search claim boundary:** Google's official guidance says ordinary SEO fundamentals remain applicable to AI Overviews/AI Mode, with no special additional optimization requirement. Making technically accurate text available can make the episode usable as a web source; it does not guarantee indexing, training inclusion, citation, or product recommendation. “Publish transcripts and LLMs will promote your platform” is **not verified**. ([Google AI-features documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), inspected version last updated 2025-12-10; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Proposed measurement:** record search impressions/clicks for episode pages, identifiable referral traffic, and customer self-reports; manually audit a fixed set of relevant questions over time and retain actual cited URLs. Treat changing AI answers as noisy observations, not a rank guarantee, and distinguish crawlers from human visits. No verified infrastructure-podcast transcript-to-LLM-pipeline uplift was found. ([Google AI-search guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features), inspected update 2025-12-10; checked 2026-11-25)

### B10. Failure stories, podfade, and practices that became obsolete

- **A documented portfolio contraction:** Changelog explained that the work of producing multiple weekly shows constrained experimentation; its 2025 strategy consolidated around The Changelog and full-length video. This is a first-person capacity tradeoff with named affected shows, including Ship It. It should inform season sizing and staffing, rather than being labeled a proven collapse of developer-podcast demand. ([Changelog strategy explanation](https://changelog.com/posts/a-new-era-for-the-changelog-podcast-universe), 2024-12-05, effective January 2025)
- **Fresh practitioner podfade example:** Ned Bellavance's 2025 review says **Chaos Lever went on indefinite hiatus**, while Day Two DevOps continued. The same practitioner can sustain one format and suspend another; backlog, competing paid work, and host capacity belong in the operating model. Do not infer failure solely from an industry-wide inactivity percentage. ([Ned's retrospective](https://nedinthecloud.com/2025/12/30/2025-year-in-review/), 2025-12-30)
- **Transparent but vendor-produced podfade research:** Slice's October 2026 analysis of Podcast Index counted **4,391,041 cleaned shows**, of which **413,475 (9.4%)** published within 90 days. Among **3,636,474** shows classified as stopped—nothing in the prior year, started before 2026—**24.4%** stopped after one episode and **56.5%** before episode seven. The publisher sells clip tools; the analysis was not independently reproduced here. Finished seasons, truncated feeds, and missing/deleted episodes complicate “failure” interpretation. This does not prove clips prevent podfade or that 90.6% of newly launched shows fail. ([Podcast Census 2026](https://tryslicemedia.com/data/podcast-census-2026), updated 2026-10-02; underlying index snapshot 2026-09-26)
- **Recording failure is real operational risk:** Descript's status page documents an April 17, 2026 connection incident affecting Rooms/SquadCast. Its own user/staff discussion records transition concerns. Maintain a tested local backup and do not schedule an important maintainer solely on an untested migrated workflow. A status incident is evidence of interruption, not evidence of permanent product failure. ([official incident](https://status.descript.com/incidents/01KPE2BWYSDYCG8YQME6NQ2K4H), 2026-04-17; [transition discussion](https://descript.canny.io/feature-requests/p/scheduling-tool-for-rooms), relevant comments 2026-04-15)
- **What stopped being a sound assumption:** “RSS distributes the full video everywhere,” “Apple HLS launched in 2025,” “Spotify still requires 12 episodes,” and “Shorts views mean the same thing before and after March 2025” are contradicted by current platform documentation and dated announcements. These are concrete mechanics changes, unlike a vague assertion that audio podcasts or interviews no longer work. ([YouTube RSS rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13525207?hl=en), publication undated; [Apple HLS](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/02/apple-introduces-a-new-video-podcast-experience-on-apple-podcasts/), 2026-02-16; [Spotify thresholds](https://newsroom.spotify.com/2026-01-07/spotify-partner-program-updates/), 2026-01-07; [Shorts change](https://support.google.com/youtube/thread/333869549?hl=en), 2025-03-26; checked 2026-11-25)
- **Bans/backlash boundary:** YouTube documents copyright and monetization consequences; no named 2025–2026 infrastructure podcast banned specifically for routine guest interviews, legitimate clips, or transcripts was **verified**. The July 2025 inauthentic-content clarification must not be embellished into such a case. Use the documented consolidation, hiatus, and recording-transition stories as the actual failures/tradeoffs. ([YouTube monetization policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392), update 2025-07-15; [podcast eligibility rules](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12751636?hl=en), publication undated; checked 2026-11-25)
