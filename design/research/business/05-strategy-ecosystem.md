# Long-term strategy, ecosystem marketing, and PR for open-source infrastructure, 2025–2026

Research cut-off: **25 November 2026**. Prepared as source material for a free public course, with Cozystack-like cloud infrastructure platforms as the reference class.

## Reading this evidence

**Labels:** **Fact** means the cited source directly supports the statement; **Reported result** means a company, foundation, or survey publisher reports the result, without independent causal verification; **Interpretation** means a proposed application of that evidence; **Not verified** identifies an evidence gap. A documented action is not automatically a proven growth tactic. Registration, certification, deployment, revenue, and influenced pipeline are different outcomes.

Dates in citations are publication dates unless explicitly marked **updated**, **effective**, or **publication date not stated**. The latter is intentional: a crawl date is not a publication date. Undated live rules were checked on 2026-11-25; they are useful for current mechanics but do not establish when a rule changed. **[OLD]** marks sources or underlying historical material before 2024. Older incident examples are retained only where requested or necessary to explain a current consequence. No outreach, paid purchase, or private analyst briefing was conducted.

Cozystack's **29 September 2026** announcement says it completed the General Technical Review, the technical part of incubation due diligence. Neither establishes an approved Incubating promotion. The brief's description of Cozystack as Incubating is **not verified**; use the documented stage until a TOC decision confirms otherwise. ## 1. Category design: build a buying vocabulary and an operating practice

### What the platform-engineering category demonstrates

- **[OLD] Framework, not a 2025 benchmark:** Play Bigger advocates defining a problem and category, organizing a market around that definition, and becoming its leading reference. Its often-repeated **76%** claim concerns category value/economics derived from historical venture-backed-company research, including a **2014** study; it is not a current estimate of infrastructure software revenue share, nor evidence that inventing a label causes dominance. Treat it as a strategic hypothesis with selection and survivorship concerns. (https://playbigger.com/publications/what-is-category-design, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://playbigger.com/publications, publication date not stated; underlying study 2014 [OLD])

- **Fact:** Platformengineering.org explicitly connects its origins with launching Humanitec, then building community channels including PlatformCon and educational videos. This is a first-party example of a vendor supporting a professional identity and shared vocabulary around the problem its product addresses. It should not be presented as an independent community spontaneously validating the vendor. (https://platformengineering.org/about, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Reported result:** The community's January 2026 discussion of its 2025 research describes **518 platform-engineering professionals surveyed worldwide**, with platform product-manager adoption reaching **35%** and nearly half of initiatives operating below **$1 million**. These are signs of an organized practice and budget structure, not a census of all enterprises or Humanitec's market share. The audience was selected around platform engineering, creating adoption bias. (https://platformengineering.org/events/state-of-platform-engineering-in-2026-salary-maturity-and-shifting-down-2026-01-20, 2026-01-20)

- **Fact:** Institutional recognition is visible in Gartner's **Market Guide for Internal Developer Portals**, published **31 March 2025**, and **Hype Cycle for Platform Engineering**, published **25 June 2025**. A portal is a user-facing entry point; the platform encompasses broader capabilities and operational responsibilities. Do not teach portals, platforms, and Kubernetes infrastructure as interchangeable product categories. (https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6306515, 2025-03-31; https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6586902, 2025-06-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7188130, 2025-11-14)

- **Fact / concrete mechanism:** CNCF's platform-maturity assessment initiative links assessment to sustainable adoption and opportunities for open-source, commercial, and consulting services. Syntasso's practitioner account describes turning the maturity model into an assessment. This is a reproducible category-building mechanism: give buyers a diagnostic vocabulary, then help them implement the resulting work. Revenue attributable to this initiative is **not verified**. (https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1679, initiative recorded 2025-05-13; https://www.syntasso.io/post/from-framework-to-practice-cncf-s-platform-engineering-maturity-assessment, 2025-09-02)

### What is unsupported, and how to apply the case

- **Reported participation result:** The community’s 2025 report retrospectively records **9,500 virtual-workshop participants**, nearly **40,000 additional engagements** across talks/panels/community conversations, **255+ talks**, and **30 workshops**. These are organizer-defined participation measures, without independently verified deduplication, and supersede using the earlier 40,000-attendee forecast as a result. The report identifies Broadcom VCF as sponsor. Humanitec revenue caused by this category-building activity remains **not verified**. (https://5890440.fs1.hubspotusercontent-eu1.net/hubfs/5890440/State%20of%20Platform%20Engineering%20Vol%204%202025.pdf, 2025; exact publication day not stated; pp. 4 and 29)

- **Interpretation:** For a Cozystack-like platform, start with a recognizable buying problem—operating a multi-tenant cloud, replacing a defined VMware estate, or enabling a regional hosting provider—then show where the platform sits beneath an IDP. A new category is defensible only if customers repeat its problem definition and partners can deliver against it. Measure independently authored references, inclusion in actual requirements, and deployments using the reference architecture; these are proposed metrics, not published benchmarks. (https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6586902, 2025-06-25; https://github.com/cncf/toc/issues/1679, initiative recorded 2025-05-13)

- **Failure boundary:** No dated algorithm or policy change was found that made category design stop working. The demonstrated limitation is evidence quality: vendor-built communities, forecasts, and research recognition cannot establish incremental commercial success. A course should distinguish that limitation from declaring the discipline obsolete. (https://platformengineering.org/about, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6306515, 2025-03-31)

## 2. Analyst relations for small vendors: briefings, inclusion, and costs

### Mechanics that matter

- **Fact:** Gartner accepts vendor-briefing requests from nonclients. Analysts decide based on research interest, not a fee or commercial relationship. Briefings are on the record and primarily transmit information to analysts; they are not consulting sessions. The published process describes approximately **45 minutes**, **one or two analysts**, and typical scheduling **two to four weeks** after acceptance. There is no briefing purchase requirement. (https://www.gartner.com/en/contact/vendor-briefings, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact / 2025 clarification:** Gartner's June 2025 explanation separates evaluative research from strategic guidance. Inclusion depends on research-specific criteria, and analysts cannot coach a vendor on how to improve its position or discuss protected scoring through paid inquiry. The idea that buying an analyst subscription buys Magic Quadrant inclusion contradicts Gartner's stated policy. (https://www.gartner.com/en/about/ombuds/news/how-gartner-experts-work-with-vendors, 2025-06-30)

- **Fact:** Magic Quadrants evaluate vendors on Ability to Execute and Completeness of Vision; inclusion and assessment are market-specific. Hype Cycles organize technologies and practices; they can contain sample vendors. A Market Guide is a different research format. Being a sample or representative vendor is not equivalent to being a Magic Quadrant Leader. A universal minimum ARR threshold for all three formats is **not verified**. (https://www.gartner.com/en/research/magic-quadrant, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6586902, 2025-06-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6306515, 2025-03-31)

- **Cost boundary:** Public, generally applicable 2025–2026 prices for Gartner subscriptions, analyst-advisory packages, and report reprint rights were **not verified**. Keep briefing access, paid advisory, research access, content licensing, and outside AR-agency fees as separate budget lines. Do not publish an invented “$30k to enter Gartner” price. (https://www.gartner.com/en/contact/vendor-briefings, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/about/policies/content-compliance, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

### Concrete case, limitations, and usable practice

- **Reported result:** Grafana announced Leader placement in the 2025 Observability Platforms Magic Quadrant on **10 July 2025**; separately, it announced **more than $400 million ARR and 7,000 customers** on **30 September 2025**. This establishes research recognition alongside scale. It does not establish that analyst relations caused the growth, and Grafana at this size is not a small-vendor inclusion benchmark. (https://grafana.com/press/releases/, dated entry 2025-07-10; https://grafana.com/press/2025/09/30/grafana-labs-surpasses-400m-arr-and-7000-customers-gains-new-investors-to-accelerate-global-expansion/, 2025-09-30)

- **Interpretation:** A small vendor's briefing should supply a demonstrable product, the buying problem, deployment model, named alternatives, customers willing to be references, and evidence of supportability. Ask which research covers the problem rather than assuming an IDP Magic Quadrant exists. Successful briefing acceptance and accurate analyst understanding are intermediate outcomes; no public small-vendor briefing-to-revenue conversion benchmark was verified. (https://www.gartner.com/en/contact/vendor-briefings, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Rule / failure risk:** Gartner does not permit custom analyst quotes endorsing a vendor or product. Quotes for releases should come from eligible published research, normally less than **12 months** old, and require Content Compliance approval. Informal analyst comments are not publishable endorsements. The content policy identifies a trademark-line change from **July 2025**; it does not relax independence or endorsement restrictions. No specific small OSS vendor sanction for violating these rules was verified. (https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/media-resources/public-relations-policy, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.gartner.com/en/about/policies/content-compliance, publication date not stated; July 2025 change stated; checked 2026-11-25)

## 3. Integrations, marketplaces inside products, and OEM distribution

### What actually has a measurable distribution outcome

- **Reported result / strong case:** Supabase launched its white-label Platforms offering on **5 December 2025**, reporting that Lovable, Bolt.new, and Baidu MeDo had already created **millions of Supabase projects in the preceding months**. The implementation exposes provisioning and management through an API or remote MCP server and supports embedded administration and project transfer. This is distribution embedded in another product's workflow; the disclosed outcome is projects created, not paid production workloads or incremental ARR. (https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-supabase-for-platforms, 2025-12-05)

- **Reported result / operational detail:** Supabase's Lovable case study says Lovable integrated in **January 2025**, uses the Management API for approximately **90% of its interactions with Supabase**, and creates roughly **one million Lovable projects per week**, many backed by Supabase. Do not count every Lovable project as a Supabase customer. The case is jointly commercial, its publication date is not displayed, and it provides no control group. (https://supabase.com/customers/lovable, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact:** Vercel distinguishes native marketplace integrations that can sell third-party products inside its platform from other integrations, guides, and templates. Supabase's Partner Catalog is an application-based directory of third-party integrations, with eligibility conditions. A directory listing and an embedded transaction/provisioning path are materially different distribution assets. (https://vercel.com/docs/integrations, updated 2025-12-04; https://supabase.com/docs/guides/integrations/partner-catalog, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

### Partner programs for integrators and hosting providers

- **Reported production case:** Vates and hosting provider Exodata converted a strategic alliance into **nine independent XCP-ng platforms across six regions**. Exodata reports performance parity without service-quality regression by **month three**, supported by technical account management and training. Its overall estate of 2,000 VMs is not stated to be the migrated-VM count. This is a supplier-published customer account, without audited performance or savings data. (https://vates.tech/blog/what-sovereign-it-looks-like-in-practice-the-exodata-use-case-with-vates/, 2025-10-29)

- **Fact / case:** Sidero separates **technology partners**, **solution providers**, and **resellers**. Its directory includes Fairbanks as a European reseller/solution provider and Latitude.sh as a technology partner. This makes implementation services and resale distinguishable from interoperability. Public partner-sourced revenue, margin schedules, or conversion rates for these specific relationships are **not verified**. (https://www.siderolabs.com/partners, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Interpretation:** For a cloud-building platform, a hosting-provider/OEM package should make tenant provisioning, metering, billing integration, branding, upgrades, incident ownership, and customer handover explicit. An SI package should instead define migration deliverables, training, and escalation. Programmable provisioning is relevant to cloud operators, but identical bare-metal economics are not established. (https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-supabase-for-platforms, 2025-12-05; https://www.siderolabs.com/partners, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Failure boundary:** Supabase's launch explicitly describes demand to remove manual backend provisioning as partners scaled. A logo directory alone cannot reproduce that mechanism. Track retained production tenants, consumption, and support costs. A public OEM failure-rate benchmark is **not verified**. (https://supabase.com/blog/introducing-supabase-for-platforms, 2025-12-05)

## 4. AWS, Azure/Microsoft, and Google Cloud marketplaces

### Current fees: deployment and transaction type change the answer

- **Fact — AWS:** Public SaaS and AWS Data Exchange offers carry a **3%** listing fee; public server offers—AMI, container, and machine learning—carry **20%**. Private offers are **3% below $1m TCV**, **2% from $1m to below $10m**, and **1.5% at $10m or more**; renewals are **1.5%**. The base schedule states an effective date of **5 January 2024**. “AWS charges 3%” is unsafe shorthand for infrastructure products distributed as images or containers. (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/marketplace/latest/userguide/listing-fees.html, base schedule effective 2024-01-05; publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact — AWS additions:** Channel Partner Private Offers add **0.5 percentage points** to the listing fee; the documented example is **3.5%** for a sub-$1m SaaS private offer. South Korean buyer transactions add **1 percentage point**, effective **1 April 2025**. Professional services normally carry **0.5%** on private offers, with a conditional **0%** base for qualifying multi-product solution offers; regional/channel uplifts still apply. The latter feature's launch date was not verified. (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/marketplace/latest/userguide/listing-fees.html, South Korea change effective 2025-04-01; other publication/change dates not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact — Google Cloud:** The Vendor Net Revenue Schedule, effective **21 April 2025**, leaves the vendor **97%** on standard offers and new private offers below $1m, **98%** on $1m–below $10m private offers, and **98.5%** on $10m+ offers. Thus Google's share is **3%, 2%, or 1.5%**. Eligible channel shifts, migrations, and native renewals also leave **98.5%**, subject to definitions and verification. Older offers do not automatically receive the new economics for their remaining term. (https://cloud.google.com/terms/marketplace-revenue-share-schedule, last modified 2025-03-10; effective 2025-04-21)

- **Fact — Microsoft/Azure:** Microsoft's publisher FAQ states a **3% standard store service fee** for transactable offers. It describes access through Marketplace, CSP resellers, and co-sell. This standard fee is not a verified all-in rate for every negotiated offer, region, or partner arrangement. Azure consumption, implementation, support, taxes, and any reseller economics remain separate from this quoted marketplace fee. (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/marketplace-offers/marketplace-faq-publisher-guide, updated 2026-07-23; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact — eligibility:** Google requires both listing/operational compliance and a business-case review; failure to qualify for the standard model can result in an adjusted revenue share. Its rules also require notifying Google about material changes that affect compliance. A self-hosted OSS project is not automatically a transactable cloud-marketplace product just because it has a container image. (https://docs.cloud.google.com/marketplace/docs/partners/get-started, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

### What works, with the denominator intact

- **Reported benchmark:** AWS-sponsored IDC research published in **August 2025** surveyed **287 sellers** across regions and interviewed **13**. Sellers reported **41% faster growth** in their marketplace business than in their overall software business; **45%** said Marketplace shortened sales cycles; **33%** reported better renewals. The sample overrepresented larger, established sellers. These are seller reports, not controlled tests, and **45% of respondents** is not a **45% reduction in sales-cycle duration**. (https://pages.awscloud.com/rs/112-TZM-766/images/25-GLOBAL-en-US-idc-whitepaper-ardm-aws-marketplace-is-a-powerful-growth-engine-reprint.pdf, 2025-08)

- **Practitioner case / limited quantification:** In that study, ScyllaDB describes shifting existing customers' renewals into Marketplace; HashiCorp describes retention correlation. The study reports **twice the software-sales growth** among co-selling participants versus nonparticipants, but does not establish random assignment or publish those companies' individual uplift. Procurement and field-sales collaboration are plausible mechanisms; simply listing a product is not the tested intervention. (https://pages.awscloud.com/rs/112-TZM-766/images/25-GLOBAL-en-US-idc-whitepaper-ardm-aws-marketplace-is-a-powerful-growth-engine-reprint.pdf, 2025-08)

- **Fact / alliance case:** Grafana signed a **five-year AWS strategic collaboration agreement** in March 2026 combining technical integration, go-to-market alignment, migration, and marketplace access. This demonstrates that an open-source vendor can partner commercially with a hyperscaler while differentiating through open observability. An incremental revenue result from this new agreement is **not verified**. (https://grafana.com/press/2026/03/10/grafana-labs-signs-strategic-collaboration-agreement-with-aws-to-accelerate-open-observability-adoption-at-scale/, 2026-03-10)

- **What changed / failure risk:** Google's April 2025 schedule makes the blanket “all cloud marketplaces take 3%” assumption obsolete. No named small OSS vendor marketplace ban or failed listing with attributable losses was verified; the documented risk is eligibility, product packaging, and offer-specific economics, not an invented marketplace-discovery algorithm. (https://cloud.google.com/terms/marketplace-revenue-share-schedule, effective 2025-04-21)

## 5. Technology alliances: NVIDIA and Red Hat

### Mechanics and strategic value

- **Fact:** NVIDIA Partner Network distinguishes cloud service providers, OEMs, ISVs, systems integrators, solution providers, and services partners. The cloud-service-provider role covers hosted services using NVIDIA products; OEMs incorporate technology into their own branded platforms. The program explicitly ties benefits and requirements to partner type and competency. It is not a generic badge earned by saying a platform supports GPUs. (https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/partners/become-a-partner.md/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact:** Red Hat's 2025 OpenShift certification policy distinguishes **partner-validated** from **Red Hat-certified** products and targets commercial software deployed on OpenShift. Certification is a product and compatibility process, not a general endorsement of the vendor's entire platform. An OpenShift-compatible application, certified Operator, and alternative Kubernetes distribution are different propositions. (https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_software_certification/2025//html-single/red_hat_openshift_software_certification_policy_guide/index, 2025 edition; exact publication date not verified)

- **Interpretation / suitable case:** Vates describes working with Bull at a Vienna event and increasing DACH investment, connecting virtualization alternatives, local delivery, and sovereignty. For a smaller infrastructure vendor, a tested reference configuration and a service partner able to install it provide a more concrete joint offer than a broad alliance announcement. Event attendance was at most roughly **100** according to the author; partner-generated revenue from that event is **not verified**. (https://vates.tech/blog/what-we-heard-in-vienna-and-why-vates-is-investing-in-dach/, 2026-06-08)

### Costs and failure boundary

- **Not verified:** Universal 2025–2026 NVIDIA NPN fees, MDF entitlements, Red Hat certification charges, minimum sales commitments, and lead guarantees were not established from the public pages reviewed. Obtain the program terms for the relevant role and geography; do not reuse an old distributor's program deck as a current price list. Engineering validation and ongoing compatibility work are part of the proposed cost model even where a badge fee is absent. (https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/about-nvidia/partners/become-a-partner.md/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_software_certification/2025//html-single/red_hat_openshift_software_certification_policy_guide/index, 2025 edition)

- **Failure boundary:** A measured small-vendor sales uplift from either certification, or a documented 2025–2026 revocation in this reference class, is **not verified**. Do not teach certification as guaranteed pipeline. The defensible learning objective is to map the buyer's required deployment environment to the correct validation and support relationship. (https://docs.redhat.com/en/documentation/red_hat_software_certification/2025//html-single/red_hat_openshift_software_certification_policy_guide/index, 2025 edition)

## 6. Foundations, governance, and conformance as strategy

### Donation and membership are separate decisions

- **Fact:** CNCF has a technical project-lifecycle process evaluated through TOC due diligence. Its onboarding template requires a Project Contribution Agreement, trademark transfer to the Linux Foundation, and a separate neutral project organization. This is a governance and asset decision, not just buying exposure. Project acceptance and corporate membership are separate processes. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/lifecycle/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://github.com/cncf/sandbox/blob/main/.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/project-onboarding.md, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.cncf.io/about/join/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Rule discrepancy:** The current lifecycle page permits Apache 2.0 or a CNCF allowlist license, while the onboarding template reviewed says incoming projects must use Apache 2.0. Do not resolve this inconsistency by inventing a universal exception; the applicable licensing path for a specific contribution needs confirmation through the foundation process. (https://contribute.cncf.io/projects/lifecycle/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://github.com/cncf/sandbox/blob/main/.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/project-onboarding.md, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Reported case:** Cozystack's April 2025 announcement documents admission to Sandbox; its September 2026 technical review documents a later maturity milestone. These are concrete outcomes of foundation participation. Increased paid adoption caused by CNCF admission is **not verified**. Admission should be described by its actual stage rather than marketed as a blanket security or production-readiness guarantee. (https://www.cncf.io/blog/2025/04/28/open-source-paas-cozystack-becomes-a-cncf-sandbox-project/, 2025-04-28; https://cozystack.io/blog/2026/09/cozystack-cncf-general-technical-review/, 2026-09-29)

- **Reported result / broader strategy:** Anthropic donated MCP to the Linux Foundation's Agentic AI Foundation in December 2025 after reporting **10,000+ active public servers** and **97 million monthly Python/TypeScript SDK downloads**. The counts precede or accompany donation; they do not demonstrate that donation created adoption. The strategic mechanism is making a cross-vendor interface easier for competitors to adopt under shared stewardship. (https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-the-model-context-protocol-and-establishing-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation, 2025-12-09)

### Certified Kubernetes and Kubernetes AI Conformance

- **Fact:** Certified Kubernetes review checks technical evidence, product identity, and a participation agreement. Commercial vendors must be CNCF members or pay an equivalent certification fee; nonprofits and community distributions can qualify without that fee. Acceptance produces a certification record and directory visibility. The badge applies to the qualifying product/version, not every downstream service the vendor sells. (https://github.com/cncf/k8s-conformance/blob/master/reviewing.md, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact / new program:** CNCF launched its Kubernetes AI Conformance program on **11 November 2025** to standardize AI workload support. The original Certified Kubernetes badge should not be used as shorthand for satisfying this additional AI profile. Verify the specific certified offering and applicable requirements before using either claim in a bid. (https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2025/11/11/cncf-launches-certified-kubernetes-ai-conformance-program-to-standardize-ai-workloads-on-kubernetes/, 2025-11-11)

- **Reported result / dated change:** By **24 March 2026**, CNCF reported growth from **18 to 31 certified AI platforms**, including OVHcloud and Spectro Cloud, and stricter Kubernetes **v1.35** requirements. That is a roughly **72%** increase, consistent with “over 70%,” not literally a doubling. It measures certified offerings, not revenue or customer deployments. Buyers need the current profile and version rather than relying indefinitely on a launch-era badge. (https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2026/03/24/cncf-nearly-doubles-certified-kubernetes-ai-platforms/, 2026-03-24)

- **Failure boundary:** Foundation hosting does not ensure perpetual project health: CNCF's 2025 annual report records **13 projects archived during the year**. The same report describes maturity advancement in terms of adoption, activity, and contributors across organizations. No general donation-to-revenue multiplier is established by these records. (https://www.cncf.io/reports/cncf-annual-report-2025/, 2026-02-24; https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/cncf_ar25_033126a.pdf, 2025 annual report; landing-page publication 2026-02-24; revised PDF publication date not stated)

## 7. Certifications and education as long-term distribution

### Certification scale, mechanism, and limits

- **Reported result:** CNCF's 2025 annual report says CKA reached **291,000 exam enrollments**, up **16%** from 2024, and its Kubernetes MOOC reached **429,000 enrollments**, up **7%**. These are cumulative program milestones as presented, not 291,000 new certified professionals in 2025. Enrollment is not a pass, unique practitioner, active operator, or product purchase. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/cncf_ar25_033126a.pdf, 2025 annual report; landing-page publication 2026-02-24; revised PDF publication date not stated)

- **Reported result / category formation:** The same report records **720 CNPA registrations** since its April 2025 launch and **170 CNPE registrations** since November 2025, and **230 Kubernetes Certified Service Providers** at year end. These show a training-to-service-delivery infrastructure taking shape; they do not establish the sales uplift for any vendor. (https://www.cncf.io/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/cncf_ar25_033126a.pdf, 2025 annual report; landing-page publication 2026-02-24; revised PDF publication date not stated)

- **Fact / mechanics:** CKA is a performance-based command-line exam. HashiCorp's current catalog separates Terraform and Vault credentials and includes different skill levels. A platform should distinguish a course-completion certificate from an independently administered practical certification. A verified 2025–2026 total of unique HashiCorp-certified practitioners and a certification-driven revenue effect were **not verified**. (https://www.cncf.io/training/certification/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://developer.hashicorp.com/certifications, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Dated change:** CNCF introduced CARE recertification changes in **March 2026**, including renewal relationships between credentials, while reporting more than **3,500 Kubestronauts**. Old course material that assumes every credential must always be renewed independently can become inaccurate. Badge counts also overlap: one practitioner can hold multiple certifications. (https://www.cncf.io/blog/2026/03/23/cncf-introduces-a-new-recertification-program-as-kubestronaut-community-surpasses-3500/, 2026-03-23)

### Books, free courses, and university programs

- **Fact:** O'Reilly openly offers sponsorship of books, reports, and interactive content. Sponsoring distribution and authoring an editorial book are different routes. A downloadable O'Reilly title is not automatically independent validation of the sponsor's product. Public standard sponsorship prices, attributable infrastructure-software pipeline, and 2025–2026 book-to-paid-user conversion benchmarks are **not verified**. (https://www.oreilly.com/content-marketing/available-products.html, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://www.oreilly.com/work-with-us, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Reported case:** MongoDB's February 2026 academia announcement describes curricula, free credits, and certifications, plus partnerships with HCL GUVI and Telangana TASK that add regional-language delivery and access through **100+ academic institutions**. MongoDB reports reaching **hundreds of thousands** of students against a goal of **500,000 by 2028**. The goal is not an achieved count; the report does not disclose a completion denominator or later commercial conversion. (https://www.mongodb.com/company/blog/innovation/mongodb-academia-expands-access-to-ai-and-data-skills-in-india-through-guvi-and-task-partnership, 2026-02-24)

- **Fact / current implementation:** ScyllaDB University offers practical courses, including 2025 labs and 2026 optimization material. - **Reported evidence:** Pew analyzed Google browsing by **900 U.S. adults** in March 2025. - **Interpretation:** Documentation can remain commercially valuable when humans or agents use it without a conventional referral. (https://vercel.com/docs/agent-resources/markdown-access, publication date not verified; checked 2026-11-25)

(https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2025/07/21/google-users-are-less-likely-to-click-on-links-when-an-ai-summary-appears-in-the-results/, 2025-07-22)

## 11. EU sovereign cloud: demand, policy, and the limits of positioning

### Demand with a counterweight

- **Reported demand:** Bitkom's **11 June 2025** report surveyed **604 German companies with at least 20 employees**. **78%** considered Germany too dependent on U.S. cloud providers; **82%** wanted competitive German/European hyperscalers; **50% of cloud-using companies** said U.S. policy forced them to reconsider strategy. These are attitudes in Germany, not an EU-wide migration rate or committed procurement budget. (https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Wirtschaft-ruft-nach-deutscher-Cloud, 2025-06-11)

- **Reported constraint:** In the same research, **65%** would accept none of the offered disadvantages for a protected German cloud; only **7%** would accept a **10–20% price premium**, **8%** reduced functionality, and **12%** slower feature delivery. Sovereignty creates an opening but does not remove price, functionality, and service expectations. Do not combine these response options into an invented single willingness-to-pay measure. (https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/Wirtschaft-ruft-nach-deutscher-Cloud, 2025-06-11)

- **Market benchmark:** Synergy estimated European providers at about **15% of their regional cloud-infrastructure market**, versus **70% for AWS, Microsoft, and Google combined**. The scope includes IaaS, PaaS, and hosted private cloud; Europe includes markets beyond the EU. European suppliers' revenue growth and flat share can coexist because the whole market grows. The public release does not expose a company-level sampling frame. (https://www.srgresearch.com/articles/european-cloud-providers-local-market-share-now-holds-steady-at-15, 2025-07-24)

### Policy and procurement: concrete events

- **Fact:** The EU Data Act began applying on **12 September 2025** and includes cloud-switching and interoperability measures. It does not mean all EU organizations must use an EU-owned cloud. A platform's portability claim should identify what can actually be exported and operated elsewhere, rather than treating “open source” as automatic compliance. (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/data-act, publication date not stated; application date 2025-09-12; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact / category becoming procurement:** The Commission launched a sovereign-cloud tender of up to **€180 million over six years** on **10 October 2025**, using eight sovereignty objectives covering matters including legal control, operations, supply chains, technology openness, and security. The framework is a multidimensional assessment, not merely a data-center-address test. (https://commission.europa.eu/news-and-media/news/commission-moves-forward-cloud-sovereignty-eur-180-million-tender-2025-10-10_en, 2025-10-10)

- **Fact / completed selection:** On **17 April 2026**, the Commission announced four awarded providers: **Post Telecom with CleverCloud and OVHcloud; STACKIT; Scaleway; and Proximus with S3NS, Clarence, and Mistral**. This is a concrete partner/consortium outcome. The €180m is a procurement ceiling, not booked revenue or equal awards. The release expressly says non-European technology can meet minimum sovereignty requirements under appropriate controls. (https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_833/IP_26_833_EN.pdf, 2026-04-17)

### Gaia-X: usable trust infrastructure, not a replacement hyperscaler

- **Fact:** Gaia-X's live compliance tooling implements credential submission and compliance credentials through Digital Clearing Houses; Loire is an identified implementation/version. This is trust and interoperability infrastructure, not a cloud compute provider. The existence of working compliance endpoints does not establish widespread customer usage. (https://wizard.gaia-x.eu/compliance, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25; https://compliance.gaia-x.eu/, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Reported implementation scale:** A 2026 presentation by Gaia-X leadership hosted by CSI Piemonte describes a compliant-service catalog with **600 services, 15 providers, and five providers offering Label Level 3**. These are catalog entries/providers, not 600 independent production buyers. The presentation's exact publication day is **not verified**. (https://www.csipiemonte.it/sites/default/files/inline_download/eventi/2026/AHLE.pdf, 2026; exact publication date not verified)

- **Failure / adoption limitation:** Reporting from the November 2025 Gaia-X summit quotes its CEO acknowledging only a handful of operational data spaces/databases delivering benefits, despite more than 150 projects in preparation. This is an attributed participant assessment, not an independently audited deployment count. It cautions against equating certification/catalog availability with sustainable demand. (https://www.itpro.com/infrastructure/europe-digital-sovereignty-gaia-x, 2025-11-26)

- **Interpretation:** A Cozystack-like vendor's sovereignty dossier should map code access, legal control, remote support, key custody, software updates, telemetry, hardware dependencies, and exit procedures to the customer's actual criteria. Work through a regional operator or consortium when that is how procurement is structured. “European hosting” alone and “all non-EU technology is prohibited” are both inadequate propositions under the Commission example. (https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_26_833/IP_26_833_EN.pdf, 2026-04-17)

The prevalence of independently purchasing B2B infrastructure agents is **not verified**. (https://www.anthropic.com/news/donating-the-model-context-protocol-and-establishing-of-the-agentic-ai-foundation, 2025-12-09)

- **Reported distribution case:** The Supabase/Lovable integration illustrates agents generating infrastructure through an already-selected partner. Lovable's use of the Management API for roughly 90% of interactions is evidence of programmatic consumption, not proof that an unconstrained LLM compared all databases and chose Supabase on merit. Commercial integration and model preference must not be conflated. (https://supabase.com/customers/lovable, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)

### Mechanics for agent-readable docs and MCP

- **Fact:** Vercel makes documentation available as Markdown, including page-level `.md` access. This provides a machine-consumable route alongside the human site. Its separate AGENTS.md experiment suggests that making context reliably available can matter more than adding an optional retrieval capability that an agent never invokes. The result is limited to that test setup. (https://vercel.com/docs/agent-resources/markdown-access, publication date not verified; checked 2026-11-25; https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals, 2026-01-27)

- **Fact / search rule:** Google's current Search guidance says special AI files, including **llms.txt**, are not required for inclusion and are not used by Google Search in that way. This does not mean Markdown is useless to coding agents. “Add llms.txt and rank in every LLM” is **not verified**, and is inconsistent with treating Google's rules as a universal agent protocol. (https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide, updated 2026-07-10; checked 2026-11-25)

- **Fact / dated product change:** Supabase launched its hosted remote MCP server on **3 October 2025**, reducing local setup and adding OAuth-based connection. The launch explicitly documented an all-or-nothing permission decision in that implementation. Do not silently assume fine-grained permissions based on what OAuth can theoretically support; inspect current server behavior and authorization scopes. (https://supabase.com/blog/remote-mcp-server, 2025-10-03)

- **Failure case:** Supabase's **16 September 2025** security write-up describes a demonstrated stored prompt-injection scenario involving database content and an MCP-connected client, then discusses layered defenses. An easy connection can also increase the blast radius of untrusted instructions. The case is a demonstrated security weakness, not a verified count of breached production customers. (https://supabase.com/blog/mcp, 2025-09-16)

### Evidence on discovery and a usable evaluation design

- **Reported benchmark:** Cloudflare's July 2025 analysis reported roughly **71,000 Anthropic HTML crawls per referral** in the observed period. Its metric uses traffic visible to Cloudflare and recognized crawlers/referrers; it is not a count of all model answers, training inclusion, or new customers. Large crawl volume is not evidence of a successful distribution channel. (https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-search-crawl-refer-ratio-on-radar, 2025-07-01)

- **Interpretation:** Publish stable product descriptions, versions, prerequisites, prices or pricing rules, license terms, limits, reference architectures, and safe examples. Provide a scoped MCP/API path for evaluation environments and explicit human authorization for costly or destructive operations. The proposal follows the observed docs-success and MCP-security mechanisms; it is not an established formula for winning recommendations. (https://supabase.com/blog/mcp, 2025-09-16)

- **Proposed measurement:** Use a fixed set of realistic tasks—choose a regional platform under residency constraints, compare VMware migration options, deploy a tenant, restore a backup—and repeat them across named model/version configurations. Record citations, factual errors, completed operations, permission failures, and cost. Keep partner-default distribution separate from neutral recommendation tests. No validated cross-model “share of AI recommendations” benchmark for Cozystack-like platforms was found. (https://vercel.com/blog/agents-md-outperforms-skills-in-our-agent-evals, 2026-01-27; https://supabase.com/customers/lovable, publication date not stated; checked 2026-11-25)
